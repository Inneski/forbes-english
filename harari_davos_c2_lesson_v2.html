Analogies are the recurring test

He uses four: the knife, the mercenaries, the immigrants, the religions of the book.

An analogy illustrates; it does not prove. Each one is worth asking what it captures and where it breaks.

The first distinction

tool and agent — the word the whole address rests on

a tool

Something acted through. You decide; it transmits the decision.

A knife cuts salad or it does not, and either way the choosing happened somewhere else. Tools have no interests.

an agent

Something that acts. It can learn, choose, and pursue an end you did not set.

Verbatim: “It is not just another tool. It is an agent. It can learn and change by itself.”

Why the word is load-bearing

Everything later — personhood, mercenaries, lying — needs this to be true.

If AI is a tool, the rest of the speech does not follow. That is why he spends the opening on it rather than on capability.

The second distinction

a person and a legal person are not the same claim

a legal person

An entity the law lets own, contract, sue and be sued. Need not be human.

Companies have been legal persons for a century and a half. So, in some jurisdictions, are rivers.

Why the precedents were safe

A river cannot open an account. A human always did it on the river’s behalf.

The personhood was a fiction laid over human decisions. That is precisely what made it harmless.

Why this case differs

An AI can discharge the role itself — no executive, no trustee.

Fiction and fact converge, so the precedents stop governing. This is the hinge of the address, and it is entirely a lexical distinction.

How to weigh an analogy

The mercenaries — what it carries and where it gives way

The story, in summary

A British king hires Anglo-Saxon fighters against his enemies. They win, look around, and keep the country.

Marked as a summary because it is one. The transcript is dialogue, not the tidy sentence above.

What it captures

Bought loyalty can be outbid. Capability you rent can be turned on you.

And the sharper point: we already accept this about people, which is why the inconsistency about machines is striking.

Where it gives way

Mercenaries wanted land. What an AI is supposed to want is left unargued.

The analogy borrows its menace from human motive. Strip the motive and you have a capable system, not a usurper. Say so in your writing.

The skill this lesson is really for

Reporting a claim without adopting it

Neutral: states, notes, observes

Harari notes that corporations already hold legal personhood.

Use where the fact is uncontested. Choosing a neutral verb for a contested claim is itself an endorsement.

Marked: contends, maintains, insists

He contends that anything made of words will be taken over.

Signals a position that others dispute — without saying whether you do. This is the workhorse of academic writing.

Structural: concedes, pre-empts, qualifies

He pre-empts the “glorified autocomplete” objection.

Describes what a move does in the argument. Examiners reward this because it shows you read the structure, not just the content.

The same skill, one level finer

Hedging — how much weight to put on a claim

On the claim

arguably, on his account, if this is right

If Harari is right that institutions are linguistic, then… lets you follow an argument without buying it.

On the strength

tends to, in large part, need not follow

The inference need not follow is a criticism. Does not follow is a stronger one you then have to prove.

The failure mode

Hedging everything reads as having no position at all.

Hedge the contested step and commit to the rest. A paragraph where every clause is qualified scores worse than one clear claim.

🗣Speaking · in pairs

One of you takes Tracey’s position, one takes Harari’s. Neither of you has to believe it.

  • Report your partner’s last claim back to them using contends or maintains — accurately enough that they accept the summary.
  • Concede one point genuinely before you answer it. Notice what conceding does to your credibility.
  • Attack one inference rather than a premise. Say which step you think does not carry.
  • Take one analogy and say where it gives way, without saying the conclusion is therefore wrong.
