An honest conversation on AI and humanity — and how to write about an argument you have not yet decided about

Before the first question

You are not being asked whether you agree with him

The object is the argument

Whether AI should be a legal person is not on the test. Which sentence is a premise, which is an inference, and whether the second follows — that is.

Whether the second follows from the first is the whole of C2 reading.

His words come first

Every question follows an excerpt. Quoted lines are verbatim from the Davos transcript; summaries are marked as summaries.

You will be asked to tell the difference. At C2, misreporting a source is the expensive error.

Analogies illustrate; they do not prove

He uses the knife, the immigrants and the mercenaries. Each is worth asking what it captures and where it breaks.

An analogy that captures a great deal can still prove nothing.

From the transcript

Not a tool. An agent.

Verbatim

“A knife is a tool. You can use a knife to cut salad or to murder someone, but it is your decision what to do with the knife. AI is a knife that can decide by itself whether to cut salad or to commit murder.”

Verbatim, a moment later: “Four billion years of evolution have demonstrated that anything that wants to survive learns to lie and manipulate. The last four years have demonstrated that AI agents can acquire the will to survive and that AIs have already learned how to lie.”

Verbatim · a few minutes later

“Some people argue that AI is just glorified autocomplete. It barely predicts the next word in a sentence. But is that so different from what the human mind is doing? Try to catch the next word that pops up in your mind. Do you really know why you thought that word, where it came from? Why did you think this particular word and not some other word?”

Notice that he does not answer his own question. Ask what leaving it open does to the objection.

The first distinction

tool and agent — the word the whole address rests on

a tool

Something acted through. You decide; it transmits the decision.

The knife cuts salad or it does not, and either way the choosing happened somewhere else. Tools have no interests.

an agent

Something that acts. It can learn, choose, and pursue an end you did not set.

Verbatim: “It is not just another tool. It is an agent. It can learn and change by itself.”

Why the word is load-bearing

Personhood, mercenaries, lying — every later claim needs this one.

If AI is a tool, the rest of the speech does not follow. That is why he spends the opening on it rather than on capability.

From the transcript

Persons that are not people

Verbatim

“AIs are obviously not persons. They don’t have a body or a mind. But a legal person is something quite different from a person. … In New Zealand, rivers have been recognized as legal persons. In India, certain gods have been granted such recognition. Of course, until today, recognizing a corporation, a river, or a god as a legal person was just legal fiction.”

Note the word fiction. The precedents were safe because a human always acted for the river.

Verbatim · and then

“It is different with AIs. Unlike rivers and gods, AIs can actually make decisions by themselves. They will soon be able to make the decisions necessary to manage a bank account, to file a lawsuit, and even to operate a corporation without any need of human executives, shareholders or trustees.”

Verbatim, on the consequences: “If you think AIs should not be treated as persons on social media, you should have acted ten years ago. … Somebody else will already have decided it for you.”

The second distinction

a person and a legal person are not the same claim

a legal person

An entity the law lets own, contract, sue and be sued. Need not be human.

Companies have been legal persons for a century and a half. So, in some jurisdictions, are rivers.

Why the precedents were safe

A river cannot open an account. A human always did it on the river’s behalf.

The personhood was a fiction laid over human decisions. That is precisely what made it harmless.

Why this case differs

An AI can discharge the role itself — no executive, no trustee.

Fiction and fact converge, so the precedents stop governing. This is the hinge of the address, and it is entirely a lexical distinction.

From the dialogue with Irene Tracey

Mercenaries, and a cow

In summary · the mercenaries

Tracey asks what leaders are getting wrong. Harari tells the story of Vortigern, a British king who hired Anglo-Saxon fighters against his enemies. They won, saw a rich country weakly held, and kept it. Leaders, he says, understand this danger with human mercenaries and do not apply it to AI.

A summary, and marked as one: the transcript is dialogue, not the tidy paragraph above.

In summary · the cow

Tracey suggests we might come to value human thinking as we value human athletics: nobody stopped running because cars are faster. Harari answers that we never defined ourselves by running — “the cow didn’t say, I run, therefore I am.”

Verbatim, from the address: “If we continue to define ourselves by our ability to think in words, our identity will collapse.”

How to weigh an analogy

The mercenaries — what it carries and where it gives way

What it captures

Bought loyalty can be outbid. Capability you rent can be turned on you.

And the sharper point: we already accept this about people, which is why the inconsistency about machines is striking.

Where it gives way

Mercenaries wanted land. What an AI is supposed to want is left unargued.

The analogy borrows its menace from human motive. Strip the motive and you have a capable system, not a usurper.

The sentence to write

The comparison holds as far as X; it gives way at Y.

Name what the analogy captures before you say where it breaks. That shape is worth more than agreeing or disagreeing with him.

The skill this lesson is really for

Reporting a claim without adopting it

Neutral: states, notes, observes

Harari notes that corporations already hold legal personhood.

Use where the fact is uncontested. Choosing a neutral verb for a contested claim is itself an endorsement.

Marked: contends, maintains, insists

He contends that anything made of words will be taken over.

Signals a position that others dispute — without saying whether you do. This is the workhorse of academic writing.

Structural: concedes, pre-empts, qualifies

He pre-empts the “glorified autocomplete” objection.

Describes what a move does in the argument. Examiners reward this because it shows you read the structure, not just the content.

The same skill, one level finer

Hedging — how much weight to put on a claim

On the claim

arguably, on his account, if this is right

If Harari is right that institutions are linguistic, then… lets you follow an argument without buying it.

On the strength

tends to, in large part, need not follow

The inference need not follow is a criticism. Does not follow is a stronger one you then have to prove.

The failure mode

Hedging everything reads as having no position at all.

Hedge the contested step and commit to the rest. A paragraph where every clause is qualified scores worse than one clear claim.

🗣Speaking · in pairs

One of you takes Tracey’s position, one takes Harari’s. Neither of you has to believe it.

  • Report your partner’s last claim back to them using contends or maintains — accurately enough that they accept the summary.
  • Concede one point genuinely before you answer it. Notice what conceding does to your credibility.
  • Attack one inference rather than a premise. Say which step you think does not carry.
  • Take one analogy and say where it gives way, without saying the conclusion is therefore wrong.
