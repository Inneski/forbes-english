Before the questions

Three openings do almost all of the work

Can I…?

“Can I see the menu, please?”

For one thing, for yourself. Please is not optional in English — without it the same words sound like an order.

Could we…?

“Could we have the bill, please?”

The same request from two people or more. Could is a step politer than can, and works everywhere can does.

I’d like…

“I’d like a coffee, please.”

This is how you order. Short for I would like. I want is correct English and still sounds rude here.

The words themselves

Four you cannot get through a meal without

table · menu · bill

“A table for two.” “Here is your menu.” “Could we have the bill?”

A table is what you ask for at the door, the menu is what you read, the bill is what you pay. American menus say check for the bill.

ready to order

“Are you ready to order?” — “Not yet, thank you.”

The waiter’s most common question. Not yet is the polite way to buy yourself another minute.

allergic to…

“I’m allergic to nuts.”

The preposition never changes: allergic to. Not of, not at. This is the one phrase on the slide worth learning perfectly.

🗣Roleplay · in pairs

One waiter, one customer. Then swap over, and change the order the second time.

  • Arrive at the door. Ask for a table for two, and ask to sit outside.
  • Ask for the menu. The waiter asks if you are ready — you are not. Buy a minute.
  • Order one thing to eat and one thing to drink. Say please every time.
  • Tell the waiter about an allergy, then ask for the bill.
