The four tenses this lesson uses

Present simple and past simple

Present simple — habits and facts

My cat jumps onto the door handle every morning.

Signals: every day, usually, often, never, on Mondays. She/he/it takes -s.

Past simple — one finished action

He learned it by watching us.

Signals: yesterday, last week, in 1983, two days ago. Regular verbs take -ed; many common ones do not.

The test between them

Is there a finished time, or a repeated one?

A stated past time forces the past simple. A repeated time forces the present simple. Find the time word before you look at the verb.

Present continuous — and why it is here

The form

am / is / are + verb-ing

Eleven is hiding in the woods. Right now, as we speak.

Signals

now · at the moment · Look! · Listen!

If the sentence points at this moment, the continuous is the only choice.

Why it matters here

It is the commonest wrong answer in this lesson.

It is the commonest wrong option in the questions ahead. You cannot reject a form you have never been shown.

Present perfect — and the German trap in it

The form

have / has + past participle

I have never seen it. She has escaped. We haven't spoken.

When

Experience up to now, with no stated time.

And always after since and for when the situation still holds.

The trap

German seit takes the present. English does not.

✗ We don't speak since Monday. ✓ We haven't spoken since Monday. This single pattern is worth more marks than any other on this page.

The participles you need for the perfect

speak → spoken · see → seen

take → taken · go → gone

teach → taught · think → thought

find → found · get → got

be → been · do → done

write → written · know → known

The perfect needs the third form, not the past simple. I have spoke is the error; I have spoken is the sentence.

One more structure

The first conditional

The pattern

If + present simple → will + infinitive

If I don't study, I will get a bad grade.

The rule that gets broken

Never will in the if-clause.

✗ If I will not study… — this is the single commonest conditional error in German-speaking classrooms.

Why it is called the first

It is about a real, possible future.

Two scored items on this page depend on it. German uses the present in both halves too, which is why the will slips in.

German traps

Four false friends and a fixed preposition

machen → do, not make

do your homework · do sport · do your best

Make is for producing a thing: make a cake, make a noise, make a plan.

bekommen → get, not become

get a message · get a present

English become = werden. „I became a message“ says you turned into one.

lernen → study or learn

study for a test · learn Spanish

And two fixed forms: good at something, rely on somebody. Neither preposition follows the German.

The text

Reading — part 1 of 4

Stranger Things is an American science fiction horror series set in the fictional town of Hawkins, Indiana, in the 1980s. It was created by Matt and Ross Duffer — the Duffer Brothers — and first appeared on Netflix in 2016. The show relies on nostalgia for 1980s popular culture: the music, the clothes, and even the lettering of its title sequence.

Two of the bold words are tested later. Appear here means to become available to the public, not to materialise out of thin air.

The text

Reading — part 2 of 4

The story begins when a boy named Will Byers goes missing. His friends Mike, Dustin and Lucas set out to find him. During their search they meet a strange girl who has escaped from a secret government laboratory. She is known only as Eleven. Scientists there taught her to use her telekinetic abilities, and treated her as a test subject rather than a child.

Has escaped is present perfect: it happened before this moment in the story, and the result — she is out — still holds.

The text

Reading — part 3 of 4

The laboratory is connected to a dark parallel dimension called the Upside Down. Certain creatures there, in particular a monster called the Demogorgon, pose a severe danger to anyone who meets them. Eleven had been held in the laboratory for years, and she avoided being caught again after her escape.

Read the order carefully: she was held for years, and then escaped. A comprehension question depends on it.

The text

Reading — part 4 of 4

Within days she had formed a bond with Mike, Dustin and Lucas, learning to trust and rely on them. The series explores friendship, courage, the stigma around being different, and the dangers of government secrecy.

Note within days. Season 1 covers about a week — the friendship forms fast, which is part of why it lands.

Hawkins, one week later

Use at least four: have you ever…? haven’t seen since if… will rely on good at severe
🗣Speaking · in pairs

One of you was in Hawkins that week. The other is from the local paper.

  • Reporter: ask three questions with Have you ever…? and one with How long…?
  • Witness: answer using since at least twice. Watch the tense — since takes the perfect.
  • Both: make three first-conditional predictions about what happens next in the town.
  • Both: say one thing each of you is good at, and one person you rely on. Watch both prepositions.
