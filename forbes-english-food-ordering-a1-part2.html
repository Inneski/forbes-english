Activity 1 · Multiple Choice

Choose the best answer

These questions are trickier — read carefully. Choose the most natural and correct phrase for each situation.

Question 1 of 6
The waiter asks: "How would you like your steak?" You want it cooked until there is no pink inside. You say:
✅ "Well done" is the correct cooking term for a steak with no pink. The other options are grammatically possible but are not the natural phrases used in restaurants. Other options: rare medium medium rare
Question 2 of 6
You are vegetarian. The waiter describes a dish. You want to check if it has meat. You ask:
✅ "Does this dish contain any meat?" is the natural question. We use contain to ask about ingredients in food. Option D is a statement, not a question.
Question 3 of 6
Your soup is cold. You want to tell the waiter politely. You say:
✅ "Excuse me, I think my soup is cold." is polite and natural. Starting with Excuse me and using I think makes it softer and more polite — important in restaurant English.
Question 4 of 6
The waiter asks: "Would you like still or sparkling water?" You want water with bubbles. You say:
✅ "Sparkling, please." is the natural, concise answer. When someone gives you two options, you can answer with just the one you want + please. Bubbly is informal and not still is indirect.
Question 5 of 6
You want to order the same dish as your friend. You say:
✅ "I'll have the same as him, please." is the natural phrase. I'll have… is very common for ordering food — it sounds confident and polite.
Question 6 of 6
You are not sure what "crème brûlée" is. You ask the waiter:
✅ "Could you tell me what crème brûlée is?" is correct and polite. Could you tell me… is a great structure for polite questions. Option D has incorrect word order — in English, indirect questions use statement word order: what it is, not what is it.
