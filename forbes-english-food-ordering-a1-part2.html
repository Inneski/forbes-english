Before the questions

Three phrases carry the whole meal

I’d like…

“I’d like the soup, please.”

To order. Short for I would like — softer than I want, which sounds like a demand.

Could you…?

“Could you bring some water?”

To ask for something. The verb after it never changes: bring, not to bring or bringing.

Excuse me…

“Excuse me, my soup is cold.”

To get attention, and to open a complaint. Without it the same sentence sounds angry.

The courses

starter → main course → dessert

American menus say appetizer and entrée instead of starter and main course. Same meal.

Three questions you will be asked

“Still or sparkling?”

“How would you like your steak?”

“Anything else?”

Steak has exactly three answers: rare, medium, well done.

🗣Roleplay · in pairs

One waiter, one customer. Then swap, and make the second run harder.

  • Arrive without a reservation. The waiter must find you a table anyway.
  • Order a starter and a main. Ask the waiter to recommend one of them.
  • Something is wrong with the food. Complain without once saying “bad”.
  • Ask for separate bills, and check whether service is included.
