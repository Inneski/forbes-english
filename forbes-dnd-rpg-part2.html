The Winter After the Victory

You won. The contract with Maison Éclore is signed, the champagne is long finished, and the real work has started — the part nobody writes a case study about.

Winning an account and keeping one require almost opposite language. The pitch rewarded boldness. The next twelve months reward something harder: saying no without damage, delivering bad news before it is discovered, defending numbers that missed, and asking for more when you have not been perfect.

Five more dungeons. A brand manager who never chose you, a hall of small favours that bleeds you dry, a community crisis at 8:40 in the morning, a finance director with the figures printed, and finally the throne room again — twelve months older, with a year of evidence on the table.

Your class carries over. Your reputation does not. Do not fumble.

Part I — The Parisian Conquest — covers winning the account. You do not need it to play this, but the executives here remember you.
