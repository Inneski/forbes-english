Used to — Past Habits & States

We use used to to talk about things that happened regularly in the past, or states that existed before but no longer do. Venezuela — with its extraordinary oil history, dramatic landscapes, and vibrant culture — gives us vivid, real-world context to practise this structure.

[+]  Subject  +  used to  +  base verb  +  ...
[−]  Subject  +  didn't use to  +  base verb
[?]  Did  + subject  +  use to  +  base verb  ?

Be used to + -ing — Familiarity & Habit

Be used to + -ing describes something that feels normal or familiar — something you are accustomed to doing now or in the past. Unlike used to + base verb, this structure is about comfort and familiarity, not a lost past habit. The verb be changes tense; the -ing form stays the same.

[present]  Subject  +  am / is / are used to  +  verb-ing
[past]    Subject  +  was / were used to  +  verb-ing
[neg]     Subject  +  isn't / wasn't used to  +  verb-ing
[?]       Is / Was  + subject  +  used to  +  verb-ing  ?

Side by Side

Compare the two structures

used to + base verb (past habit) be used to + -ing (familiarity)
Describes a past habit that stopped Describes something that feels normal / familiar
Only refers to the past — it no longer happens Can be present, past, or future depending on the verb be
She used to wake up at 5am to catch the bus to Caracas. She is used to waking up early — she's lived in the countryside all her life.
Venezuelans used to eat three full meals a day with ease. He wasn't used to eating just arepas — he grew up near the coast with fresh fish.
Always followed by the base verb (infinitive) Always followed by the -ing form (gerund)
