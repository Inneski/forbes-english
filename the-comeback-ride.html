Who gets to call it cool?

Writing in British GQ in 2023, Charlie Thomas saw a similar appeal in smaller motorcycles. Take Royal Enfield’s Meteor. A modest engine and straightforward controls sound like an odd recipe for excitement. Yet a rider might remember the beat of that engine long after forgetting what the speedometer said. For Thomas, part of the attraction lies in how the bike feels.

Imagine two people looking at the same round dial in a showroom. One remembers her father’s bike. The other has never ridden and is relieved to see just one thing to read. Both ask for a test ride. The dealer calls that a victory for nostalgia. He might want to ask the second customer first.

Language in use

Changing your mind

grow on someone
  • You did not like it at first; slowly, you start to like it.
  • The THING is the subject: IT GREW ON ME. Never “I grew on it”.

The square headlight grew on me after a couple of weeks.

win someone over
  • Something persuades you to like it or agree with it, often after doubts.
  • A pronoun goes in the middle: IT WON ME OVER, not “won over me”.

Then I heard the engine. That sound won me over.

Language in use

Looking back, paying for it

hark back to something
  • It reminds you of, or copies the style of, an earlier time.
  • A little formal; + a noun: it HARKS BACK TO the 1960s.

The cream paint harks back to the scooters of the sixties.

come at a price
  • Something good brings a disadvantage with it.
  • The good thing is the subject: SIMPLICITY COMES AT A PRICE.

That clean display comes at a price: the mileage is hidden in a menu.

Careful claims

Say what the data shows

is consistent with
  • The data fits the idea, but other ideas could fit it too.
  • It does not say the idea is true.

The ranking is consistent with a preference for simpler bikes.

may partly explain
  • One possible cause among several, and you are not sure.
  • Two ways of being careful in one phrase: MAY and PARTLY.

A low price may partly explain the ranking too.

proves
  • Only for something the data settles completely.
  • Sales figures almost never prove a reason.

That proves riders are tired of complicated bikes. ✗
