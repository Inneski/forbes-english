Activity 3 · Reading

Safety Briefing — Unit 7 — 1/2

The turnaround at Refinery Unit 7 is scheduled to begin on Monday morning. As deputy lead of the fire response team, Maria has to prepare her crew before the shutdown begins. She checks that all personal protective equipment (PPE) is available — helmets, fire-resistant suits, gloves, and self-contained breathing apparatus.

During a turnaround, workers isolate each piece of equipment before starting any work. This means closing valves, releasing pressure, and locking out energy sources. The process is called lockout/tagout (LOTO). Maria's team is responsible for monitoring the area while the maintenance crew carries out inspections and renovations.

Activity 3 · Reading

Safety Briefing — Unit 7 — 2/2

A key part of Maria's role is conducting regular hot work permits. Whenever a worker needs to use equipment that produces heat or sparks — such as a welding torch — Maria must assess the risk, check for flammable vapours, and sign the permit before work can start. She also coordinates with the shift supervisor to schedule breaks and ensure that fire extinguishers are charged and accessible at all times.

By the end of the first week, most of the vessel inspections are complete. The team found two hairline cracks in a heat exchanger that require immediate welding repair. Maria files a detailed incident report and briefs the site manager on the findings before the day shift ends.
