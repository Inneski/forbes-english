The Square

Forty-four words, one blocky afternoon

A2 · Elementary Vocabulary 24 slides
Things in the square

Inside a building

window

An opening in a wall, with glass in it. Light comes in; you look out.

She opened the window.

door

You open it to go into a room, a building or a car.

Please close the door.

bathroom

The room where you wash.

The bathroom is upstairs.

rug

A small, thick cloth on the floor. A carpet covers the whole floor; a rug does not.

A blue rug under the table.

Things in the square

In your bag

bag

A soft container, open at the top, for carrying things.

My books are in my bag.

pocket

A small bag sewn into your clothes.

The coin is in his pocket.

coin

Flat, round money made of metal.

a two-euro coin

magnifying glass

A lens with a handle. It makes small things look big.

He read the small print with a magnifying glass.

Things in the square

To read, to play, to do

magazine

A thin book with many pictures. It comes out every week or month.

a football magazine

newspaper

Big sheets of paper with today's news. It comes out every day.

Dad reads the newspaper at breakfast.

board games

Games you play on a board, moving pieces — chess, for example.

We play board games on Sunday.

homework

School work you do at home. No plural: never homeworks.

I have a lot of homework.

Things in the square

On you, and on the table

clothes

What you wear: trousers, dresses, jackets. Always plural.

Put your clothes on.

swim trunks

Short trousers a man wears to swim. Plural too.

He forgot his swim trunks.

toast

Bread made brown and hot. Say a piece of toast, not a toast.

Two pieces of toast, please.

friend

A person you know and like.

She is my best friend.

Things people do

What your hands do

push

Move something away from you.

Push the door.

pull

Move something towards you.

Pull the door.

reach

Put your arm out to touch or take something.

She reached for the coin.

hit

Bring your hand, or a bat, hard against something.

He hit the ball.

Things people do

Hands, and where you go

scratch

Mark a surface with something sharp.

The cat scratched the door.

build

Put parts together: a house, a wall, a machine.

They built a bridge.

make

Put things together to create something. Wider than build — you make a cake, but you build a house.

I made a cake.

walk

Move on your feet, one foot always on the ground.

We walked across the square.

Things people do

Sound, direction, mind

whistle

Push air through your lips and make a high, clear sound.

He whistled for the dog.

turn

Move on to a new point — a new subject, or a new direction.

Now let us turn to page ten.

think

Have an opinion or an idea about something.

I think it is true.

know

Have the information in your head already.

I know her name.

Ideas, feelings, small words

Starting, leaving, finishing

start

When something begins to happen.

The event starts at seven.

finish

Bring a job to an end. Complete it.

Finish your homework.

desert

Leave people who still need you. The verb is de-SERT; the dry, sandy DE-sert is the noun.

He deserted his friends.

event

Something that happens, usually planned and important.

The market is the big event of the week.

Ideas, feelings, small words

True, false, bad

true

In agreement with the facts.

It is true that Tuesday follows Monday.

false

Not true.

That answer is false.

bad

Of poor quality, or of a low standard.

bad weather, a bad film

bad boy

A man who does not follow the rules — often said with a smile.

the bad boy of Formula One

Ideas, feelings, small words

Feelings, and how much

angry

Very annoyed. Mad.

Dad was angry about the window.

important

Of great value or effect. It matters.

an important event

very

To a high degree. It makes an adjective stronger.

very angry, very important

sometimes

Occasionally — not all of the time.

Sometimes I walk to school.

Ideas, feelings, small words

The four smallest words

a

Use it the first time you name something.

I saw a door. The door was blue.

in

Inside something, or surrounded by it.

The coin is in my pocket.

they

Two or more people or things you have already named.

My friends came. They were late.

ciao

An informal hello — and an informal goodbye. Italian, and English borrowed it.

Ciao! See you tomorrow.
