Reading Passage — About Eckhart Tolle

Eckhart Tolle was born Ulrich Leonard Tolle in 1948 in Lünen, Germany. By his late twenties, he had suffered from prolonged periods of deep depression and anxiety. Then, at the age of twenty-nine, he underwent what he described as a profound inner transformation, which became the foundation of his later teachings.

Tolle's central message is deceptively simple: most human suffering arises not from external circumstances, but from an over-identification with one's own thoughts. He argues that the mind has a tendency to dwell on the past or project itself into the future, thereby missing what he calls "the Now" — the only moment in which life truly exists. In his view, the incessant mental chatter that occupies our attention is the primary obstacle to inner peace.

His book The Power of Now, published in 1997, initially attracted little attention. However, after Oprah Winfrey recommended it in 2000, it rose to international prominence and has since sold over ten million copies worldwide. A follow-up work, A New Earth, was similarly endorsed by Winfrey and introduced the concept of the "pain-body" — a semi-autonomous accumulation of emotional pain that, Tolle contends, feeds on negative thinking.

Tolle does not align himself with any single religion; rather, he draws on insights from Buddhism, Christianity, and other traditions. Critics have questioned whether his ideas are truly original or represent a skillful synthesis of existing spiritual philosophies. Nonetheless, his influence on popular culture and the wellness industry has been substantial, and he remains one of the best-selling spiritual authors of the twenty-first century.
