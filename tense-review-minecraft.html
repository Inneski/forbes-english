Tenses in the Overworld

Twelve tenses, thirty questions, and the three decisions that pick between them

B2 · Upper-intermediate The twelve tenses 21 slides
Before the questions

Three questions choose the tense

When?

Past, present or future. This picks the auxiliary and nothing else: was, is, will be. Every one of the twelve tenses answers this first.

Get this wrong and no amount of aspect will rescue the sentence.

Finished, or still running?

The simple form reports a whole event: he built the portal. The continuous shows it in progress and unfinished at that moment: he was building the portal.

This is why an interruption takes the continuous and the interruption itself takes the simple.

Finished before what?

The perfect ties one time to another. Had built is finished before a past point; has built before now; will have built before a future point.

Twelve tenses is three times two times two. Nothing else is going on.

The family learners avoid

Perfect continuous: how long, up to when

What it adds

Have been + -ing takes the perfect’s link between two times and asks how long the activity ran up to the later one. I have been mining all morning counts the morning.

The simple perfect counts results; the continuous counts duration.

All three times

She has been crafting (up to now), he had been carrying it (up to a past moment), I will have been working nine hours (up to a future moment).

The auxiliary changes; been + -ing never does.

The tell in the sentence

A stretch of time (for three days, all morning, for nine hours) next to a boundary (by the time…, since…) is asking for a perfect continuous.

No duration in the sentence? Then the simple perfect is almost always right.

The two traps

Where B2 loses the marks

Finished time blocks the present perfect

Last year, in 2011, yesterday close the time off, and the present perfect needs it open. I have visited the End last year is not English — it is I visited.

At the time of writing and since 1.16 keep it open, so those take the perfect.

Time clauses do not take will

After when, by the time, as soon as, until, English uses a present form for future meaning: when the player enters the Deep Dark…

The main clause still takes will. Only the time clause drops it.

While wants something in progress

While and as set up a background action, so they take a continuous form. The thing that cuts across it — the Creeper, the Ghast — is a single event and takes the simple.

While the Wither has destroyed fails on this; it wants was destroying.
