English Grammar Examination

Thus Spoke Grammar

A Philosophical Journey Through the English Language

❧   ✦   ❧

You stand at the crossroads of language and philosophy. Each question below draws from the writings of Friedrich Nietzsche. Conquer grammar — or grammar conquers you.

I
Articles — Der, die, das... but in English

Choose the correct article (a / an / the / no article) to complete each sentence.

Nietzsche believed that ___ Übermensch must create his own values.

💡 We use the because "Übermensch" refers to a specific concept already known to both speaker and listener.

He was ___ extraordinary philosopher who challenged all of Western thought.

💡 Use an before words beginning with a vowel sound — "extraordinary" starts with the sound /ɛ/.

"___ God is dead," Nietzsche wrote in The Gay Science.

💡 Names and proper nouns treated as unique entities (like "God" in monotheistic usage) take no article.
II
Verb Tenses — The Eternal Recurrence of Grammar

Select the correct verb form to complete the sentence.

Nietzsche ___ in Basel when he first conceived of the will to power.

💡 Use past continuous (was living) for a background action in progress when another event occurred.

By the time he collapsed in Turin, Nietzsche ___ ten major works.

💡 Use past perfect (had written) for an action completed before a specific past moment.

Philosophers around the world still ___ Nietzsche's ideas today.

💡 Simple present (study) is used for habits and ongoing truths. "Still... today" confirms this.
III
Prepositions — Navigating the Abyss

Choose the correct preposition to complete each sentence.

Nietzsche was born ___ October 15, 1844, in the small town of Röcken.

💡 On is used with specific dates. Remember: on Monday, in October, at noon.

He suffered ___ severe migraines throughout most of his adult life.

💡 "Suffer from" is the fixed collocation for illnesses and conditions in English.

Beyond Good and Evil had a profound influence ___ twentieth-century thought.

💡 "Influence on" is the standard collocation — don't confuse it with "influence over," which implies control.
IV
Conditionals — What If the Eternal Returns?

Select the grammatically correct conditional form.

"If you gaze long enough into an abyss, the abyss gazes also into you."

— Beyond Good and Evil

If Nietzsche ___ alive today, he would probably reject social media entirely.

💡 Second conditional uses "if + past subjunctive (were)" for hypothetical situations. "If I were you..." is the classic model.

If he ___ his sister's manipulation of his manuscripts, his legacy might be very different.

💡 Third conditional uses "if + past perfect" to talk about unreal past situations and their hypothetical results.
V
Active vs. Passive Voice — The Will to Power… of Verbs

Identify whether each sentence is in the Active (A) or Passive (P) voice.

"Thus Spoke Zarathustra was written by Nietzsche in just ten days."

💡 Passive voice: the subject ("Zarathustra") receives the action. Form: to be + past participle.

"Nietzsche rejected the conventional morality of his time."

💡 Active voice: the subject (Nietzsche) performs the action directly. Preferred for clarity and directness.

"His works have been misinterpreted by nationalist movements for over a century."

💡 Passive voice in present perfect: have/has + been + past participle. Used here to emphasize the subject, not the agent.
