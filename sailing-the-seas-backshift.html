The Backshift Current

Ana says: “I am tired.” An hour later you tell a friend: she said she was tired. Between saying and reporting, a current runs, and it carries every tense one step back into the past. Read each line across, from the words as they were said to the words as you report them.

Reading the current

One step back, every time

The reporting verb is in the past (said, told me), so what was said moves back with it. Each tense has exactly one place to go.

Present simple drifts to past simple

am / is to was, work to worked.

“I work here.” He said he worked there.

Present continuous drifts to past continuous

am leaving to was leaving.

“I'm leaving.” She said she was leaving.

Present perfect drifts to past perfect

have finished to had finished.

“I've finished.” He said he had finished.

Past simple drifts to past perfect too

saw to had seen. Two harbours feed one.

“I saw it.” She said she had seen it.

Will drifts to would, can to could

The modals have a past form of their own.

“I'll help.” He said he would help.

Say or tell

Say takes no person; tell must have one.

She said she was tired. She told me she was tired.

The drifting buoys and the still rocks

What moves with the tense, and what cannot move

A report is made later and somewhere else, so the words for time and place drift too. And some forms are already as far back as English goes.

The drifting buoys

These change when the time or the place of the report is different.

        Signal words

        You are in Direct Harbour

        • quotation marks
        • the speaker's own I and my
        • today, tomorrow, here
        • the original tense
        • “I'll help,” he said.
        • a comma before the quotation

        You are in Reported Harbour

        • said (that), told me (that)
        • he, she, his, her
        • that day, the next day, there
        • one tense further back
        • He said he would help.
        • no quotation marks

        One test: are there quotation marks? If not, check the tense, the pronoun and the time word, in that order.
