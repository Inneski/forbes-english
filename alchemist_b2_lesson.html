The Alchemist

A reading, vocabulary and grammar lesson inspired by Paulo Coelho's novel about a shepherd boy, a recurring dream, and the journey to find one's "Personal Legend."

Warm-up

Before you read

Discuss these questions with a partner, or think about your own answers before starting the lesson.

  • Do you believe some events in life are guided by fate, or is everything down to chance and choice?
  • Have you ever given up something comfortable to pursue a dream? What happened?
  • What do you know about alchemy? What was it trying to achieve?
Reading

The story so far

Santiago is a young shepherd from Andalusia who is content with his simple life, travelling the countryside with his flock. After a strange dream repeats itself, insisting that a treasure awaits him near the Egyptian pyramids, he decides to consult a fortune-teller and later meets an old man who claims to be a king in disguise. The old man persuades Santiago that he must abandon his flock and set off in search of his "Personal Legend" — the unique purpose each person is destined to fulfil.

Santiago's journey takes him across North Africa, where he is robbed of his money almost immediately and forced to start again from nothing. He works for a crystal merchant, learns patience, and eventually joins a caravan crossing the desert towards an oasis. Along the way he meets an Englishman obsessed with alchemy, falls in love with a woman named Fatima, and is tested repeatedly by hardship, war between rival tribes, and his own doubts.

Eventually Santiago meets an alchemist who teaches him to listen to the desert, the wind and the sun, and to trust what the novel calls the "Soul of the World." Through a series of trials, Santiago learns that the treasure he has been chasing was never only a physical thing buried in the ground; the real reward is the wisdom gained from the pursuit itself.

Vocabulary

Key words from the story

Look at these words before you try the exercise below.

omen — a sign believed to predict a future event
flock — a group of sheep or goats
caravan — a group of travellers crossing a desert together
oasis — a fertile spot with water in a desert
pursue — to follow or chase something with determination
merchant — a person who buys and sells goods for profit
destined — certain to happen or be, as if planned by fate
hardship — severe difficulty or suffering
trial — a difficult test of one's abilities or patience
wisdom — the quality of having good judgement from experience
Grammar focus · Past Perfect in narrative

Telling a story in order

We often use the Past Perfect (had + past participle) to show that one past event happened before another past event in a story. Compare:

Santiago sold his sheep because he had decided to search for the treasure.

(First: he decided to search for treasure. Then, as a result: he sold his sheep.)

Speaking

Discuss

  • The alchemist tells Santiago that "the fear of suffering is worse than the suffering itself." Do you agree?
  • Santiago loses everything twice during his journey. How might this be useful preparation for a modern career change?
  • The old man says everyone has a "Personal Legend" but most people give up on it. Why do you think people abandon their dreams?
  • Would you describe The Alchemist as more about destiny or about persistence? Explain your view.
