The Truth About the Harry Quebert Affair

A literary mystery lesson built around narrative tenses and thriller vocabulary — investigate a decades-old disappearance, one grammar clue at a time.

iCase File: Aurora, New Hampshire

In the summer of 1975, a fifteen-year-old girl named Nola disappeared from the quiet town of Aurora. Thirty-three years later, the case reopened when workers found human remains buried in the garden of Harry Quebert, a celebrated novelist who had been living peacefully in the town ever since. Quebert's former student, a young writer named Marcus Goldman, was struggling with a severe case of writer's block when the scandal broke. Convinced that his mentor was innocent, Marcus travelled to Aurora to dig deeper into the affair himself. As he interviewed old neighbours and searched through forgotten letters, he slowly uncovered a web of lies that had protected the town for over three decades.

A short original synopsis written for this lesson — not an excerpt from the novel.

iiGrammar Focus: Narrative Tenses

Storytelling in English usually mixes three past tenses. Watch the colour of each tag as you answer.

Past Simple Past Continuous Past Perfect

Past Simple moves the main story forward · Past Continuous sets the background scene or gets interrupted · Past Perfect shows what had already happened before the main story.
