# KRAKEN: THE BLACK TIDE — story bible (binding for every writing agent)

A Block Camp RPG. Jaws, moved to the west coast of Scotland, with a kraken
instead of a shark. Written to `docs/CHATGPT-RPG-BRIEF.md` and
`lesson-template/build/rpg/README.md`.

## Fixed facts

| | |
|---|---|
| Title | KRAKEN: THE BLACK TIDE |
| Grammar | **Present Perfect** (have/has + past participle) — and nothing else |
| Level | **B1** |
| World | The west coast of Scotland — a sea loch, late August |
| Accent | `#2E7D65` (Block Camp camp 7, Present Perfect) |
| Scoring | 5 points a question, 4 marker barrels, 3 chances, 18 questions per path, max 90, pass 75 |

## Cast — use these names and nothing else

- **Isla Brodie** — you. The new police sergeant in Kilmara, from the city.
  She does not like deep water and has never worked on a coast before.
- **Dr. Maren Hoy** — marine biologist from the Oban lab. Young, certain,
  carries a sonar case. Has studied squid for years and has never seen one
  this size.
- **Angus Quinn** — old creel fisherman, skipper of the *Selkie*. Scarred
  forearm. He has hunted this thing before and nobody believed him.
- **Provost Baird** — keeps the harbour open. Regatta Week brings the money.

## Places

- **Kilmara** — the village, a harbour on a sea loch.
- **The Sound** — the deep water outside the harbour mouth.
- **The Corry** — a whirlpool between two islands. The kraken's water.
- The *Selkie* — Quinn's boat. The *Morag* — the wreck of a trawler.

## Tone

Jaws' shape: the thing has already killed, the town will not close the water,
three people go out in a boat that is too small. Tense, not gory. Nobody dies
on screen. A lost boat and a man pulled out of the water is as far as it goes.

## Hard rules for every line you write

1. **Word budgets are walls, not aims.** The panel does not scroll.
   - `title` 3-4 words, **max 5**
   - `act` 4 words, **max 6**
   - `story` 7-10 words, **max 28**
   - `clue` 10 words, **max 16**
   - `prompt` 7 words, **max 13**
   - each option 25 characters, **max 45**
   - `explanation` 12 words, **max 25**
2. **Every question tests Present Perfect and nothing else.** The two wrong
   options are real B1 learner errors: "have saw", "has went", "have been
   here since three weeks", "I have seen him yesterday", "did you ever see",
   "he have gone". Never nonsense, never a joke option.
3. **The clue carries the evidence, never the answer.** If the clue already
   uses the target form, the question is free — rewrite it.
4. **Every scene stands alone.** The player reads one panel and the last one
   is gone. Never "as we saw", never "remember the".
5. **Straight apostrophes and quotes only.** No curly quotes anywhere.
6. Titles in CAPITALS. The player is "you". No question depends on knowing
   the story — only on the grammar.
7. **Never mention a previous version of anything.**
8. The explanation **names the rule**, it does not repeat the answer:
   "Present Perfect for a past action with a result now: has + taken. Took
   needs a finished time."
9. Answer options are English and are never translated or glossed.
10. **Grammar tokens go in CAPS** — HAVE / HAS + PAST PARTICIPLE, SINCE, FOR,
    YET, ALREADY, JUST, EVER, NEVER, BEEN, GONE. Addendum §A is binding and
    has worked examples; read it before writing a single explanation.

## Endings

- `master` — every answer right. The harbour opens again.

- `failed` — out of chances. The Selkie has not come back in.

# Addendum — the house conventions the first draft missed

The whole-script critic caught seven blockers on the first pass. These are the
rules that produced them. They are binding on every rewrite.

## A. Grammar tokens go in CAPS

**This is a hardwired house rule and it is not in HOUSE-STYLE.md or
rpg/README.md.** It lives in the shipped builders. Read the rule cards in
`build_lost_yellow_road.py` before writing any card, note or explanation.

Never write a grammar token as ordinary lowercase prose. Put the target form
in CAPS and give the pattern as `subject + AUX + verb`, not as a description:

    card name   FORM - HAVE / HAS + PAST PARTICIPLE
    card text   The tide HAS turned. The lamps HAVE burned all night.
    note        A finished time takes the PAST SIMPLE: the boat WENT out at six.
    explanation NEVER already means not at any time, so HAVE stays positive:
                HAVE NEVER + HELD. HELD, not HOLD.

Tense NAMES stay in title case - Present Perfect, Past Simple - and the forms
inside them go in CAPS. Cited words take double quotes only when they are not
already in CAPS. Every explanation in this lesson uses the same style; a seam
where one chapter is lowercase and the next is CAPS is a blocker.

## B. Option lengths: aim for a tie, never for a long key

HOUSE-STYLE is explicit: **the correct option must never be the longest one,
and the fix is to lengthen the distractors, never to shorten the key.** Three
options of equal length is the target.

But a key that is always the SHORTEST is the same free elimination in the
other direction, and the first draft was shortest or tied-shortest thirteen
times out of twenty-five. So: **write all three options within a few
characters of each other.** No option should be solvable by measuring it.

The other surface tells to kill, because a learner who has learned nothing can
use them:
- the same error type in consecutive questions;
- one distractor being the only one that makes sense in the story, so the
  grammar never decides the answer;
- a distractor that is nonsense rather than a real learner error;
- a distractor eliminable by the clue alone.

## C. The timeline ledger - every scene must fit it

| when | scenes |
|---|---|
| Thursday, dawn to dusk | `drift` `bell` `provost` `hoy` `jar` `shed` |
| Friday, morning | `sound`, then the REEF or CAVES branch |
| Friday, afternoon | `wreck1` `wreck2` `wreck3` |
| Friday, dusk | `decision` |
| Friday, night | the CORRY branch or the NIGHT WATCH branch |
| Saturday, daylight | `barrels` `last`, and the endings |

Regatta Week starts on the Friday. Anything that happened before the story
starts happened on Monday, Tuesday or last winter - never on Friday.

**Quinn's arithmetic.** He went to sea at fourteen and has fished these waters
for forty years, so he is fifty-four now. The *Morag* went down in 1998,
twenty-eight years ago, when he was twenty-six and one of her crew.

**The boats.** The sea has taken two. One of them is the creel boat that comes
back empty in `drift`. So one berth stays empty, not two, and no scene may
count three missing boats.

## D. Who is where

- The *Selkie* carries you, Quinn and Hoy out of the harbour at `sound`.
- **REEF branch:** all three aboard the *Selkie* throughout.
- **CAVES branch:** the *Selkie* anchors off the headland. Quinn rows his punt
  in ahead of you; you and Hoy follow in the inflatable. At `cave4` Quinn is
  aboard the inflatable again and you run out to the waiting *Selkie*.
- Hoy has the sonar and must actually use it on whichever branch is taken.
- Everyone is back aboard the *Selkie* from `wreck1` onwards.

## E. Nothing is said twice

Two branches run in parallel and a player who replays takes the other one.
**No sentence, image, verb-and-object pair or reveal may appear in both.**
Specifically: the reef and the caves; the Corry and the night watch; and the
briefing must not print a string that is also a correct option.

Hold a reveal until the scene written to carry it: the bitten barrel is
discovered at `shed`, not trailed at `drift`.
