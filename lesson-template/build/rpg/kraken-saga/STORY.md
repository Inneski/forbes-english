# KRAKEN: THE BLACK TIDE — a saga in three parts

Jaws, moved to the west coast of Scotland, with a kraken instead of a shark.
Three parts, one story, written as a Block Camp RPG script with longer story
panels than a standard Block Camp game, and with two kinds of question:

- **[CCQ]** — a concept-checking question. You receive information (a
  shipping forecast, a harbour log, a radio call, something a character tells
  you) and then a character asks you a question about it. You answer from the
  information, not from the grammar.
- **[GRAMMAR]** — a gap in a line of dialogue. Present Perfect Simple,
  Present Perfect Continuous, and occasionally Past Simple where a finished
  time forces it. The two wrong options are real B1 learner errors.

Level B1. Grammar tokens in CAPS in every explanation. Straight quotes only.
Titles in CAPITALS. The player is "you"; the hero has a name. No name is
shared with Jaws.

## Cast and places

- **Sergeant Isla Munro** — you. New police sergeant in Inverbrae, six days
  in, from the city. Cannot swim well. Has a daughter, Kirsty, twelve.
- **Angus Tulloch** — creel fisherman, skipper of the *Kittiwake*. Fifty-four
  and looks seventy. A long pale scar down his right forearm. One of four
  survivors of the *Ossian*, 1998.
- **Dr Rhona Vass** — marine biologist from the Oban lab. Nine years on
  squid. Carries a silver sonar case and a scar on her wrist.
- **Provost Lachlan Menzies** — keeps the harbour open. Regatta Week is the
  money.
- **Fergus Brannan** — harbourmaster. Reads the forecast. Rings the bell.
- **Fiona and Ewan Sinclair** — a mother, and her son on the red kayak.
- **Sandy Weir** — race marshal in the inner bay.
- **Davie and Tam Lennox** — the creel boat that drifts in empty.
- **Dougie Ferrier** — fishes alone in the *Kestrel*. Nobody missed him.
- **The Docherty brothers** — two chancers from Glasgow with a RIB and a
  rifle, after the reward.
- **Inverbrae** — the village, a harbour on a sea loch.
- **The Narrows** — the deep water outside the harbour mouth.
- **The Cauldron** — a whirlpool between two islands. The kraken's water.
- **The *Kittiwake*** — Tulloch's boat. **The *Ossian*** — the trawler that
  went down in 1998.

**The one thing the engine cannot do yet.** A Block Camp panel does not
scroll and holds 28 words of story. These panels run 60–150 words. Either the
builder gets a paged story panel (a "MORE" tap before the question) or each
story block is split across two or three taps. The questions, clues and
options below are already inside the shipped budgets.

## Scoring, rewards and punishments

Each part is one playable game.

| | |
|---|---|
| Points | +5 a correct answer |
| Chances | 3 per part. A wrong answer costs one. At 0 the part ends in `failed` |
| Collectibles | 4 per part, earned at four named scenes. Miss one and the best ending is `missing` |
| Per path | 14 questions, max 70, pass 60 |

The collectibles are different in each part and the story pays them off:

| part | HUD label | the four things |
|---|---|---|
| One | EVIDENCE | the log page · the forecast slip · the beak ring · Tulloch's chart |
| Two | MARKER BARRELS | four yellow barrels, one at a time |
| Three | THE LAST FOUR | the gas bottle · the rope · the harpoon · the flare pistol |

Every wrong answer also has a one-line **story consequence** written under
the scene. These are flavour for the explanation line, not a second branch.

Route choices are real and every branch carries the same number of
questions and the same grammar slots, so both players score out of 70.

## How to read the shipping forecast — the briefing card for Part One

The forecast is a real thing and its words have exact meanings. The CCQs use
them, so the player is taught them before the first question.

| word | means |
|---|---|
| IMMINENT | within 6 hours of the time the forecast was issued |
| SOON | 6 to 12 hours after issue |
| LATER | more than 12 hours after issue |
| VEERING | the wind is turning clockwise (south, then southwest, then west) |
| BACKING | the wind is turning anticlockwise |
| Force 8 | a gale. Force 9, a severe gale |
| Sea state | smooth, slight, moderate, rough, very rough |
| Visibility | good, moderate, poor |

Sea areas: **Malin** and **Hebrides** are the two off the west coast.
Inshore: **Ardnamurchan Point to Mull of Kintyre**.

# PART ONE — INVERBRAE

**Cover lead (35 words max):** Two men have not come back. The regatta opens on
Friday and the Provost will not close the water. You are the new sergeant.
You have been in Inverbrae for six days.

**Chips:** `+5 CORRECT` · `4 EVIDENCE` · `3 CHANCES`

**Briefing cards:**
1. FORM — HAVE / HAS + PAST PARTICIPLE. *Two men HAVE NOT COME back.*
2. FORM — HAVE / HAS + BEEN + -ING. *The bell HAS BEEN RINGING since dawn.*
3. A FINISHED TIME takes the Past Simple. *The Ossian WENT DOWN in 1998.*
4. THE FORECAST — IMMINENT is inside 6 hours, SOON is 6 to 12, LATER is
   more than 12.
Note: KNOW, BELIEVE and HAVE (own) refuse the continuous: HAS KNOWN, never
HAS BEEN KNOWING.

### PROLOGUE — THE NARROWS, WEDNESDAY NIGHT  (story only, no question)

Ten o'clock, and the light has almost gone. Davie Lennox and his nephew Tam
are hauling creels at the mouth of the Narrows, where the deep water starts.
Tam has been fishing with his uncle for three summers and he knows the
sounds of the boat by heart: the winch, the creel on the rail, the gulls.

Tonight there are no gulls.

"Davie. Where have the birds gone?"

Davie doesn't answer. The rope on the winch has gone tight. Not creel-tight.
Tight like something has taken hold of it and is pulling it down, slowly,
with all the time in the world. The stern dips. The yellow marker barrel
rolls across the deck.

"Cut it," says Davie. "Cut the rope."

Tam has the knife in his hand when the water beside the boat goes white.

### 1.1 THE DRIFTING BOAT  [CCQ]
**Act:** CHAPTER 1 · INVERBRAE · THURSDAY · **Picture:** 03 · **Object:** the yellow barrel in the stern

**Story.** First light. You have been in Inverbrae six days and you have not
yet seen the harbour empty. It is empty now. The Lennox boat has drifted in
against the pier on its own, the mooring rope trailing in the water, the
wheelhouse door open. Nobody aboard. In the stern, one yellow marker barrel
lies on its side, chewed and split, as if a dog the size of a bus had
worried it and let it go.

Fergus Brannan, the harbourmaster, is waiting for you with the night log open
in his hands. He has not slept.

**You read — the harbour log, Wednesday night:**
> 20:40 — Lennox, creel boat, two crew, out to the Narrows. Due back 23:30.
> 23:30 — Not back.
> 00:15 — No answer on VHF. Tried three times.
> 05:50 — Boat drifted in against the pier. Empty.

**Brannan:** "I tried the radio when they were overdue. Read me the log,
sergeant. How long had they been overdue when I tried?"

- A) Forty-five minutes  ✔
- B) Six hours and twenty minutes
- C) About fifteen minutes

**Why:** the boat was due at 23:30 and the first radio call was at 00:15.
Six hours after that is when the boat drifted in, not when he called.

**Right:** +5. EVIDENCE 1/4 — the log page. Brannan tears it out and gives it
to you. "Keep that. Somebody's going to ask."
**Wrong:** -1 chance. "You've read it wrong. Read the next one right."

### 1.2 THE HARBOUR BELL  [GRAMMAR]
**Act:** CHAPTER 1 · INVERBRAE · THURSDAY · **Picture:** 04 · **Object:** the brass bell

**Story.** There is a brass bell on a stone post at the head of the pier. It
is rung when a boat is lost. Old Mrs Rennie, who has lived above the harbour
for eighty years, tells you it has not been rung since the *Ossian* went down,
and that was before you were born. Brannan has the rope in his hand and he is
looking at you, because you are the police and it is your call.

Behind him the village has started to come down the hill. Nobody has said
the word yet.

**You hear:**
**Brannan:** "Say it, sergeant, and I'll ring it."
**You:** "Ring it. Two men ___ back."

- A) didn't came
- B) haven't come  ✔
- C) haven't came

**Why:** the negative Present Perfect is HAVEN'T + PAST PARTICIPLE. COME is
the participle; CAME is the Past Simple and never follows HAVE.

**Right:** +5. The bell rings. Mrs Rennie sits down on the step.
**Wrong:** -1 chance. Brannan rings it anyway. "You'll learn the words for
it. Everybody here has."

### 1.3 THE PROVOST  [GRAMMAR]
**Act:** CHAPTER 1 · INVERBRAE · THURSDAY · **Picture:** 05 · **Object:** the red regatta buoy

**Story.** Provost Menzies finds you on the quay at nine, where the bunting has
been going up all week for Regatta Week. Menzies has been Provost for eleven
years and has never lost a vote. He stands beside a big red mooring buoy
that has just been painted for the visitors, and he does not look at the
Lennox boat once.

**You hear:**
**Menzies:** "This town has had three bad winters, sergeant. It has not had a
bad August, and it will not start now. Regatta Week is four hundred visitors
and every bed in the village."
**You:** "Two men haven't come home."
**Menzies:** "Two men had an accident. It happens on a coast. Tell me, in
your career, ___ a harbour in August? No. Because nobody has."

- A) did you ever closed
- B) have you ever closing
- C) have you ever closed  ✔

**Why:** EVER goes in a Present Perfect question: HAVE + subject + EVER +
PAST PARTICIPLE. DID takes the base verb, not CLOSED; HAVE takes the
participle, not CLOSE.

**Right:** +5. Menzies gives you one thing: a police boat on the water for the
regatta, at the council's expense. "That is what you get. Not the harbour."
**Wrong:** -1 chance. Menzies has already stopped listening.

### 1.4 THE FORECAST  [CCQ]
**Act:** CHAPTER 1 · INVERBRAE · THURSDAY · **Picture:** 06 · **Object:** the radio on the harbourmaster's desk

**Story.** Brannan's office is a wooden hut at the end of the pier with a
kettle, a barometer and a radio that has been on since 1987. At five past
five he turns it up. He tells you the rules before it starts: IMMINENT means
inside six hours, SOON means six to twelve, LATER means more than twelve, and
the clock starts from the time the forecast is issued.

**You hear — the shipping forecast, issued 17:05 Thursday:**
> "Malin, Hebrides. Southwesterly 3 or 4, increasing 5 to 7 later. Slight
> or moderate, becoming rough later. Fair. Good."
> "Inshore, Ardnamurchan Point to Mull of Kintyre. Southwesterly 3 or 4,
> increasing 5 to 7 later. Smooth or slight, becoming moderate."

**Brannan:** "The regatta starts at ten tomorrow morning. If the forecast is
right about LATER, what's the earliest the wind can get up to force 7?"

- A) After five tomorrow morning  ✔
- B) At ten tomorrow, when the racing starts
- C) Before midnight tonight

**Why:** LATER means more than twelve hours after the time of issue. Twelve
hours after 17:05 is 05:05 on Friday. It can come after that; nothing in the
forecast says it will wait for the racing.

**Right:** +5. EVIDENCE 2/4 — the forecast slip. Brannan writes it out in
pencil and hands it over. "Show him that. He won't read it, but show him."
**Wrong:** -1 chance. "You've got the words the wrong way round, and out
there that's the difference between a boat and no boat."

### 1.5 THE COLLIE  [GRAMMAR]
**Act:** CHAPTER 1 · INVERBRAE · FRIDAY · **Picture:** 07 · **Object:** the yellow ball on the water

**Story.** Friday, ten o'clock, and the regatta opens in sunshine. There are
children on paddleboards inside the moorings, a pipe band on the quay and a
black-and-white collie on the slipway chasing a yellow ball into the water,
again and again. The dog's owner is a man in a deckchair with a flask.

You are on the pier with the radio Menzies has paid for. You have been
watching the water for an hour and you have not seen a single fish jump. The
gulls have gone somewhere else.

The ball floats back to the slip on its own. The collie does not.

**You hear:**
**The owner:** "Bracken! Here, boy! He's been swimming here every summer of
his life."
**You, on the radio:** "Brannan. Get the boards in. The dog ___."

- A) just goes under
- B) has just gone under  ✔
- C) has just went under

**Why:** JUST sits between HAS and the PAST PARTICIPLE: HAS JUST GONE. WENT
is the Past Simple and never follows HAS; the Present Simple has no JUST-now
meaning here.

**Right:** +5. Brannan's launch is already moving.
**Wrong:** -1 chance. Ten seconds. It matters.

### 1.6 THE KAYAK  [CCQ]
**Act:** CHAPTER 1 · INVERBRAE · FRIDAY · **Picture:** 08 · **Object:** the red kayak lifting out of the water

**Story.** Beyond the moorings, where the harbour opens into the Narrows,
Ewan Sinclair is paddling a red kayak. He is fifteen and he has been told
twice to come in. A black arm comes out of the water beside him — not a fin,
an arm, thicker than a man, with a rim of wet light along it — and the kayak
goes up, not down, and then under.

You are on the pier. You cannot swim well and you do not move. The nearest
boat is a small green creel boat called the *Kittiwake*, and its skipper is
already turning.

**You hear — the VHF, a man's voice, very calm:**
> "Inverbrae lifeboat, Inverbrae lifeboat, this is Kittiwake. One person in the
> water, one kayak lost. I have him aboard. He is breathing. Request an
> ambulance at the slip. Over."

**Later, on the slip, the skipper of the Kittiwake — Angus Tulloch — asks
you:** "The lifeboat asked me what I had aboard from the water. What did I
tell them, sergeant? Because they'll ask you next."

- A) Two people, both breathing
- B) One kayak, nobody aboard it
- C) One person, breathing  ✔

**Why:** the call says one person in the water and one kayak lost. The kayak
is gone; the boy is aboard and breathing.

**Right:** +5. Ewan Sinclair goes to Oban in the ambulance with a broken arm.
He has not said a word since he came out of the water.
**Wrong:** -1 chance. Tulloch looks at you for a long second. "You were on
the pier. I saw you."

### 1.7 MRS SINCLAIR  [GRAMMAR]
**Act:** CHAPTER 1 · INVERBRAE · FRIDAY · **Picture:** 09 · **Object:** the ambulance's blue light

**Story.** Fiona Sinclair comes down the slip in the rain with her hair
plastered to her face and she does not shout. That is worse. She waits until
the ambulance doors are shut on her son and then she walks up to you in
front of the whole village.

**You hear:**
**Sinclair:** "You knew. There was something in the Narrows and you knew.
You ___ since Thursday, and you let them race."
**You:** "I've been here six days."
**Sinclair:** "And my son has been here fifteen years."

- A) have known  ✔
- B) have been knowing
- C) are knowing

**Why:** KNOW is a state verb and refuses the continuous. Duration up to now
with SINCE takes HAVE KNOWN, never HAVE BEEN KNOWING, and never the Present
Continuous.

**Right:** +5. You have no answer for her, but you have the grammar.
**Wrong:** -1 chance. You have no answer for her either.

### 1.8 THE VILLAGE HALL  [CCQ]
**Act:** CHAPTER 1 · INVERBRAE · FRIDAY NIGHT · **Picture:** 10 · **Object:** the gaff hook on the radiator

**Story.** The village hall, eight o'clock, every chair taken and people
standing at the back. Menzies is at the front with a microphone he does not
need. At the back, by the radiator, stands a man in an oiled green jumper
with a flat cap and a long pale scar down his right forearm. Angus Tulloch.
When the noise gets too much he drags the point of a steel gaff hook down
the radiator, slowly, and the room goes silent.

**You hear:**
**Menzies:** "The council has put up ten thousand pounds for whoever brings it
in. Ten thousand. There are boats in this harbour that could use it."
**Tulloch:** "Ten thousand won't buy you the beak, Provost. Ten will buy you
a photograph and a funeral. Thirty thousand, my boat, my crew, my way. For
thirty I'll bring you the beak and you'll never see it on this coast again.
I've been after this thing since 1998. I'll not rush it for ten."

Dr Rhona Vass has been standing beside you since the radiator. She arrived
off the Oban road an hour ago with a silver instrument case, and she has not
stopped writing.

**Vass:** "So what does he actually promise for thirty thousand?"

- A) To have it caught before Sunday
- B) The beak, and the animal gone  ✔
- C) A photograph and the ten thousand

**Why:** Tulloch names a price, a method and a result: the beak and the
animal gone from the coast. He says nothing about when. The photograph is
what he says ten thousand buys.

**Right:** +5. Vass: "Then he's the only person in this room who's talking
about the right animal."
**Wrong:** -1 chance. Vass: "Listen to him again. He said it twice."

### 1.9 THE JAR  [CCQ]
**Act:** CHAPTER 1 · INVERBRAE · SATURDAY · **Picture:** 11 · **Object:** the glass jar

**Story.** The village hall kitchen, Saturday morning, cold light through the
window. On the table is a wide glass jar of clear fluid and inside it,
turning slowly, is a black curved ring the size of your fist. Vass found it
jammed in the splintered gunwale of the Lennox boat. She has studied squid
for nine years, in Baja, in Norway, in the Sea of Cortez, and she has been
looking at this jar for two hours.

**You hear:**
**Vass:** "A squid's beak grows with the animal. A four-metre squid has a
beak about the size of my thumb. This ring is the size of my fist. The
animal that left it is fourteen metres at least. Maybe twenty. Nothing that
size has ever been recorded alive in these waters. Not once."
**You:** "You've seen a lot of squid."
**Vass:** "I have. I've never seen this."

**Vass:** "Here's the test, sergeant. If somebody hauls up a four-metre squid
this afternoon and hangs it on the crane, what size of beak should it have?"

- A) About the size of a fist
- B) About fourteen centimetres
- C) About the size of a thumb  ✔

**Why:** Vass gives the scale: four metres, a thumb-sized beak; fourteen
metres or more, a fist-sized beak. Fourteen is the animal's length, not the
beak's.

**Right:** +5. EVIDENCE 3/4 — the beak ring. Vass seals the jar and puts it in
your hands. "Don't let the Provost near it."
**Wrong:** -1 chance. "Then you'll believe the crane. Everybody will."

### 1.10 THE WRONG CATCH  [GRAMMAR]
**Act:** CHAPTER 1 · INVERBRAE · SATURDAY · **Picture:** 12 · **Object:** the squid on the harbour crane

**Story.** Midday, and the harbour is full of people again. Two brothers
from Glasgow called Docherty have come up the coast in an orange RIB with a
rifle and a net, and hanging from the harbour crane, grey and dripping, is a
squid four metres long. Menzies is standing beside it in his best coat.
Photographers. A cheer.

Vass walks under it with a tape measure and measures the beak. It is the size
of her thumb. She comes back to you white with anger and speaks very quietly.

**You hear:**
**Menzies, to the crowd:** "Inverbrae has caught its monster!"
**Vass, to you:** "That's not it. Look at the colour. It ___ for a week,
sergeant. It's been dead longer than the Lennox boat's been missing. They
found it. They didn't catch it."

- A) has been dead  ✔
- B) is dead since
- C) has dead

**Why:** a state that has lasted up to now takes HAS + BEEN + adjective +
FOR a period. IS ... SINCE is a learner error for duration, and BEEN cannot
be dropped after HAS.

**Right:** +5. Menzies catches your eye across the crowd and mouths one word:
*smile*.
**Wrong:** -1 chance. Vass walks away. "I'll explain it to somebody who
listens."

### 1.11a THE KNIFE  [GRAMMAR]
**Act:** CHAPTER 2 · THE KNIFE · SATURDAY NIGHT · **Picture:** 14 · **Object:** the filleting knife under the lamp

**Story.** Eleven o'clock on the quay. The crowd has gone and the squid hangs
in the light of one harbour lamp, turning. Vass has rolled her sleeves up. You
are holding a bucket and a torch and you have not asked what the bucket is
for.

**You hear:**
**Vass:** "Hold the torch steady. I've done this to a hundred of these and
never one that smelled like this. Pass me the bucket. I ___ my dinner yet
and I'd like to keep it that way."

- A) didn't have yet
- B) haven't had  ✔
- C) haven't have

**Why:** YET goes with the Present Perfect negative: HAVEN'T + HAD. HAVE
after HAVEN'T is the base form and wrong; DIDN'T with YET mixes a finished
tense with an unfinished word.

**Right:** +5. The knife goes in.
**Wrong:** -1 chance. Vass: "You hold the torch. I'll do the English."

### 1.12a THE STOMACH  [CCQ]
**Act:** CHAPTER 2 · THE KNIFE · SATURDAY NIGHT · **Picture:** 15 · **Object:** the plastic bottle in the tray

**Story.** What comes out is mackerel, half of a plastic bottle and a lot of
water. No rope. No paint. No cloth. Vass lays it all in a steel tray under
the lamp and looks at it for a long time.

**You hear:**
**Vass:** "Its last meal was mackerel and a bottle. The Lennox boat was taken
on Wednesday night. Ewan's kayak was red, and there isn't a flake of red in
here. This animal has been dead since before any of it happened."
**You:** "So what does its stomach prove?"
**Vass:** "You tell me. You're the police."

- A) It ate the Lennox men
- B) It took the red kayak
- C) It's not our animal  ✔

**Why:** no rope, no red paint, and it has been dead since before Wednesday.
It could not have taken the boat or the kayak.

**Right:** +5. Vass: "Now we know what it isn't. That's more than the Provost
knows."
**Wrong:** -1 chance. Vass stops explaining things to you.

### 1.11b THE TORCH  [GRAMMAR]
**Act:** CHAPTER 2 · THE KESTREL · SATURDAY NIGHT · **Picture:** 16 · **Object:** the torch beam on the Kestrel's crushed wheelhouse

**Story.** Eleven o'clock, flat calm, no moon. You take the police RIB out
past the moorings with Vass in the bow holding a torch. Dougie Ferrier fishes
alone and lives alone, so nobody missed him. His boat, the *Kestrel*, is off
the skerry, drifting, and the torch finds it before you do: the wheelhouse
crushed inward like a paper cup, the hull holed below the waterline.

**You hear:**
**Vass:** "He went out on Thursday. Nobody ___ him since. Nobody reported it
because there was nobody to report it."

- A) has seen  ✔
- B) has saw
- C) have seen

**Why:** NOBODY is singular and takes HAS. SEEN is the participle; SAW is the

**Right:** +5. The RIB drifts alongside.
**Wrong:** -1 chance. The torch shakes. That is not the grammar.

### 1.12b THE HOLE  [CCQ]
**Act:** CHAPTER 2 · THE KESTREL · SATURDAY NIGHT · **Picture:** 17 · **Object:** the round hole in the hull, lit from inside

**Story.** Vass leans over and pulls a black curved shard out of the planking
beside the hole. She holds it against the ring in the jar. Same curve, same
edge. Then she puts the torch through the hole and you both look.

**You hear:**
**Vass:** "A propeller cuts. A rock tears. Look at the edges. This is round,
forty centimetres across, and every plank is bent inwards. This was pushed.
Something outside the boat pushed the hull in."

Something moves under the crushed wheelhouse. You do not wait to see what.

**Vass, as you open the throttle:** "Well? What made the hole?"

- A) The skerry tore it open from below
- B) Something outside pushed it in  ✔
- C) Ferrier's own propeller cut it

**Why:** round, planks bent inward, pushed from outside. A propeller would
cut and a rock would tear; Vass rules out both.

**Right:** +5. Vass puts the shard in her pocket. "Two beaks, one animal."
**Wrong:** -1 chance. Vass: "You looked straight at it."

*Rejoin at 1.13.*

### 1.13 RACE DAY  [CCQ]
**Act:** CHAPTER 3 · RACE DAY · SUNDAY · **Picture:** 18 · **Object:** the capsized white dinghy in the inner bay

**Story.** Regatta Sunday, the big race, and Menzies has refused to cancel it.
You have the police RIB out, the lifeboat on standby and Tulloch's *Kittiwake*
sitting at the harbour mouth because he has decided to be there. In the
inner bay the junior fleet is racing in white dinghies. Your daughter Kirsty
is twelve, and she is in one of them.

At half past eleven two boys tow a black bin bag past the beach on a rope
and four hundred people scream and run. You are still shouting at the boys
when the radio goes.

**You hear — the VHF, Sandy Weir, race marshal, inner bay:**
> "Inverbrae harbour, this is race marshal, inner bay. Something has come
> through the moorings. Two dinghies over. I'm in the water. It's under
> the —"
**Brannan:** "Marshal, say again. How many in the water?"
**Weir:** "Six. Six children, two boats, and me. Get the boats in. Get
the —"

**Brannan, running beside you:** "How many people are in the water,
sergeant, and how many of them are children?"

- A) Six; two of them children
- B) Eight; six of them children
- C) Seven; six of them children  ✔

**Why:** six children and the marshal make seven people. Two is the number of
boats, not of children.

**Right:** +5. Every child comes out of the water. Kirsty comes out in
Tulloch's arms, in shock, without her boat. Sandy Weir comes out last, and
his dinghy does not come out at all.
**Wrong:** -1 chance. You went in. You cannot swim well. Tulloch pulled you
out too.

### 1.14 THE SIGNATURE  [GRAMMAR]
**Act:** CHAPTER 3 · RACE DAY · SUNDAY · **Picture:** 19 · **Object:** the council chequebook on Menzies's desk

**Story.** Menzies's office above the harbour, four o'clock, the regatta
bunting still up outside and nobody under it. You are wet to the chest. The
council chequebook is on the desk between you and Menzies has not touched it.
He looks ten years older than he did on Thursday.

**You hear:**
**You:** "You ___ that pier open for four days. Sign it."
**Menzies:** "I've lived here all my life, sergeant."

**Act:** CHAPTER 1 · THE CAULDRON · TUESDAY · **Picture:** 43 · **Object:** the smoke from the engine hatch

**Story.** Black smoke comes up through the engine hatch. Tulloch goes down
into it with a rag over his face and comes up again coughing, and when he
tries the starter the engine turns over once and stops.

**You hear:**
**Vass:** "You've run it too hard. It ___ smoking for an hour and you kept
going."
**Tulloch:** "It's been smoking for twenty years. It'll start."
It does not start.

- A) has been  ✔
- B) is being
- C) have been

**Why:** an activity running FOR a period up to now takes HAS + BEEN + -ING.
IT takes HAS, not HAVE; the Present Continuous cannot carry FOR AN HOUR up
to now.

**Right:** +5. The barrels have stopped moving. So has the boat. They are two
hundred metres apart and the water between them is starting to turn.
**Wrong:** -1 chance. Tulloch throws the spanner.

**Act:** CHAPTER 1 · THE CAULDRON · TUESDAY · **Picture:** 44 · **Object:** Vass's yellow handheld radio

**Wrong:** -1 chance. Vass takes the handheld back. "We have less than you
think."

### 3.9 THE WHEELHOUSE  [CCQ]
**Act:** CHAPTER 3 · THE LAST RUN · TUESDAY · **Picture:** 53 · **Object:** the eye, huge and gold-ringed, at the wheelhouse window

**You hear — Tulloch, in your head, from Sunday:**

- B) From above, at the head

**Why:** Tulloch's rule has three parts: wait for the eye, fire level, aim

**Wrong:** -1 chance. You fire early into the head and the harpoon skids

## Notes for the builder

- Every question has its key in the slot marked ✔. The keys are dealt
  17 / 17 / 17 across the three slots over the fifty-one questions.
- No correct option is the longest of its three. Where two tie, that is
  deliberate.
- The two branches in each part carry identical grammar slots in the same
  order, so a replay on the other branch meets the same points and nothing
  is said twice.
- The shipping forecast and radio calls are quoted text on the panel. They
  are not in the art: nothing in any plate carries lettering.
- Nobody dies on screen. Two men and a marshal's boat are lost before the
  story starts or off the panel; Tulloch goes over the side and the water
  closes; the endings decide whether he is found.
