Descent two · present simple passive

The way things are done

The tense of rules, processes and signs — and the passive is where it does most of its work. Nobody signs a notice we empty the bins on Fridays; they write bins are emptied on Fridays, because the bins are the point and the emptier is nobody in particular.

Descent briefing

When to use it

The passive is not a different tense. It is the {TENSE} with the doer let go — and the reason you let them go is always one of these six.

You do not know who

Nobody can tell you the doer, so the sentence does without one.

"The bins are emptied on Fridays."

The doer does not matter

The event is the news; who did it is a footnote.

"English is spoken here."

The doer is obvious

Saying it would be a waste of everyone's time.

"Tickets are checked at the barrier."

You would rather not say

The passive is how English removes a person from a sentence.

"Applications are reviewed in order of arrival."

Putting the important thing first

Whatever you put at the front is what the sentence is about.

"This bridge is inspected every two years."

Putting the doer back with by

When the doer is the point, it goes at the end.

"The accounts are audited by an outside firm."
Building the passive

How it's built

Affirmative

subject + am / is / are + past participle

"The office is cleaned every night."

Negative

the not goes after the first auxiliary

"These forms aren't accepted by email."

Questions

the first auxiliary moves to the front

"Is the room booked already?"

With the doer

… + by + doer, at the end

"The report is written by the duty officer."

Turning an active sentence around

Three moves, always the same: the object comes to the front, the verb becomes am / is / are + participle, and the old subject either disappears or goes to the back behind by. Only the middle step changes from camp to camp.

ActivePassiveWhat moved
They empty the bins on Fridays.The bins are emptied on Fridays.The bins came first.
People speak English here.English is spoken here.People carried nothing.
An outside firm audits the accounts.The accounts are audited by an outside firm.The firm matters, so it stayed.
Someone checks tickets at the barrier.Tickets are checked at the barrier.Singular to plural, so are.

Three mistakes worth naming

Almost every error with this one is one of these three.

Not thisThisWhy
The bins are empty on Fridays.The bins are emptied on Fridays.Without the participle you have written an adjective: the bins are simply not full.
English speaks here.English is spoken here.English does not do the speaking. If the subject is not the doer, you need the passive.
It is happen every year.It happens every year.Happen takes no object, so it has no passive at all.
The fork in the path

Active or passive?

Neither is better. The question is only what the sentence is about — because whatever you put at the front is what the reader will think it is about.

QuestionPassiveActive
What is the sentence about?The thing it happens to.
"The bins are emptied on Fridays."		The one who does it.
"They empty the bins on Fridays."
Do you know the doer?Often not — and you do not need to.Yes, and you are naming them.
Where does the doer go?At the end with by, or nowhere at all.At the front, as the subject.
Which is shorter?Longer by two or three words.Usually shorter. Prefer it when both work.

This is the tense of every sign, manual and set of instructions you will ever read in English. Doors are closed at 6pm. Payment is taken on collection. Nobody is named, because the rule applies whoever is on duty — and that anonymity is the whole point.

Trail markers

Signal words

Reach for the passive when

  • the doer is unknown
  • the doer is irrelevant
  • the doer is obvious
  • the process is the point
  • a report needs distance
  • the object is the topic

Verbs with no passive at all

  • happen · occur
  • arrive · go · come
  • die · live
  • exist · belong
  • seem · appear
  • become · fall

The test for whether a verb can go passive: does it take an object? They built it has one, so it turns. It happened has none, so it cannot.
