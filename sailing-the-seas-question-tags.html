The Return Voyage

A question tag is the little question on the end of a sentence: It's cold, isn't it? On this chart every tag is a round trip across the reef. Sail out on a positive and you come home on a negative; sail out on a negative and you come home on a positive. Four lone stacks keep their own rules.

Examples in
Reading the voyage

Out on one, home on the other

A tag turns a statement into a check: you think you know, and you want the other person to agree. Three things decide it: the direction, the helper and the pronoun.

Positive out, negative home

A positive statement takes a negative tag.

It's cold, isn't it?

Negative out, positive home

A negative statement takes a positive tag.

It isn't far, is it?

Sail home in the same boat

The tag uses the same helper as the statement: be, have, can, will, should…

You can swim, can't you?

No helper? do rows you home

In the present simple and past simple, the tag uses do, does or did.

She went home, didn't she?

A pronoun, never the noun

The tag repeats the subject as a pronoun: the ferry becomes it, the crew becomes they.

The ferry leaves at six, doesn't it?

The tune tells the reader

Voice falls at the end: you are sure, you want agreement. Voice rises: it is a real question.

You haven't seen my keys, have you?

The five routes

Read each row left to right: out, home, and how the other person usually answers.

OutHomeThe answer

The lone stacks

Four tags with their own rules

These stand apart from the routes. Learn them as they are.

The four stacks

Each one breaks the pattern in its own way.

QuestionWhy
I am late, aren't I?there is no amn't I; aren't I takes its place
Let's go, shall we?let's is a suggestion, so the tag is shall we
Close the hatch, will you?after an order, will you or would you makes it polite
Nobody came, did they?nobody, nothing, never are negative, so the tag is positive; nobody becomes they

        Signal words

        The statement is negative

        • not: isn't, can't, didn't
        • never, no
        • nobody, no one, nothing
        • hardly, rarely, seldom
        • so the tag is positive: is it?, do you?

        The statement is positive

        • no not anywhere
        • always, everyone, something
        • I am → aren't I?
        • an order → will you?
        • so the tag is negative: isn't it?, don't you?

        One test: say the tag as a full question. Isn't it cold? If it sounds right, the boat is right.
