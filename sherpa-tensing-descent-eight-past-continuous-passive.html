Descent eight · past continuous passive

It was being done

The background of a story, with the doer taken out of it. Something was in the middle of happening to a thing when something else cut across — and who was doing it is either unknown or beside the point.

Descent briefing

When to use it

The passive is not a different tense. It is the past continuous with the doer let go — and the reason you let them go is always one of these six.

You do not know who

Nobody can tell you the doer, so the sentence does without one.

"The office was being watched."

The doer does not matter

The event is the news; who did it is a footnote.

"The road was being resurfaced at the time."

The doer is obvious

Saying it would be a waste of everyone's time.

"He was being questioned when the lawyer arrived."

You would rather not say

The passive is how English removes a person from a sentence.

"Decisions were being made without us."

Putting the important thing first

Whatever you put at the front is what the sentence is about.

"Two wards were being rebuilt that winter."

Putting the doer back with by

When the doer is the point, it goes at the end.

"The house was being painted by a firm from the village."
Building the passive

How it's built

Affirmative

subject + was / were + being + past participle

"The road was being resurfaced."

Negative

the not goes after the first auxiliary

"The files weren't being copied."

Questions

the first auxiliary moves to the front

"Was the room being cleaned?"

With the doer

… + by + doer, at the end

"It was being handled by the night shift."

Turning an active sentence around

Three moves, always the same: the object comes to the front, the verb becomes was / were + being + participle, and the old subject either disappears or goes to the back behind by. Only the middle step changes from camp to camp.

ActivePassiveWhat moved
They were resurfacing the road.The road was being resurfaced.The road came first.
Someone was watching the office.The office was being watched.Someone said nothing.
A firm was painting the house.The house was being painted by a firm.The firm stayed, at the end.
They were rebuilding two wards.Two wards were being rebuilt.Plural subject, so were.

Three mistakes worth naming

Almost every error with this one is one of these three.

Not thisThisWhy
The road was resurfacing.The road was being resurfaced.Without being, the road is doing the work itself.
The road was been resurfaced.The road was being resurfaced.Been belongs to the perfect. The continuous takes being.
The office was being watched for years.The office had been watched for years.A duration measured back needs a perfect, not the continuous.
The fork in the path

Active or passive?

Neither is better. The question is only what the sentence is about — because whatever you put at the front is what the reader will think it is about.

QuestionPassiveActive
What is the sentence about?The thing it happens to.
"The road was being resurfaced."		The one who does it.
"They were resurfacing the road."
Do you know the doer?Often not — and you do not need to.Yes, and you are naming them.
Where does the doer go?At the end with by, or nowhere at all.At the front, as the subject.
Which is shorter?Longer by two or three words.Usually shorter. Prefer it when both work.

This is how a witness describes a scene they were not part of: the door was being forced, papers were being burned. It gives you the action in progress and admits you could not see who was doing it — which is often the honest sentence, not the evasive one.

Trail markers

Signal words

Reach for the passive when

  • the doer is unknown
  • the doer is irrelevant
  • the doer is obvious
  • the process is the point
  • a report needs distance
  • the object is the topic

Verbs with no passive at all

  • happen · occur
  • arrive · go · come
  • die · live
  • exist · belong
  • seem · appear
  • become · fall

The test for whether a verb can go passive: does it take an object? They built it has one, so it turns. It happened has none, so it cannot.
