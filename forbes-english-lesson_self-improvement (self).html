The Language of Self-Improvement

Habits, mindset and the case for small steps — the words for talking about getting better

Before anything else

Three and a half minutes, and then the argument

Try Something New for 30 Days

Matt Cutts · TED · 3 min 27 s

youtube.com/watch?v=UNP03fDSj1U

Watch it first. The next three cards are what he argues, so you can check whether you heard the same thing.

Thirty days is the unit

Long enough for a habit to form, short enough that you can see the end from the start.

Small beats dramatic

The changes that stick are the sustainable ones. The dramatic ones come back off.

And the part people miss: the months stopped “flying by forgotten”. Doing something deliberately each day made the time countable — and therefore memorable.

From the reading

Why small steps lead to big changes

Start smaller than feels serious

Fifteen minutes of reading. One short walk.

People stall because the change they picture is too big to begin. The size of the first step is the only thing that decides whether there is a second one.

The 1% rule

1% better each day → 38× better in a year.

The arithmetic is the argument. Small and daily compounds; large and occasional does not.

Build the habit, not the willpower

A habit is something you do without deciding to.

Willpower runs out. A habit does not need any, which is why it outlasts motivation.

The other half of it

Mindset, and knowing yourself

A growth mindset

Your abilities are not fixed. You can always learn.

The practical test: after a failure, do you name a thing to practise, or a fact about yourself?

Mistakes are information

Not a verdict — an instruction about what to do next.

People with a growth mindset see a mistake as a reason to change the method, not a reason to stop.

It is not only about goals

Knowing your strengths, weaknesses and values.

The reading ends here, and it is the harder half: once you know who you are, the choices get easier to make.
