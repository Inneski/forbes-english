The Model Answer Vault

Two band 9 essays taken apart move by move — then the same two questions answered badly

C1 · IELTS Writing Task 2 Opinion & Discussion essays Band 9 vs band 6
Before you start

How to read a model answer

Read for the moves, not the words. Every paragraph below is doing a job. Learn the job and you can do it on any topic; learn the sentence and you can only do it on this one.

Do not memorise phrases. An examiner has read "in this modern era" ten thousand times, and a memorised opening scores nothing at all — it is treated as words you did not write.

Each essay here comes twice: once at band 9, once at around band 6. The gap between them is where the marks are.

What is actually marked

Four criteria, equally weighted

Task response. Did you answer the question that was asked, take a clear position, and support it? This is where most answers are lost.

Coherence and cohesion. Does the essay move in a straight line, and do the joins between ideas feel necessary rather than decorative?

Lexical resource. Range and precision — not long words. The right ordinary word beats the wrong impressive one every time.

Grammatical range and accuracy. A mix of structures, controlled. Simple sentences are fine; an essay built only from simple sentences is not.

Essay one · Opinion

Read the task before you read the model

Some companies now let their employees decide for themselves how many days a week they work from home. To what extent do you agree that this decision should be left to the individual employee?

"To what extent" is an instruction to commit. Fully, partly, or not at all — but say which, in the introduction, and hold it to the end.

The topic is who decides, not whether home working is good. An essay listing the benefits of working from home has answered a different question.

Band 9 · paragraph 1

The introduction does two jobs

A growing number of organisations have handed the question of home working to their staff rather than imposing a company-wide rule. In my view, employees should have the final say over where they work, provided that the demands of the role and the needs of the team are genuinely met.

Sentence 1 — paraphrase. The task is restated in the writer's own words. Not one phrase is lifted from the question paper.

Sentence 2 — position, with its limit. "Provided that" plants the condition the whole essay will go on to defend.

Question 1

Why state your view this early?

The writer's position arrives in the second sentence of the essay. Why is that the right place for it?

Band 9 · paragraph 2

One claim, then evidence for it

The strongest argument for individual choice is that the conditions people need in order to concentrate vary enormously, and a manager cannot see them. A developer with young children may do her best work between six and nine in the morning at home; a colleague living alone in a small flat may find the same arrangement isolating and produce very little. A blanket policy therefore has to be written for an average employee who does not exist, and it ends up suiting almost nobody.

Topic sentence. One claim, stated first, and the rest of the paragraph never leaves it.

Two specific people — not "many people". The contrast between them is the argument, not decoration around it.

Band 9 · paragraph 3

Argue against yourself, then resolve it

This freedom cannot be unconditional, however, because certain kinds of work collapse without physical proximity. Training a new colleague, repairing a disagreement, or developing an idea nobody has yet put into words all depend on the incidental contact an office provides and a scheduled video call does not. The compromise many firms have reached seems the sensible one: the team fixes a small number of days on which everyone is present, and individuals choose freely around them.

Concession, then resolution. The limit promised by "provided that" in the introduction is now argued and settled — the essay does not sit on the fence, it moves through the objection and comes out the other side still holding its position.

Question 2

Conceding a point

Paragraph 3 admits that employee choice has limits. Why does that raise the score rather than weaken the argument?

Band 9 · paragraph 4

The conclusion adds nothing new

I therefore agree that this decision belongs primarily to the employee, but only within limits the team has set for itself. The person doing the work is the best judge of where it gets done; the team is the best judge of when everyone must be in the room. Autonomy produces better work than compulsion does; on its own, however, it does not produce a functioning team.

Same position, condition intact. "Primarily" and "only within limits" carry the whole essay in four words.

No new argument. The last line sharpens what was already proved. A fresh idea here costs marks rather than earning them.

Band 6 · same question

The same task at band 6, shortened

Nowadays, in this modern era, working from home is a very controversial issue which is discussed by many people around the world. In this essay, I will discuss both the advantages and the disadvantages of this topic and give my opinion.

Firstly, working from home has many advantages. People can save a lot of time because they do not need to travel to the office. Travelling is very tiring and it also causes pollution, which is bad for the environment.

Three problems already. "In this modern era" is memorised filler. "I will discuss both the advantages and the disadvantages" announces the wrong essay type — the task asked how far the writer agrees. And pollution is not what the question is about.

Band 6 · same question

It gets worse at the end

Secondly, there are also some disadvantages. Some people cannot concentrate at home because there are many distractions. Moreover, communication is more difficult and this can cause problems. Furthermore, the government should make new laws about this issue to protect workers.

In conclusion, working from home has both advantages and disadvantages. It depends on the person. In my opinion, companies should decide, because they know their own business best.

The position arrives in the last line, and contradicts the line before it. "It depends on the person" — then the decision is handed to the company. Note also the four linkers doing no work: Firstly, Secondly, Moreover, Furthermore.

Question 3

Diagnose it

That answer is organised into paragraphs and contains few grammar mistakes. What still holds it at band 6?

Essay two · Discussion

A discussion essay has three jobs

Some people believe university education should be funded entirely by the state. Others argue that students should pay the full cost of their own degrees. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Both views, fairly. Each side gets a paragraph and its best argument, not a straw man built to be knocked down.

And your own opinion — a third job, not an optional extra. Omit it and the answer cannot reach band 6 for task response.

Band 9 · paragraph 1

Say where you stand before you discuss

Whether the cost of a degree should fall on the taxpayer or on the graduate is among the harder questions in education policy. Both positions rest on a defensible principle, but I believe a system funded largely by the state, with a contribution from graduates who go on to earn well, serves a country best.

The question reframed as a choice between two payers — sharper than the original wording, and not copied from it.

The opinion is already here. The two body paragraphs are then read as the reasoning behind a view the examiner already knows.

Band 9 · paragraph 2

View one, at its strongest

Those who argue for full public funding point out that the benefits of a degree do not stop at the graduate. A country with enough doctors, engineers and teachers is a country that works, and none of those professions can be staffed without heavy public investment in training. Where fees are high, moreover, the students deterred are almost never the wealthy ones, which means the country loses talent it has already paid to school.

"Those who argue" marks it as reported, not owned. The paragraph then gives the view its two best arguments — public return and access — and does not undercut either one. Presenting a view fairly is what "discuss" means.

Band 9 · paragraph 3

View two, given the same respect

The opposing argument is essentially one of fairness to those who never go to university. A graduate can expect to earn substantially more over a lifetime than someone who left school at eighteen, and it is hard to justify asking the second person, through taxation, to subsidise the first. Charging fees also introduces a discipline, since students who are paying ask harder questions about what a course is worth, and weak courses lose their applicants.

Equal length, equal seriousness. The writer rejects full fees and still gives this view its two best arguments. A reader cannot yet tell which side the essay will land on — and that is correct at this point.

Question 5

Whose side is it on?

Both views get a full, fair paragraph. So how does the reader know which one the writer actually supports?

Band 9 · paragraph 4

An opinion built from both views

My own view takes something from each. The state should carry the bulk of the cost, because the return is collective; but a repayment linked to later earnings recognises the private gain as well, and does so without turning the price of a degree into a barrier at eighteen. That arrangement keeps the door open to every capable student while asking the most of those who gain the most.

Not a compromise for its own sake. It names which principle it takes from each side and why that combination survives both.

Nothing new enters. Every idea in this paragraph was argued above. The conclusion only decides between them.

Band 6 · same question

Where the weaker answer loses it

Education is very important for all countries in the world. In my essay I will discuss both sides of this argument. […] However, in my opinion, education is a right and not a privilege. The government must find the money from somewhere, for example from taxes on rich people. If they do this, everybody will be happy and the country will develop faster.

The opinion arrives late and from nowhere. Taxing the wealthy was not one of the two views under discussion, so the conclusion decides a question the essay never argued — and "everybody will be happy" is a claim no examiner can credit.

Question 6

It has an opinion. Why is it still weak?

The band 6 answer does state an opinion in its fourth paragraph. Why does it still lose marks for position?

🗣Discussion · in pairs

Argue aloud first. Concede one point before you attack it.

  • Your partner says cutting emissions is a job for governments, not individuals. Take the other side — and grant them their best point first.
  • Defend free hospital treatment for everyone to a partner who thinks heavy smokers should pay. Set your limit with "provided that".
  • Now argue the side you actually disagree with, well enough that your partner cannot tell.
