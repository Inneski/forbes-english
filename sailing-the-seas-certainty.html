Soundings of Certainty

You see a shape on the water. Is it the ferry? You do not know, but you can say how sure you are. Must is deep water: you are almost certain. Might is the fog: perhaps. Can’t is the rocks: you are sure it is not. Read the depth, then do the same for the past.

Examples in
Reading the depth

How sure are you?

None of these states a fact. Each says what you work out from what you can see. The deeper the water, the surer you are.

Must: almost certain it is true

You have a reason, so you are nearly sure.

It must be the ferry: it's six o'clock.

Should: what you expect

Things are normal, so this is what ought to happen.

The sea should be calm by noon.

May, might, could: perhaps

All three mean it is possible. In the fog you cannot tell.

It might be a whale. It could be a log.

Can't: almost certain it is not true

The opposite of this must is can't, not mustn't.

It can't be Tom: he's in Rome.

The past: modal + have + past participle

Keep the modal and add have + the third form. The depth stays the same.

She must have left: her coat is gone.

Might not is not can't

Might not is still the fog: perhaps not. Can't is the rocks: surely not.

He might not know. He can't know.

The fog bank and the rocks

Where learners run aground

The fog holds three words with one meaning. The rocks hold the form that most languages get wrong: the negative of a sure guess.

The fog bank: three words, one depth

For a guess, these are the same strength. Can is not among them.

SayNotNote
It may be true.It can be true.can is ability, not a guess
It might rain.It can rain later.for one guess about now or later
It could be Ana.It can be Ana.the same
It might not be true.It couldn't be true. (if you mean perhaps not)couldn't is the rocks

That might be Ana at the door, or it could be the postman.

The rocks: surely not

When you are sure something is not true, use can't.

Not thisThisWhy
It mustn't be Tom.It can't be Tom.mustn't means it is forbidden
She mustn't have seen us.She can't have seen us.the same, in the past
He must not be at home. (as a guess)He can't be at home.the usual form, in British English
It can't to be true.It can't be true.no to after a modal

They can't have seen us: it was dark.

        Signal words

        You are in deep water

        • you have a reason: it's six o'clock
        • I'm sure, certainly
        • must be
        • must have been
        • can't be for the opposite
        • you would bet on it

        You are in the fog

        • perhaps, maybe, possibly
        • I'm not sure
        • may, might, could
        • might have been
        • might not for perhaps not
        • you would not bet on it

        One test: can you say I'm sure? Then it is must or can't. If you would say perhaps, it is the fog.
