The Line Graph

Task 1's commonest chart after the bar. Direction, degree and tense — and how to describe a decade without describing every point on it.

C1 Advanced Writing Task 1 17 slides
What it asks for

A line graph is about change over time

A bar chart compares things. A line graph shows what happened to them. So the overview names the direction each line took, and the body describes the stages of that movement.

Everything follows from that. The verbs are movement verbs, the adverbs are speed, and the tense is decided by the dates printed on the axis — not by anything you choose.

The commonest failure is not vocabulary. It is describing every point — a report that walks through twelve readings in order has no overview and no grouping, and it will not reach band 6.

Lexical resource

Two ways to say the same movement

Verb + adverb.
Printer ownership fell steadily.
Smart speakers rose sharply.

There was a + adjective + noun.
There was a steady fall in printer ownership.
There was a sharp rise in smart speakers.

Same fact, two grammars. Using both across a report is what Lexical Resource rewards — using one of them six times is what caps it.

Question 1

Which one is the overview?

Printers began at 62% and ended at 30%. Smart speakers began at 0% and ended at 46%.

Question 2

Which tense, and why?

The axis is labelled 2010 to 2020. What decides the tense of your report?

Degree

How fast, in four words

Sharply / steeply. A lot, quickly. Speakers after 2016.

Steadily. At about the same rate throughout. Printers.

Gradually. Slowly, over a long stretch.

Slightly / marginally. Only a little. Speakers before 2014.

Steadily does not mean slowly. It means at a constant rate, and it is the word most often misused — a steep, even fall is a steady one.

Question 3

Where the lines cross

In 2018 printers led 36% to 28%. In 2020 speakers led 46% to 30%. The chart plots nothing in between.

Question 4

Verb and adverb

Printer ownership fell across the whole decade.

Smart speaker ownership rose after 2016.

Question 5

Now the noun form

Same two facts, the other grammar. This is the swap that widens Lexical Resource.

There was a steady in printer ownership.

The years after 2016 saw a sharp in smart speaker ownership.

🗣Discussion · in pairs

Describing a line you cannot see is the fastest way to find out whether your trend language is doing any work.

  • Describe one of the two lines aloud, without figures and without naming the device. Your partner draws it. Compare, then swap.
  • Argue for two minutes that the printer line is the more interesting one, then swap and argue for the speaker line.
  • One of you says a trend sentence with a verb. The other says it again as a noun phrase. Keep going until one of you stalls.
