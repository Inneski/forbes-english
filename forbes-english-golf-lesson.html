Past Simple
vs Present Perfect

Master two of English's most important tenses through the world of golf — fairways, birdies, and champions.

1
The Key Difference
Past Simple
A finished action at a specific time in the past — the time is stated or clearly known.
"Tiger Woods won the Masters in 2005."
"She played her best round yesterday."
The connection to the present is not important.
Present Perfect
A past action with a connection to now — either the time is unspecified or the result matters today.
"Rory McIlroy has won four major championships."
"She has lost her 7-iron — she can't find it!"
Focus on experience, change, or unfinished time periods.
Past Simple form: Subject + verb-ed (regular) / irregular form
"He putted beautifully."  |  "She drove 300 yards."
Present Perfect form: Subject + have/has + past participle
"He has putted beautifully."  |  "She has never driven that far."
2
Time Signal Words
Past Simple — look for these
yesterday last week/year in 1997 ago when I was… in the 2000s at the Open
Present Perfect — look for these
already yet just ever / never so far this season recently
⚠ Watch out! "This season" uses present perfect if the season is still ongoing — but past simple if the season is finished!
3
Side-by-Side Golf Comparisons
Past Simple
He scored a hole-in-one in 2019.
Present Perfect
He has scored a hole-in-one. (at some point in his life)
Past Simple
The caddie gave her the wrong club on the 18th hole.
Present Perfect
The caddie has given her the wrong club — she's furious!
Past Simple
The club hosted the Open Championship last summer.
Present Perfect
The club has hosted the Open three times.
