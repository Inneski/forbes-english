When nobody did it

Coming down, the same tenses are lit from the other side. The passive keeps the event and lets the doer go — because you do not know who, or it does not matter, or you would rather not say. Same past simple, same place on the line, different subject.

Descent briefing

When to use it

The passive is not a different tense. It is the same past simple with the doer let go — and the reason you let them go is always one of these six.

You do not know who

Nobody can tell you the doer, so the sentence does without one.

"The window was broken during the night."

The doer does not matter

The event is the news; who did it is a footnote.

"The bridge was built in 1890."

The doer is obvious

Saying it would be a waste of everyone's time.

"He was arrested on Tuesday."

You would rather not say

The most quietly political sentence in English does exactly this.

"A mistake was made."

Putting the important thing first

English gives the front of the sentence to what the sentence is about.

"Three villages were evacuated."

Putting the doer back with by

When the doer is the point, it goes at the end, where the weight falls.

"The report was written by an intern."
Building the passive

How it's built

Affirmative

subject + was / were + past participle

"The letter was sent on Friday."

Negative

subject + wasn't / weren't + past participle

"The forms weren't signed."

Yes / No questions

Was / Were + subject + past participle?

"Was it repaired?"

With the doer

subject + was / were + participle + by + doer

"It was repaired by the landlord."

Turning an active sentence around

Three moves, always in the same order: the object comes to the front, the verb becomes was/were + participle, and the old subject either disappears or goes to the back behind by.

ActivePassiveWhat moved
The storm damaged the roof.The roof was damaged.The roof came first; the storm went.
Someone stole my bike.My bike was stolen.Someone was carrying no information.
Brunel built the bridge.The bridge was built by Brunel.Brunel matters, so he stayed — at the end.
They cancelled the trains.The trains were cancelled.Plural subject, so were.

Three mistakes worth naming

Almost every error with the passive is one of these three.

Not thisThisWhy
The window was break.The window was broken.After be comes the third form, never the infinitive or the past simple.
It was happened last night.It happened last night.Verbs with no object have no passive — happen, arrive, die, exist, seem.
The bridge was built from Brunel.The bridge was built by Brunel.The doer takes by. With is for the instrument: cut with a knife.
The fork in the path

Active or passive?

Neither is better. The question is only what the sentence is about — because whatever you put at the front is what the reader will think it is about.

QuestionPassiveActive
What is the sentence about?The thing it happened to.
"The roof was damaged."		The one who did it.
"The storm damaged the roof."
Do you know the doer?Often not — and you do not need to.Yes, and you are naming them.
Where does the doer go?At the end with by, or nowhere at all.At the front, as the subject.
Which is shorter?Longer by two or three words.Usually shorter. Prefer it when both work.

The pair worth remembering: "I made a mistake" and "A mistake was made" say the same thing about the world and completely different things about the speaker. That choice is the passive's real power, and the reason to know when someone is using it on you.

Trail markers

Signal words

Reach for the passive when

  • the doer is unknown
  • the doer is irrelevant
  • the doer is obvious
  • the process is the point
  • a report or notice needs distance
  • the object is the topic

Verbs with no passive at all

  • happen · occur
  • arrive · go · come
  • die · live
  • exist · belong
  • seem · appear
  • become · fall

The test for whether a verb can go passive: does it take an object? They built it has one, so it turns. It happened has none, so it cannot.
