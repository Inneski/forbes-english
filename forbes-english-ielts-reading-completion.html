Summary and Sentence Completion

Where the word limit does the damage — answers come straight from the passage, spelling counts, and one word too many scores nothing

C1 · Advanced Reading · both modules 18 points
Before you start

Three gaps, one instruction line, and the line comes first

What you are given

A summary of part of the passage, or a set of separate sentences, with gaps in them. The words that fill the gaps are in the passage. The summary usually runs in passage order, but it covers only a section — find where that section starts and stay in it.

Academic and General Training set it the same way. Everything here applies to both.

Read the instruction first

NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER. ONE WORD ONLY. The limit is printed above the gaps and it is the first thing to read — before the summary, before the passage. It decides what a correct answer looks like before you have found one.

A hyphenated word is one word. A number is a number, not a word. The is a word, and it counts.

Sometimes there is a box

When the task gives you a list of words, A–H, you choose from the list, not from the passage, and you write the letter. The words might not be in the text at all, and the list has more options than gaps.

Same skill underneath: find the place in the passage, then check the grammar of the gap.

Before you start

The word is in the passage. Lift it.

Find the place by meaning

The summary does not repeat the passage; it paraphrases everything around the gap. Match the meaning of the sentence to find the right lines, then read those lines for the one word the summary did not change.

A shared word tells you where to look. The gap word is usually the one word that was not paraphrased.

Then copy it, exactly

Same spelling, same form as printed. Do not make it plural, do not change the tense, do not add an article the summary already has. A correct idea in your own words scores nothing, because the marker compares your answer with the key, not with the passage.

If your word is not in the passage, it is not the answer.

The limit is a wall

Under NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS, a three-word answer scores nothing however right it is. Not half a mark: nothing. When an answer runs over, the extra word is almost always an article or an adjective you did not need.

Write the answer, count it, then read the instruction line again.

Before you start

Predict the word before you look for it

Name the word class first

Read the summary sentence with the gap in it and decide what it needs: a noun, a verb, an adjective, a number. A gap after the or a wants a noun; a gap after the subject wants a verb; a gap after lasted or cost wants an amount.

Decide this before you open the passage. Then you are looking for one kind of word, not any word.

The answer must read as English

Put your word in the gap and read the whole sentence. Singular or plural, past or present: the summary sentence decides, and the passage word usually already fits, because the summary was written from it.

If the sentence does not read, you have the wrong form or the wrong place.

Spelling counts

Even in a copied word. Transfer it letter by letter and check it against the passage. A word you knew, copied wrongly, scores exactly what a word you did not know would have.

Reading has no transfer time. Write on the answer sheet as you go.

    Use at least three: read the limit first copy, not paraphrase predict the class a number = one spelling counts hyphen = one word
    🗣Discussion · in pairs

    In pairs, with a short article each. Write a three-gap summary of yours — four sentences, a word limit above them — and swap. Your partner fills the gaps from the article only; you mark as the examiner would: passage word, within the limit, spelt as printed, or nothing.

    • Before filling any gap, say out loud what word class it needs and which words in the sentence tell you so.
    • For every answer, point at the words in the article. An answer you cannot point at is not accepted.
    • Find one gap in your partner’s summary that two passage words could fill, and rewrite the sentence so only one can.
    ✍️Writing · 150–200 words

    Take a passage you read this week and write a four-sentence summary of it with three gaps, the word limit stated above it. Then write the key: the exact passage words for each gap, and one tempting wrong answer with why it scores nothing.
