Descent seven · future simple passive

It will be done

The tense of promises and notices about the future — and in the passive, of promises with nobody attached to them. You will be contacted. By whom, exactly? The sentence has been built so that you cannot ask.

Descent briefing

When to use it

The passive is not a different tense. It is the future simple with the doer let go — and the reason you let them go is always one of these six.

You do not know who

Nobody can tell you the doer, so the sentence does without one.

"The road will be closed overnight."

The doer does not matter

The event is the news; who did it is a footnote.

"The results will be published on Friday."

The doer is obvious

Saying it would be a waste of everyone's time.

"You will be contacted within five days."

You would rather not say

The passive is how English removes a person from a sentence.

"Staffing levels will be reviewed."

Putting the important thing first

Whatever you put at the front is what the sentence is about.

"Two hundred jobs will be lost."

Putting the doer back with by

When the doer is the point, it goes at the end.

"The work will be carried out by a specialist firm."
Building the passive

How it's built

Affirmative

subject + will be + past participle

"The road will be closed overnight."

Negative

the not goes after the first auxiliary

"The results won't be published until Friday."

Questions

the first auxiliary moves to the front

"Will the room be cleaned?"

With the doer

… + by + doer, at the end

"It will be handled by the duty team."

Turning an active sentence around

Three moves, always the same: the object comes to the front, the verb becomes will be + participle, and the old subject either disappears or goes to the back behind by. Only the middle step changes from camp to camp.

ActivePassiveWhat moved
They will close the road overnight.The road will be closed overnight.The road came first.
Someone will contact you.You will be contacted.Someone was nobody.
A specialist firm will carry out the work.The work will be carried out by a specialist firm.The firm stayed, at the end.
They will lose two hundred jobs.Two hundred jobs will be lost.Nobody is losing them, apparently.

Three mistakes worth naming

Almost every error with this one is one of these three.

Not thisThisWhy
The road will be close.The road will be closed.After be comes the participle. Close here is an adjective meaning near.
The road will closed.The road will be closed.The be is not optional; it is what makes the sentence passive.
You will be contacted by we.You will be contacted by us.After by comes an object pronoun: us, him, them.
The fork in the path

Active or passive?

Neither is better. The question is only what the sentence is about — because whatever you put at the front is what the reader will think it is about.

QuestionPassiveActive
What is the sentence about?The thing it happens to.
"The road will be closed."		The one who does it.
"They will close the road."
Do you know the doer?Often not — and you do not need to.Yes, and you are naming them.
Where does the doer go?At the end with by, or nowhere at all.At the front, as the subject.
Which is shorter?Longer by two or three words.Usually shorter. Prefer it when both work.

Almost every official notice about the future is written this way, and it is worth noticing what that buys the writer. Staffing levels will be reviewed commits nobody to reviewing them. We will review staffing levels does. Same future, one accountable sentence and one that is not.

Trail markers

Signal words

Reach for the passive when

  • the doer is unknown
  • the doer is irrelevant
  • the doer is obvious
  • the process is the point
  • a report needs distance
  • the object is the topic

Verbs with no passive at all

  • happen · occur
  • arrive · go · come
  • die · live
  • exist · belong
  • seem · appear
  • become · fall

The test for whether a verb can go passive: does it take an object? They built it has one, so it turns. It happened has none, so it cannot.
