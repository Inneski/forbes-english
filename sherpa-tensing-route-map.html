About the route

Where you are

Nine of the camps open a passive; used to comes off camp 2 or 3, be used to off camp 2, the causative off your first descent.

Four descent stops stay dark on purpose

The passive of the present perfect continuous, the past perfect continuous, the future continuous and the future perfect continuous can all be derived — HAS BEEN BEING CLEANED, WILL BE BEING CLEANED — and native speakers avoid every one of them, rewriting round them instead. There is nothing there to learn, so there is no camp there.

Before you climb

What you’re looking at

A tense is a time and a shape

Every tense puts an action in the past, the present or the future, and gives it one of four shapes: simple (the plain fact), continuous (in progress), perfect (looking back from a later point: now, or a time in the past or future) or perfect continuous (in progress up to that point). Three times, four shapes: twelve of the thirteen camps. Every form below is “she” + CLIMB, and the number is its camp.

Two faces: active and passive

Same tense, turned round: the passive is BE in that tense + the PAST PARTICIPLE. So every stop on the way down is the twin of a camp on the way up, and it opens when you finish that camp.

Why this order

The camps climb in the order you will need them, not the order grammar books use. The foundation camps are the tenses you use every day; the ridge is common but needs more care; the summit push is rare and mostly written. Four passives have no camp: you can build them — HAS BEEN BEING CLEANED — but nobody says them.

On the map, a circle is active, a diamond is passive, and grey means not open yet; a faint diamond marked “no camp” never opens. The clouds are “used to” and “be used to”; the moon is the causative.
