Camp eleven · past perfect continuous

The hours before the hut

Camp eight measures up to now. Camp eleven moves the whole measurement back: an activity that had been running for a while before something else in the past happened — and how things looked by the time it did.

Basecamp briefing

When to use it

How long, up to a past moment

It had been running for a while before something else in the past happened.

"We had been walking for six hours when we saw the hut."

The cause of a past state

Why somebody or something was the way you found them.

"His hands were shaking. He had been carrying the pack all day."

Repeated, up to that point

The same thing over and over, right up to the moment it stopped.

"She had been calling all morning before he answered."

Reported speech

A present perfect continuous shifts back one step when you report it.

"I've been waiting." → He said he had been waiting.

The third conditional

An unreal past, with the activity rather than the result in focus.

"If I had been paying attention, I would have seen it."

The activity, not the total

How the time was filled, rather than what came out of it.

"I had been reading it." (not necessarily finished)
Building the tense

How it's built

Affirmative

subject + had been + -ing

"They had been waiting for hours."

Negative

subject + hadn't been + -ing

"It hadn't been raining long."

Yes / No questions

Had + subject + been + -ing?

"Had you been running?"

Wh- questions

How long + had + subject + been + -ing?

"How long had they been driving?"

Conjugation chart

Four pieces, and not one of them changes for the subject. Had is the same for everybody, been never moves, and the -ing form is fixed. This is the easiest tense on the mountain to conjugate and one of the hardest to know when to use.

The verbs that refuse the -ing

A state is not an activity, so it cannot be in progress. When the meaning is a state, camp ten takes over: had known, never had been knowing.

GroupVerbsSay this instead
Thinkingknow, believe, understand"I had known him for years."
Feelinglike, love, hate, want"She had wanted it since March."
Havingown, belong, have (= possess)"They had had the flat a year."
Beingbe, seem"It had been quiet all night."

Three mistakes worth naming

Almost every error with this tense is one of these three.

Not thisThisWhy
I was walking for six hours when we arrived.I had been walking for six hours when we arrived.A duration measured back from a past moment needs the perfect, not camp six.
I had been knowing him for years.I had known him for years.State verbs refuse the continuous, so camp ten takes it.
I had been reading three books.I had read three books.A number is a result, and results belong to camp ten.
The fork in the path

Continuous or simple?

Camp ten and camp eleven both sit behind a past moment. The question is only ever which half you are pointing at: the activity that filled the time, or the result that was sitting there when you arrived.

QuestionPast perfect continuousPast perfect simple
What is in focus?The activity and how long it ran.
"He had been painting."		The finished result.
"He had painted the fence."
With a number?Never. "He had been writing three emails" is wrong.Always. "He had written three emails."
With a state verb?Never — know, be, own refuse it.Always. "He had known her for years."
And camp six?Measures a duration up to the past moment.Camp six just says what was going on at it: "I was walking when…"

The pair that shows the whole difference: "I was walking when the storm hit" — that is what was going on. "I had been walking for six hours when the storm hit" — that is how long it had been going on for. Camp six gives you the scene; camp eleven gives you the bill.

Signal words

  • all morning / all day / all night

The evidence, back then

  • he was out of breath
  • the ground was still wet
  • her eyes were red
  • there was paint on his hands
  • the kitchen smelled of it
  • that was why they were late

A useful habit: find the past simple first — that is the moment you are standing on. Then ask how long the other thing had already been going when you got there.
