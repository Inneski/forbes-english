Section 2 · read, then fill the gaps
Diego’s trip

Read it once, then fill it in

Diego’s story runs from a summer habit to a flight next Friday, so it moves through six tenses without announcing any of them.

Each gap names a verb in brackets. Put it in the form the sentence needs — the time words around it will tell you which.

Diego’s trip

Diego’s trip

Diego ______ (travel) to Italy every summer, but this year he’s trying something different. Last month, he ______ (book) a flight to Lisbon after a friend ______ (recommend) it. He ______ (never / visit) Portugal before, so he’s very excited.

Last week, while he ______ (pack) his suitcase, he realised he’d left his passport at his parents’ house. Next Friday, Diego ______ (fly) from Madrid to Lisbon — he’s already chosen his seat. If the flight ______ (be) delayed, he ______ (miss) his hotel check-in time.

Diego’s trip · 1 / 3

Every summer

Write the verb in brackets in the correct form.

Diego (travel) to Italy every summer, but this year he’s trying something different.

Last month, he (book) a flight to Lisbon after a friend (recommend) it.

Diego’s trip · 2 / 3

Before the trip

Write the verb in brackets in the correct form. “Suitcase” = the bag you pack for a trip.

He (never / visit) Portugal before, so he’s very excited.

Last week, while he (pack) his suitcase, he realised he’d left his passport at his parents’ house.

Diego’s trip · 3 / 3

Next Friday

Write the verb in brackets in the correct form. “Delayed” = later than planned.

Next Friday, Diego (fly) from Madrid to Lisbon — he’s already chosen his seat.

If the flight (be) delayed, he (miss) his hotel check-in time.

True or False

Section 3 · some of these are myths
True or false

Six rules, and some of them are wrong

Every statement here is something learners are commonly told. Four are accurate. Two are the kind of half-rule that survives because nobody checks it.

Decide on the rule itself, not on whether the example sounds familiar.

True or false · 1 / 6

Plans and the Present Continuous

True or false?

The Present Continuous can describe a future arrangement, like “I’m flying to Rome on Monday.”

True or false · 2 / 6

Mustn’t and don’t have to

True or false?

“Mustn’t” and “don’t have to” mean the same thing.

True or false · 3 / 6

Were or was

True or false?

In the second conditional we often use “were” instead of “was” after I, he, she and it.

True or false · 4 / 6

Two-syllable adjectives

True or false?

Two-syllable adjectives always take “more”, never “-er”.

True or false · 5 / 6

Active into passive

True or false?

The passive of “People speak English here” is “English is spoken here.”

True or false · 6 / 6

Whose

True or false?

“Whose” is used to talk about possession.

    🗣Speaking

    In pairs. Take one prompt each and talk for two minutes without stopping, using the grammar in brackets — your partner listens for the forms above and tells you afterwards which ones you actually used.

    • Your plans for Saturday, and what you’ll do if it rains. (Present Continuous, first conditional)
    • At work or school: three things you mustn’t do, and three you don’t have to do. (modal verbs)
    • A trip that went wrong: what were you doing when it happened, and what happened next? (Past Continuous, Past Simple)
