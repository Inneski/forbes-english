Descent one · present continuous passive

Being done right now

The longest thing you will say in ordinary English without anyone noticing: is being done. Three words to say that something is happening to a thing at this moment, and that whoever is doing it is not what the sentence is about.

Descent briefing

When to use it

The passive is not a different tense. It is the {TENSE} with the doer let go — and the reason you let them go is always one of these six.

You do not know who

Nobody can tell you the doer, so the sentence does without one.

"The road is being resurfaced."

The doer does not matter

The event is the news; who did it is a footnote.

"My car is being repaired at the moment."

The doer is obvious

Saying it would be a waste of everyone's time.

"The suspect is being questioned."

You would rather not say

The passive is how English removes a person from a sentence.

"Some difficult decisions are being made."

Putting the important thing first

Whatever you put at the front is what the sentence is about.

"Three new wards are being built."

Putting the doer back with by

When the doer is the point, it goes at the end.

"The kitchen is being redone by a firm from Leeds."
Building the passive

How it's built

Affirmative

subject + am / is / are + being + past participle

"The road is being resurfaced."

Negative

the not goes after the first auxiliary

"The files aren't being copied."

Questions

the first auxiliary moves to the front

"Is the room being cleaned?"

With the doer

… + by + doer, at the end

"It is being handled by the duty team."

Turning an active sentence around

Three moves, always the same: the object comes to the front, the verb becomes am / is / are + being + participle, and the old subject either disappears or goes to the back behind by. Only the middle step changes from camp to camp.

ActivePassiveWhat moved
They are resurfacing the road.The road is being resurfaced.The road came first.
Someone is questioning the suspect.The suspect is being questioned.Someone said nothing.
A firm from Leeds is redoing the kitchen.The kitchen is being redone by a firm from Leeds.The firm stayed, at the end.
They are building three wards.Three wards are being built.Plural subject, so are.

Conjugation chart

Being never changes and never leaves. Only am, is or are moves, and the participle sits behind both of them.

Three mistakes worth naming

Almost every error with this one is one of these three.

Not thisThisWhy
The road is resurfacing.The road is being resurfaced.The road is not doing the resurfacing. Without being the sentence says it is.
The road is been resurfaced.The road is being resurfaced.Been belongs to the perfect. The continuous takes being.
I am being knowing him for years.I have known him for years.State verbs refuse the continuous, active or passive.
The fork in the path

Active or passive?

Neither is better. The question is only what the sentence is about — because whatever you put at the front is what the reader will think it is about.

QuestionPassiveActive
What is the sentence about?The thing it happens to.
"The road is being resurfaced."		The one who does it.
"They are resurfacing the road."
Do you know the doer?Often not — and you do not need to.Yes, and you are naming them.
Where does the doer go?At the end with by, or nowhere at all.At the front, as the subject.
Which is shorter?Longer by two or three words.Usually shorter. Prefer it when both work.

Listen for it in the news and in bad news generally: the matter is being looked into, steps are being taken, your call is being held in a queue. Three words that promise activity while naming nobody at all — which is exactly why it is worth being able to hear.

Trail markers

Signal words

Reach for the passive when

  • the doer is unknown
  • the doer is irrelevant
  • the doer is obvious
  • the process is the point
  • a report needs distance
  • the object is the topic

Verbs with no passive at all

  • happen · occur
  • arrive · go · come
  • die · live
  • exist · belong
  • seem · appear
  • become · fall

The test for whether a verb can go passive: does it take an object? They built it has one, so it turns. It happened has none, so it cannot.
