## **1. Warm-Up / Lead-In**

Create 3–5 short discussion questions connected to the lesson topic.

These should activate existing knowledge and encourage personal opinions.

Example types:

* “What is the most common problem with…?”
* “When was the last time you…?”
* “Which of these situations would be most difficult?”
* “Do you agree or disagree with this statement?”

This section should not be scored.

---

## **2. Vocabulary or Language Discovery**

Introduce the key target language.

Depending on the topic, this may be:

* Useful vocabulary
* Functional phrases
* Grammar structures
* Collocations
* Phrasal verbs
* Sentence frames
* Discourse markers

Include a discovery-style task such as:

* Match words to meanings
* Match phrases to situations
* Choose the best definition
* Categorise expressions
* Notice the difference between similar phrases

Include at least **5 items**.

Feedback should explain meaning and usage clearly.

---

## **3. Controlled Practice 1 — Fill in the Blank**

Create at least **5 fill-in-the-blank questions**.

Learners complete sentences using the target language.

Rules:

* Sentences must have realistic context.
* The target language should be practised naturally.
* Avoid isolated grammar drills with no meaning.
* Provide feedback after each answer.
* Explain why the answer works.

This section should be scored.

---

## **4. Controlled Practice 2 — Error Correction**

Create at least **5 error-correction questions**.

Learners must identify and correct realistic learner mistakes.

Rules:

* Mistakes should reflect common errors for the target language.
* Feedback must explain the problem.
* Corrected sentences should sound natural.
* Avoid trick questions.

This section should be scored.

---

## **5. Controlled Practice 3 — Sentence Reordering**

Create at least **5 sentence-reordering questions**.

Learners put words or sentence chunks into the correct order.

Rules:

* Use natural English sentences.
* Practise the target language clearly.
* Avoid weird textbook sentences.
* Feedback should explain the sentence structure or word order.

This section should be scored.

---

## **6. Guided Discussion**

Create a discussion section with **4–6 questions**.

The questions should require learners to use the target language from the lesson.

Include a useful language box with sentence starters.

Example:

**Useful language**

* One issue I often notice is…
* I’d probably deal with this by…
* The main advantage is…
* A possible drawback is…
* In the long run, I think…

Instruction:

“Use at least three expressions from today’s lesson in your answers.”

This section should not be scored.

---

## **7. Input Task**

Add one input-based task.

Choose one of the following formats:

### Option A — Short Reading Text

Create a short reading text of 150–220 words connected to the topic.

Then add 5 comprehension questions.

### Option B — Listening/Video Simulation

Create a short transcript-style text or interview extract connected to the topic.

Then add 5 comprehension questions.

The input task should expose learners to the target language in a realistic context.

Comprehension questions can be multiple choice, short answer, or true/false with explanation.

---

## **8. Language in Context**

After the input task, include a short noticing activity.

Learners look at 4–6 extracts from the text/transcript and explain or choose the meaning of key words, phrases, or structures.

This should help learners understand how the target language works in context.

---

## **9. Deeper Discussion**

Create 4–5 higher-level discussion questions based on the reading/listening text.

Questions should encourage learners to:

* Justify opinions
* Compare situations
* Evaluate solutions
* Discuss advantages and disadvantages
* Connect the topic to their own life, work, country, or experience

This section should not be scored.

---

## **10. Final Productive Task**

End with a meaningful speaking or writing task.

Include **one or both** of the following:

### Controlled Speaking Task

Learners prepare and present a short response, opinion, proposal, role-play, or mini-pitch.

Include:

* Clear scenario
* 3–5 guiding questions
* Useful phrases
* Success criteria

### Writing Task

Learners write a short text using the target language.

Include:

* A clear task
* A text area
* A checklist
* Suggested word count
* Reminder to use target language

Example checklist:

* I used at least three expressions from the lesson.
* I gave reasons or examples.
* I checked grammar and word order.
* I used complete sentences.
* My answer sounds natural and clear.

The final task should not be scored like a quiz. It should feel like the learner is actually using English.

# **Answer Design Rules**

For quiz-style activities:

* Correct answers must be distributed unpredictably.
* Do not make the correct answer always first.
* If using A, B, C, and D, correct answers should appear across all positions.
* All answer options should be similar in length.
* Do not make the correct answer obviously longer, shorter, more formal, or more detailed.
* Distractors must be plausible and challenging.
* Do not give visual hints before submission.
* No emojis, asterisks, bold text, colour differences, or formatting clues before the learner answers.

After submission:

* Show whether the answer is correct or incorrect.
* Show the correct answer if needed.
* Give a short explanation of why the answer is correct.
* Keep feedback clear and encouraging.
