Must & Have To

Positive and negative — the four key forms, in the colours of Stuttgart

B1 · Intermediate MUST · HAVE TO · the negative trap 19 slides
Tactics board · 1 / 3

Two ways to say it’s necessary

MUST — the speaker’s own feeling

Use MUST when the obligation comes from inside: your own belief, feeling or decision. VfB must win this derby — it means everything!

MUST can also mean “I’m sure”: He must be exhausted after 120 minutes. That is a conclusion, not an obligation.

HAVE TO — a rule from outside

Use HAVE TO when the obligation comes from outside: the rules, the referee, the coach. VfB players have to wear red and white at home games.

In positive sentences both often work: We must press / We have to press. In the negative, they split.

The critical rule · 2 / 3

MUSTN’T is not DON’T HAVE TO

MUSTN’T — forbidden

It is not allowed — by the rules, or by the speaker. Players mustn’t leave the pitch without the referee’s permission.

Do it anyway: yellow card. Written rules use the full form: MUST NOT.

DON’T HAVE TO — not necessary

There is no obligation — you can if you want to. Players don’t have to leave the pitch at half-time.

They can stay out and warm up if they like. One gets you a card; the other is just about where you stand.

The forms · 3 / 3

MUST never changes. HAVE TO does.

MUST — one form, always

SUBJECT + MUST + VERB. No -S, no TO, no DO, no past form. The keeper must stay focused. Not “musts”, “must stays”, “must to” or “does … must”.

For the past, use HAD TO — the past of both: Yesterday VfB had to win. Not “Yesterday VfB must win”.

HAVE TO — a normal verb

HAS TO with he, she and it. Questions and negatives use DO / DOES / DID + HAVE TO: Does the keeper have to wear gloves? The fans didn’t have to queue for tickets.

After DOES and DIDN’T, keep HAVE: not “does … has to”, not “didn’t had to”.

    🗣Speaking · in pairs

    One of you is the captain of a football club; the other has just signed and has questions. Four minutes, then swap.

    • Captain: tell the new player three things everyone at the club has to do, and two things they mustn’t do.
    • New player: ask two “Do I have to…?” questions. Captain: answer them, and tell them one thing they don’t have to do.
    • Captain: tell them one thing you had to do in your first week at the club, and one thing you were glad you didn’t have to do.
    ✍️Writing · 150–200 words

    A player from abroad joins your club next week. Write them a welcome email about the rules: what they have to do, what they mustn’t do, and what they don’t have to worry about. Use HAD TO at least once, for something new players had to do last season.
