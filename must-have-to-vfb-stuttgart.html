THE FOUR KEY FORMS

Both MUST and HAVE TO express obligation — something that is necessary or required. Think of it like a VfB Stuttgart team talk: the coach personally feels his squad must press harder (internal), but the DFB rules mean they have to register players by Friday (external).

But the negative forms mean completely different things — this is where most learners commit the worst foul of the season. Study the four forms below carefully!

A
Form A · Positive
MUST ✓
Internal obligation / logical deduction
Subject + MUST + base verb
  • "VfB must win this derby — it means everything!"
  • "Undav must be the most clinical striker in the league."
  • "The team must stay focused for the full 90 minutes."
🇪🇸 = deber / tener que (convicción personal o interna)
B
Form B · Negative
MUSTN'T 🚫
Prohibition — it is FORBIDDEN
Subject + MUST NOT + base verb
  • "Players mustn't argue with the referee — yellow card!"
  • "You must not handle the ball in your own box."
  • "The keeper mustn't pick up a back-pass."
🇪🇸 = no poder / está prohibido (¡Prohibición total!)
C
Form C · Positive
HAVE TO ✓
External obligation — rules, DFB, the coach
Subject + HAVE TO + base verb
  • "VfB players have to wear red and white at home games."
  • "The goalkeeper has to wear a different colour kit."
  • "Stuttgart had to play without their captain last week."
🇪🇸 = tener que (obligación externa — reglas, entrenador)
D
Form D · Negative
DON'T HAVE TO ✅
NO obligation — not necessary, free choice
Subject + DON'T/DOESN'T HAVE TO + base verb
  • "The captain doesn't have to take every set piece."
  • "Subs don't have to warm up — but it helps!"
  • "VfB didn't have to play extra time — they won 3–0!"
🇪🇸 = no tener que / no es necesario (sin obligación)
⚠ Schlüsselregel — Critical Rule

MUSTN'T ≠ DON'T HAVE TO — COMPLETELY DIFFERENT MEANINGS!

MUSTN'T = FORBIDDEN 🚫

"VfB players mustn't leave the pitch without the referee's permission."

→ It is prohibited. Automatic yellow card!
🇪🇸 = no puedes / está prohibido

≠
DON'T HAVE TO = NOT NECESSARY ✅

"The reserve keeper doesn't have to warm up during the game."

→ Not required, but he can if he wants.
🇪🇸 = no tienes que (no es necesario)

🔑 The Golden Rule / La Regla de Oro MUSTN'T = forbidden, illegal, you'll be punished → 🇪🇸 no se puede / está prohibido
DON'T HAVE TO = not necessary, optional, your choice → 🇪🇸 no es necesario / no tienes que

Also: MUST has NO past form! Use HAD TO for past.
❌ "Yesterday VfB must win."  →  ✅ "Yesterday VfB had to win."

Question 01

Hint: It is forbidden by the rules. 🚫 (prohibición)

Question 02

VfB head coach Sebastian Hoeneß told the squad: "We press from the first minute — Dortmund won't give us a second."

Hint: External necessity — the match situation demands it. (tener que)

Question 03

The club told the unused substitutes: "You warm up on the pitch — use the tunnel if you prefer."

Hint: Not required — their choice. (no es necesario)

Question 04

Deniz Undav said after the loss: "I score in the next game. I feel it — the fans deserve it." ⚽

Hint: Deep personal conviction — comes from inside. (convicción interna)

Question 05

The top scorer play in the cup game — Hoeneß will rest him for Saturday's Bundesliga clash.

Hint: 3rd person (he), not required. (no es necesario, 3ª persona)

Question 06

Last season, VfB Stuttgart play three matches in six days because of postponed fixtures.

Hint: Past tense! MUST has no past form — use HAD TO. 🇪🇸 (tuvieron que)

// Taktiktafel — Reference
THE MATCHDAY CHEAT SHEET
⚽ MUST (+) = strong personal obligation / logical deduction → internal → "We must win this!"
🇪🇸 deber / convicción personal
🚫 MUSTN'T (–) = FORBIDDEN → "Players mustn't handle the ball."
🇪🇸 no poder / está prohibido
📋 HAVE TO (+) = external obligation → rules, DFB, coach → "VfB have to wear red at home."
🇪🇸 tener que (obligación externa)
✅ DON'T HAVE TO (–) = NOT necessary → optional → "Subs don't have to warm up."
🇪🇸 no tener que / no es necesario
⏮ HAD TO = past of both → "VfB had to play without their striker."  (MUST has NO past form!)
🏟 The Stuttgart Test "Players mustn't leave the pitch without permission." → Referee blows the whistle. FORBIDDEN. 🟥
"Players don't have to leave the pitch at half-time." → They can stay out if they want. Optional. ✅

One gets you a card. The other is just about where you stand. Damals wie heute — know the difference!
