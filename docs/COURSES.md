# Courses in the catalogue — what exists, and what each one is missing

Built from the `lessons` table (237 rows), the files on disk, and the link
graph between them. Method, so it can be re-run rather than trusted:

- A **course** = lessons a learner can move through in order without going back
  to the library. Measured by inbound links between lesson files.
- Families were bucketed by filename and title, then checked by hand.

## The finding that shapes everything else

**Only one course actually exists.** Of 239 lesson files, **213 have no inbound
link from any other lesson**. They are reachable only from the library grid.
The single exception is Sherpa Tensing, which has a route map hub and prev/next
links between its camps.

Everything else below is a *latent* course: a coherent set of lessons that a
learner has no way to discover as a set, and no way to walk through in order.
Turning a latent course into a real one is mostly navigation, not new lessons.

Second structural fact: **78 of 237 lessons (33%) have no CEFR level set**, so
they cannot be sequenced or filtered even in principle. Per family: LEGO 5 of
9 unset, Minecraft/Dino 5 of 11, Prepositions 4 of 8, Common Mistakes 3 of 6.
Sherpa is the only family where every lesson has a level.

---

## 1. Sherpa Tensing — the complete tense system

**A2 → C1 · 26 lessons · all 26 free · 0 decks · every level set**

The flagship, and the only real course. Thirteen "camps" (one active tense
each), nine "descents" (the same tenses in the passive), three "clouds"
(*used to*, *be used to*, the causative), and a route map that indexes and
links all of them. Verified: every page the map links to exists, and every
Sherpa file on disk is linked from the map. Nothing is orphaned.

The four passives that look missing — present perfect continuous, past perfect
continuous, future continuous, future perfect continuous — are **deliberately
absent, and the map says so**: *"native speakers avoid every one of them,
rewriting round them instead. There is nothing there to learn, so there is no
camp."* That is a correct call, documented in the right place.

**What's missing:**

- **No activation stage anywhere.** Zero of the 26 are decks, so not one ends
  in a speaking or writing task. A learner completes the entire tense system of
  English without once producing a sentence of their own. This is the single
  biggest content gap in the catalogue.
- **No entry point.** No placement test routes a learner to the right camp.
- **No exit.** No cumulative assessment after Camp Thirteen or Descent Twelve.
  `full_grammar_test.html` (45 questions) is the obvious candidate but is not
  linked from the map and is `pro` while all 26 camps are free.
- **The numbering is not the difficulty order.** Camp Four is B1, Camp Five is
  A2, Camp Seven is A2, Camp Eight is B1. A learner walking 1→13 zig-zags
  across levels. Either renumber, or label the map with levels and offer an
  "A2 first" path.
- **Nothing bridges active to passive.** The descents assume the learner will
  work out the mapping; there is no lesson on *why* and *when* English chooses
  the passive.

## 2. Business & professional communication

**B2 → C1 · 16 lessons · 6 decks · 1 free**

The second-largest coherent body and the most commercially obvious course:
meetings, talking with clients, the design pitch, product speaking,
negotiation (×3), the docket, managing energy projects, feedback that lands,
decision-making under uncertainty, contingency and trade-offs, dailies review,
workplace roleplay, emails and calls.

**What's missing:**

- **Emails, Calls & Follow-ups exists only as Part 3.** Parts 1 and 2 are not
  in the catalogue. Worse, Part 3 is one of only two free lessons in this
  family — so the free sample of the business strand is the third instalment
  of a course whose first two parts were never written. Two lessons would fix
  the strand and the shop window at once.
- **No telephone lesson**, despite "Calls" in that title. Nothing covers
  taking a message, spelling out a name, or handling a bad line.
- **No B1 tier.** The whole family starts at B2, so an intermediate
  professional has no way in.
- **No written-output lesson** — no email you actually compose, no report.

## 3. Modals

**A2 → B2 · 9 lessons · 1 deck**

*must* / *have to* / *should have* taught through Minecraft, VfB Stuttgart,
LEGO (Polish support), Taekwondo (×3), the fire brigade, a firefighter lesson
and its German counterpart, and past modals.

**What's missing:**

- **No canonical lesson.** Nine themed treatments of the same few modals and
  no plain reference a learner is sent to first.
- **Obligation is over-served, possibility is not.** Almost everything is
  *must* / *have to*. *may* / *might* / *could* for possibility appear only in
  `might_vs_going_to_test.html`, which is a test with no lesson behind it.
- Nothing on the deontic/epistemic split — *he must be tired* against *he must
  leave* — which is where B2 learners actually come unstuck.

## 4. Prepositions

**A2 → C1 · 8 lessons · 0 decks · 4 of 8 with no level**

Crime (A2), a B1 double lesson, B2 advanced + Part 2, and a celebrity strand:
Marilyn Monroe (B1), JFK (B2), Friedrich I of Prussia (C1), plus Twin Peaks
(C1).

**What's missing:**

- **`JFK & Prepositions Part 2` has no Part 1.** Marilyn is almost certainly
  the intended predecessor but is not labelled or linked as such.
- The celebrity strand (Marilyn → JFK → Friedrich) is a ready-made A2→C1
  progression that is nowhere presented as one.
- Half the family has no level, so it cannot be ordered automatically.

## 5. Common Mistakes / remedial

**A2 → B2 · 6 lessons · 0 decks**

Get It Right (B1–B2), Review Parts 1–3, Swiss Precision 1–2 for German
speakers. Complete as a sequence — no orphan parts.

**What's missing:** a diagnostic at the front. This is the one family whose
whole purpose is to fix specific errors, and there is no way to find out which
errors a given learner makes before assigning six lessons.

## 6. Nietzsche on Film

**C1 · 6 lessons · 0 decks**

Base lesson, Grammar Tests I–III, Vocabulary Parts IV–V. **Complete** — the
only multi-part family besides Sherpa with no missing instalment.

**What's missing:** nothing structural. It is un-rebuilt (no decks), and it has
no activation stage.

## 7. Themed strands that are not yet courses

**LEGO (9)** and **Minecraft/Dino (11)** are the two biggest. Both are a
*setting*, not a syllabus: LEGO covers prepositions, phrasal verbs, passive
voice, present perfect and must/have to; Minecraft covers must/have to, past
modals, tense review and two C1 expeditions. A learner who likes the theme has
no path through it, and 10 of the 20 have no level set.

These would become courses cheaply — order them by grammar point, add a hub
page like the Sherpa route map, and set the levels.

---

## 8. The beginner end is the thinnest part of the catalogue

**A1: 6 lessons. A2: 21. Against B2: 55.**

And two of the six A1 lessons are orphans:

- **`Ordering Food & Drink — A1 Part 2` has no Part 1.** (`Snack Attack! (A1)`
  is a different lesson.)
- Water Polo A1 Part 2 follows Water Polo A0, which is fine, but the pair plus
  Question Words is the entire A0 offering.

A complete beginner arriving at the site has six lessons, one of which starts
at Part 2. **This is the largest commissioning gap in the catalogue** — and
the one most likely to cost sign-ups, since it is the first thing a new learner
sees.

C2 has exactly one lesson, which is a smaller problem but worth knowing.

## What I would build, in order

1. **Two lessons: Emails & Calls Parts 1 and 2.** Fixes the worst orphan, and
   repairs the free shop window for the whole business strand.
2. **A placement test.** One lesson, serves every course, and is the thing that
   makes sequencing worth anything. Nothing in the catalogue currently routes a
   learner anywhere.
3. **Set the 78 missing levels.** No new content, and it unblocks ordering and
   the library's own level filter.
4. **Give Sherpa an activation stage.** Rebuilding 26 lessons is large, so
   start with one cumulative production task at the end of the camps rather
   than 26 separate ones.
5. **Four or five A1 lessons**, including Ordering Food & Drink Part 1.
6. **Hub pages for LEGO and Minecraft**, modelled on the Sherpa route map.
   Cheap, and converts 20 scattered lessons into two courses.
7. `JFK` Part 1 — or relabel Marilyn as Part 1 and link the celebrity strand.

Items 2, 3 and 6 add almost no writing and produce most of the structure.
