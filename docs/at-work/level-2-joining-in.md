# Book 2 — Joining In (A2/B1)

*Participating and collaborating.* Read `README.md` first: the world, the
cast, the strands, the grading and the artwork spec all live there. This file
is the fifteen units.

**The company.** Tidewater, Rotterdam. A hundred and twenty people. An online
retailer of outdoor gear — tents, jackets, boots, and since last spring the
e-bikes made by Larkline in Copenhagen. Cross-functional teams, a customer
support desk that never closes, a freelance designer in London, and a
warehouse by the river that ships across Europe.

**The year.** Noor Haddad joins the product team in Unit 1, knowing the
product and nobody's name. By Unit 15 she has run a trade-fair stand alone,
handled her first conflict, read a dashboard without help and been told
there is a senior role in two years. Katrin leads the team, Marco runs
support, Jonas owns the numbers, Aisha designs from London.

**Grading.** A2 into B1. Dialogues 110–150 words, 10–12 lines, sentences of
about fourteen words. Present perfect from Unit 4, *will* from Unit 7, first
conditional from Unit 5, passives from Unit 10, past continuous from Unit 12,
verb patterns from Unit 13. No second conditional, no past perfect, no
reported speech. See README §5.

**Artwork accent:** sea green. Noor's green rucksack is in every picture she
is in. Stem and slots: README §7.

## Unit 1 · The Modern Team
*Who does what, and who you work with.*

**Can do:** explain your role and who you work with; say what people usually do and what they are doing now; welcome a new colleague in a group chat.

**Scenario.** Noor's first morning. Katrin walks her round a half-empty office — Jonas is at home, Aisha is in London — and explains who does what. Later, Marco introduces her in the team channel and the others say hello.

| strand | |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | department, team, role, product, support, marketing, data, design, finance · cross-functional, report to, work with, be responsible for, be in charge of, run (a team) · team lead, analyst, freelancer, colleague · group chat, channel, thread |
| Grammar | present simple for what people usually do against present continuous for now and this week: *Jonas usually sits here, but he's working from home today* |
| Communication | explaining your role, your team and who you work with |
| In the Room | welcoming a new member to a group chat, and answering the welcome |

**Lead-in.** Who do you work with most, and what do they do? Look at the picture. Which desks are empty, and where are those people?

**Grammar in the dialogue.**
- Jonas usually **sits** here, but he**'s working** from home today.
- What **do** you **do**, exactly? — I **work** with support and marketing.
- This week you**'re working** with Marco. He **runs** customer support.
- She**'s designing** the winter catalogue at the moment.

**In the Room — phrase box.**
- *Everyone, this is Noor. She's joining the product team today.*
- *Say hi!*
- *Welcome to the team.*
- *Hi all! Great to be here.*
- *If you need anything, just ask here.*
- *That's what the channel is for.*

**Model dialogue.** *Katrin walks Noor through the office.*

KATRIN: So, this is the product team. Jonas usually **sits** here, but he**'s working** from home today.
NOOR: What **does** Jonas **do**?
KATRIN: He**'s** our analyst. He **works** with the data, and he **reports** to me. What about you — what **do** you **do**, exactly?
NOOR: I**'m** a product assistant. I **work** with support and marketing, I think?
KATRIN: Right. You**'re** cross-functional — you **work** with everyone. This week you**'re working** with Marco. He **runs** customer support.
NOOR: And Aisha? I saw her name on the board.
KATRIN: Aisha **is** our freelance designer. She**'s** in London. She**'s designing** the winter catalogue at the moment.

*The team chat, later.*

MARCO: *Everyone, this is Noor. She's joining the product team today. Say hi!*
JONAS: *Hi Noor! Welcome to the team.* I'm Jonas — the one who isn't in the office.
NOOR: *Hi all! Great to be here.* I**'m** still **learning** all the names.
MARCO: *If you need anything, just ask here. That's what the channel is for.*

**The case.**
- *Speak:* You are Katrin. Introduce the team to a new starter: who does what, who reports to whom, and what each person is working on this week. Then swap: you are the new starter, and ask three questions.
- *Write:* Write the welcome message for a new colleague in the team channel, and the new colleague's reply: two lines each.

**Homework.**
- *Do:* Write six sentences about your team: what each person usually does and what they are doing this week.
- *Self-check:*
  1. Jonas usually ___ (sit) here.
  2. He ___ (work) from home today.
  3. What ___ you ___ (do), exactly?
  4. Aisha ___ (design) the catalogue at the moment.
  5. Marco ___ (run) support.
- *Key:* 1 sits · 2 's working · 3 do … do · 4 's designing · 5 runs

## Unit 2 · Getting the Message
*Threads, voice notes, and a very bad line.*

**Can do:** say what you sent, forwarded and received, and when; leave a clear async update; ask someone to repeat when the connection is bad.

**Scenario.** Noor sent Aisha the catalogue files, then forwarded a thread, then left a voice note — and Aisha, on a bad line from London, got about half of it. They sort out what arrived and what didn't, and move the details into the chat.

| strand | |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | thread, DM, tag, mention, forward, reply, reply all, voice note, async, notification, mute, unread · send, receive, miss, check, delete · connection, signal, line |
| Grammar | past simple for completed actions, regular and irregular, with the negative and the question: *I sent it yesterday. Did you get it? I didn't see it.* |
| Communication | leaving updates by voice note and async message, and confirming what arrived |
| In the Room | asking someone to repeat, and rescuing a call that is breaking up |

**Lead-in.** How many messages did you send yesterday, and on how many apps? Look at the picture. Which of the two can hear the other?

**Grammar in the dialogue.**
- I **sent** you the catalogue files yesterday. **Did** you **get** them?
- I **got** the photos, but I **didn't see** a message about the sizes.
- I **forwarded** the thread to you on Tuesday. Marco **tagged** you in it.
- I **missed** the voice note completely. My notifications **were** off.

**In the Room — phrase box.**
- *Sorry, you cut out. Can you say that again?*
- *Did you say Tuesday or Thursday?*
- *You're breaking up.*
- *Can you type it in the chat?*
- *I lost you for a second.*
- *Let me call you back on the phone.*

**Model dialogue.** *Noor and Aisha on a video call. The connection is bad.*

NOOR: Hi Aisha. I **sent** you the catalogue files yesterday. **Did** you **get** them?
AISHA: Some of them. I **got** the photos, but I **didn't see** a message about the sizes.
NOOR: Ah — I **forwarded** the thread to you on Tuesday. Marco **tagged** you in it.
AISHA: Sorry, *you cut out. Can you say that again?*
NOOR: I **forwarded** the thread. On Tuesday.
AISHA: *Did you say Tuesday or Thursday?*
NOOR: Tuesday. I **tagged** you, and I **left** a voice note too.
AISHA: *You're breaking up*, Noor. *Can you type it in the chat?*
NOOR: OK — one second. There. Did that arrive?
AISHA: Yes! Now I can see it. I **missed** the voice note completely. My notifications **were** off.
NOOR: No problem. *Let me call you back on the phone* — this line is terrible.

**The case.**
- *Speak:* Record a thirty-second voice note for a colleague in another city: what you did today, what you sent them, and one question. Then play it to your partner on a "bad line" — they ask you to repeat twice.
- *Write:* Write the follow-up message that goes with the voice note: three things you sent, when, and what you need back.

**Homework.**
- *Do:* Write the message that follows a voice note: three things you sent, when, and what you need back.
- *Self-check:*
  1. I ___ (send) the files yesterday.
  2. ___ you ___ (get) them?
  3. I ___ (not see) the message.
  4. Marco ___ (tag) you on Tuesday.
  5. My notifications ___ (be) off.
- *Key:* 1 sent · 2 Did … get · 3 didn't see · 4 tagged · 5 were

**Artwork.**
- `u02-opener` — two windows side by side: Noor at a desk in Rotterdam with her green rucksack on the chair, Aisha at a drawing desk in London with a tablet and stylus; between them a jagged sea-green line of a signal breaking into pieces
- `u02-scene` — a laptop screen with a frozen, pixelated face in the video window and a chat panel beside it filling with short bubbles

## Unit 3 · Products & Services
*Lighter, tougher, and the best-selling tent we have.*

**Can do:** describe a product's features and benefits; compare it with competitors using comparatives and superlatives; give an opinion strongly or carefully.

**Scenario.** Jonas and Noor write the web page for Tidewater's new tent: the lightest they sell, more waterproof than most, more expensive than the competitor's — and the free thirty-day trial. Noor learns that the most expensive thing on the site is the e-bike from Copenhagen.

| strand | |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | feature, benefit, subscription, free trial, plan, premium, basic, range · lightweight, waterproof, durable, reliable, compact · competitor, market, customer review, rating, price range · best-selling, most popular |
| Grammar | comparatives and superlatives, short and long forms and the irregulars: *lighter than, more waterproof than, the lightest, the most durable, the best* |
| Communication | describing a product against its competitors: features, benefits, price, reviews |
| In the Room | expressing strong and weak opinions, and hearing the difference |

**Lead-in.** What was the last thing you bought online, and why that one and not the cheaper one? Look at the picture. Which tent would you choose?

**Grammar in the dialogue.**
- It's **lighter than** the old one. Is that **the lightest** we sell?
- It's **more waterproof than** most tents in this price range.
- It's **more expensive** — but it's **the most durable** tent in the test.
- **The highest** rating on the site — four point eight.

**In the Room — phrase box.**
- *I really think …* / *There's no doubt about that.*
- *Absolutely.*
- *Personally, I'd say …*
- *I suppose …*
- *I'm not sure, but …*
- *I'd go for …*

**Model dialogue.** *Jonas and Noor at one screen, writing the product page.*

JONAS: OK, the new tent. What's the headline? *I really think* it's the weight.
NOOR: It's **lighter than** the old one — one point four kilos. Is that **the lightest** we sell?
JONAS: Yes, by a long way. And it's **more waterproof than** most tents in this price range.
NOOR: *I'm not sure, but* customers care more about price. Is it **cheaper than** the competitor's?
JONAS: No. It's **more expensive** — but it's **the most durable** tent in the test.
NOOR: So the benefit is: it lasts longer. *Personally, I'd say* that's **the best** argument.
JONAS: *Absolutely.* And the reviews — **the highest** rating on the site. Four point eight.
NOOR: *I suppose* we should mention the free trial? Thirty days, full refund.
JONAS: *There's no doubt about that.* People love a trial. What's **the most popular** item overall, by the way?
NOOR: I know this one. The e-bike from Copenhagen. It's also **the most expensive** thing we sell!

**The case.**
- *Speak:* Choose two products you know — two phones, two jackets, two apps. Present one to a customer against the other: three comparisons, one superlative, one strong opinion and one careful one.
- *Write:* Write the product page for the tent: a headline, three features with their benefits, one comparison with a competitor and the trial offer, in eighty words.

**Homework.**
- *Do:* Write a product page for something you own: three features, two comparisons and one superlative.
- *Self-check:*
  1. It's ___ (light) than the old one.
  2. It's the ___ (durable) tent in the test.
  3. Is it ___ (cheap) than the competitor's?
  4. The ___ (high) rating on the site.
  5. The e-bike is the ___ (expensive) thing we sell.
- *Key:* 1 lighter · 2 most durable · 3 cheaper · 4 highest · 5 most expensive

**Artwork.**
- `u03-opener` — two tents pitched side by side on a grassy dune, one small and sleek and one bulky, a sea-green flag on the sleek one; Jonas's silhouette with a second monitor under his arm and Noor with her green rucksack comparing them from the path
- `u03-scene` — a web page as a picture: a large photo block, three feature icons in a row, a star rating drawn as five shapes, one sea-green button

## Unit 4 · The Gig Worker
*What have you worked on?*

**Can do:** talk about professional experience with the present perfect; interview a freelancer about background, rates and availability; describe your own background.

**Scenario.** Katrin renews Aisha's contract for another year and lets Noor sit in so she learns how it's done: background, the projects Aisha has and hasn't done, day rate, invoices, deadlines, availability.

| strand | |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | freelancer, contractor, day rate, hourly rate, invoice, deadline, brief, deliverable, contract, availability, portfolio, client · quote, charge, bill, deliver · experience, background, in-house |
| Grammar | present perfect for experience up to now: *I've worked with four outdoor brands; I've never done packaging; Have you ever done…?* — against the past simple for a finished time: *Before that, I worked in-house* |
| Communication | interviewing a freelancer for a project: experience, rates, terms, availability |
| In the Room | talking about your background in a way that is easy to follow |

**Lead-in.** Have you ever worked as a freelancer, or hired one? Look at the picture. Who is the freelancer, and how can you tell?

**Grammar in the dialogue.**
- **I've worked** with four outdoor brands, and **I've designed** two apps.
- **Have** you ever **done** packaging? — No, **I've never done** packaging.
- **Have** you **had** any problems with our invoices? — Only once.
- Before that, I **worked** in-house at a sports brand.

**In the Room — phrase box.**
- *Tell me a bit about your background.*
- *I've been a freelancer since …*
- *Before that, I …*
- *What kind of projects have you done?*
- *My main clients are …*
- *I started out as …*

**Model dialogue.** *Katrin's desk. Aisha on video from London; Noor listening.*

KATRIN: So, Aisha — for Noor's benefit — *tell me a bit about your background.*
AISHA: Sure. *I've been a freelancer since* twenty nineteen. *Before that, I* **worked** in-house at a sports brand.
NOOR: *What kind of projects have you done?*
AISHA: Mostly catalogues and web pages. **I've worked** with four outdoor brands, and **I've designed** two apps.
KATRIN: **Have** you ever **done** packaging?
AISHA: No, **I've never done** packaging. But I'd like to.
KATRIN: Good, because we need some. Your day rate is the same as last year?
AISHA: Yes. I invoice at the end of each month, thirty days to pay. And I need briefs a week before the deadline.
KATRIN: That's fair. **Have** you **had** any problems with our invoices?
AISHA: Only once — but Marco **fixed** it the same day.
NOOR: And your availability?
AISHA: *My main clients are* you and one other. So, three days a week for you.

**The case.**
- *Speak:* Interview your partner as a freelancer for a two-month project: background, three experience questions with *Have you ever…?*, day rate, deadlines, availability. Then swap.
- *Write:* Write your own freelancer profile in sixty words: what you've done, who you've worked with, what you've never done but would like to, and your terms.

**Homework.**
- *Do:* Write your own freelancer profile in sixty words.
- *Self-check:*
  1. I ___ (work) with four brands.
  2. ___ you ever ___ (do) packaging?
  3. Before that, I ___ (work) in-house.
  4. I ___ (never do) an app.
  5. Marco ___ (fix) it the same day.
- *Key:* 1 've worked · 2 Have … done · 3 worked · 4 've never done · 5 fixed

**Artwork.**
- `u04-opener` — a bright home studio in London: Aisha at a drawing desk with a tablet and stylus, a wall of pinned catalogue pages, a laptop showing Katrin's scarf and Noor's green rucksack in two small video windows, a sea-green invoice stamp on the desk
- `u04-scene` — a calendar page with three days a week shaded sea green, a stylus resting across it

## Unit 5 · Customer Support
*If you send me a photo, I'll ship it today.*

**Can do:** handle a customer problem step by step; make promises and set conditions with the first conditional; calm a customer who is annoyed.

**Scenario.** Noor shadows Marco on a call. A tent has arrived with a pole missing, the chatbot was no help, and the customer is camping on Saturday. Marco apologises first, then lays out exactly what will happen if the pole doesn't arrive in time.

| strand | |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | ticket, refund, replacement, return, exchange, chatbot, FAQ, live chat, agent, escalate, resolve, response time, complaint, apology · frustrated, patient, rude, calm |
| Grammar | first conditional for real promises and conditions: *If you send me a photo, I'll ship a pole today. If it doesn't arrive by Friday, we'll refund you.* |
| Communication | dealing with a customer problem step by step: acknowledge, check, offer, confirm |
| In the Room | calming down an annoyed customer in the first two sentences |

**Lead-in.** When did you last complain to a company? What did they say first? Look at the picture. What is missing from the box?

**Grammar in the dialogue.**
- **If you send** me a photo of the box, **I'll ship** a pole today.
- **If it doesn't arrive** by Friday, **we'll refund** the whole tent.
- **If you prefer** a replacement tent instead, **I'll send** one now.
- **If you say** sorry first, **the rest is** easy.

**In the Room — phrase box.**
- *I'm sorry you've had this experience.*
- *I understand how frustrating that is.*
- *Let me see what I can do.*
- *Here's what we'll do.*
- *Thanks for your patience.*
- *Leave it with me.*

**Model dialogue.** *Marco takes a call. Noor listens on the second headset.*

CUSTOMER: My tent arrived yesterday, and there's a pole missing. The chatbot was useless.
MARCO: *I'm sorry you've had this experience. Let me see what I can do.* Can I have the order number?
CUSTOMER: Eight-seven-two-one. I'm camping on Saturday!
MARCO: *I understand how frustrating that is. Here's what we'll do.* **If you send** me a photo of the box, **I'll ship** a pole today.
CUSTOMER: And if it doesn't come in time?
MARCO: **If it doesn't arrive** by Friday, **we'll refund** the whole tent. You keep it.
CUSTOMER: Really?
MARCO: Really. And **if you prefer** a replacement tent instead, **I'll send** one now. Your choice.
CUSTOMER: The pole is fine. Sorry — I was quite rude at the start.
MARCO: Not at all. *Thanks for your patience.* I've opened a ticket, so **if anything changes**, **you'll get** an email.
NOOR: *(after the call)* How did you stay so calm?
MARCO: The first sentence. **If you say** sorry first, **the rest is** easy.

**The case.**
- *Speak:* You are the customer: a jacket arrived in the wrong size, and you need it for a trip on Friday. Your partner is the agent: apologise, check, and make two conditional offers. Swap roles.
- *Write:* Write the follow-up email to the tent customer: what you have done, what will happen if the pole is late, and one line that keeps them calm.

**Homework.**
- *Do:* Write the follow-up email to an annoyed customer: an apology and two conditional promises.
- *Self-check:*
  1. If you ___ (send) a photo, I'll ship it today.
  2. If it doesn't arrive, we ___ (refund) you.
  3. If you ___ (prefer), I'll send a replacement.
  4. If anything ___ (change), you'll get an email.
  5. We'll call you if the courier ___ (be) late.
- *Key:* 1 send · 2 'll refund · 3 prefer · 4 changes · 5 is

**Artwork.**
- `u05-opener` — a support desk with two headsets on a shared screen, Marco with his mug mid-call and Noor with her green rucksack under the desk listening in; on the screen, a tent drawn with one pole missing, the gap outlined in sea green
- `u05-scene` — an open cardboard box on a doormat with tent fabric inside and one long empty slot in the foam

## Unit 6 · Greener Choices
*Too much plastic, not enough card.*

**Can do:** describe quantities and problems with quantifiers; suggest improvements to packaging and the office; agree and disagree with colleagues politely.

**Scenario.** A team meeting on packaging. Jonas has the numbers — too much plastic, hardly any recycled card — and Noor has found a few local suppliers. Katrin agrees up to a point; Jonas disagrees on cost. They settle on a few changes, one at a time.

| strand | |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | packaging, plastic, cardboard, emissions, carbon, local supplier, delivery route, recycled, reusable, waste, footprint, energy, heating · reduce, cut, replace, switch to, use up · quote |
| Grammar | quantifiers with countable and uncountable nouns: *too much plastic, not enough card, a little, a few suppliers, fewer emissions, plenty of stock, not much heating* |
| Communication | suggesting green improvements and weighing the cost |
| In the Room | agreeing and disagreeing politely, at more than one strength |

**Lead-in.** What did the last parcel you received come wrapped in? Look at the picture. Which pile is bigger, and which should be?

**Grammar in the dialogue.**
- We use **too much** plastic. And **not enough** recycled card — only **a little**.
- There are **a few** local suppliers of recycled card.
- The delivery route is shorter, so **fewer** emissions.
- We have **plenty of** plastic in stock.

**In the Room — phrase box.**
- *Can I suggest something?*
- *That's a fair point, but …*
- *I see what you mean, but …*
- *I'm not sure I agree.*
- *I agree up to a point.*
- *Absolutely.*

**Model dialogue.** *A team meeting. Katrin, Jonas and Noor.*

KATRIN: Packaging. Jonas, what do the numbers say?
JONAS: We use **too much** plastic. Forty per cent of parcels have plastic inside. And **not enough** recycled card — only **a little**.
NOOR: *Can I suggest something?* There are **a few** local suppliers of recycled card. One is twenty minutes from here.
KATRIN: *That's a fair point, but* local isn't always cheaper.
NOOR: *I see what you mean, but* the delivery route is shorter, so **fewer** emissions.
JONAS: *I'm not sure I agree* on cost. We have **plenty of** plastic in stock. Throwing it away is also waste.
KATRIN: *I agree up to a point.* Use it up, then switch.
NOOR: And the office? There's **too much** printing. We could cut it.
JONAS: *Absolutely.* And **not much** heating on Fridays — half the team is at home.
KATRIN: OK. **A few** changes, one at a time. Noor, get a quote from the local supplier.

**The case.**
- *Speak:* Propose three green changes for your own office or school. Your partner agrees with one, agrees with one up to a point, and disagrees with one — politely. Then swap.
- *Write:* Write the message to the local card supplier asking for a quote: how many boxes a month, what they must be made of, and when you need them.

**Homework.**
- *Do:* Write three green proposals for your office, each with a quantity in it.
- *Self-check:*
  1. We use too ___ plastic. (much / many)
  2. Not ___ card. (enough / plenty)
  3. There are a ___ local suppliers. (few / little)
  4. We have ___ of stock. (plenty / much)
  5. A shorter route means ___ emissions. (fewer / less)
- *Key:* 1 much · 2 enough · 3 few · 4 plenty · 5 fewer

**Artwork.**
- `u06-opener` — a warehouse packing bench with two heaps, a tall heap of plastic air pillows and a small stack of brown card, a meeting table in front of it where Katrin's scarf, Jonas's second monitor and Noor's green rucksack sit round three coffee cups; a sea-green recycling mark on one box
- `u06-scene` — a delivery van on a short road between a paper mill by the river and the warehouse, drawn as a simple map with one sea-green route

## Unit 7 · Business Travel
*Munich, a layover, and a strike.*

**Can do:** arrange a trip and a schedule using *will*, *going to* and the present continuous; talk about itineraries and expenses; change or cancel an arrangement politely.

**Scenario.** Noor's first business trip — the outdoor trade fair in Munich with Katrin. Flights via Frankfurt, receipts and per diems, and Noor's plan to stay the weekend. Then an airport strike moves everything, and Noor ends up running the stand alone on Thursday.

| strand | |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | itinerary, layover, connection, expenses, receipt, per diem, booking, check-in, trade fair, stand, bleisure, direct · book, rebook, cancel, postpone, claim · short notice |
| Grammar | three future forms and what each one does: *We're flying on Tuesday* (an arrangement), *I'm going to stay the weekend* (a plan), *I'll send you the itinerary* (a decision now) |
| Communication | arranging a schedule and a corporate trip |
| In the Room | changing or cancelling an arrangement without annoying anyone |

**Lead-in.** What was your last trip for work or study, and what went wrong? Look at the picture. Which of the three future plans has changed?

**Grammar in the dialogue.**
- **We're flying** on Tuesday morning. The stand opens at ten.
- **I'll send** you the itinerary.
- **I'm going to stay** the weekend, if that's OK.
- **We'll take** the train. **I'm leaving** Wednesday.

**In the Room — phrase box.**
- *I'm afraid I have to change the plan.*
- *Sorry for the short notice.*
- *Would Monday work instead?*
- *Something's come up.*
- *I'll rebook the hotel.*
- *Can we push it back to …?*

## Unit 15 · Future Skills
*When I finish the course, I'll …*

**Can do:** talk about short- and long-term goals with future clauses; describe training, tools and career paths; congratulate a colleague on a promotion.

**Scenario.** Noor's end-of-year review with Katrin: the data course, a day a week with analytics when she passes, the AI writing tools arriving in January, and a senior role in two years if she keeps going. Then the team chat: Marco has been promoted.

| strand | |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | upskilling, course, certificate, training, promotion, role, responsibility, career path, mentor, AI tool, automation, skill, senior · apply for, sign up for, move into, take on, pass |
| Grammar | future clauses with *when*, *as soon as* and *if* + present, and *will* in the main clause: *When I finish the course, I'll ask to run the dashboards. As soon as you pass, we'll move you.* |
| Communication | discussing short- and long-term goals in a review |
| In the Room | congratulating someone on a promotion, and accepting congratulations |

**Lead-in.** What do you want to be able to do this time next year? Look at the picture. Who has just had good news?

**Grammar in the dialogue.**
- **When I finish** the data course, **I'll ask** to run the dashboards with Jonas.
- **As soon as you pass**, **we'll move** you onto the analytics team for one day a week.
- **If you keep going** like this, **there'll be** a senior role in two years.
- **As soon as I start**, **I'll buy** the cakes.

**In the Room — phrase box.**
- *Congratulations!*
- *Well deserved.*
- *So pleased for you.*
- *You've earned it.*
- *When do you start?*
- *Let's celebrate.*
- *Thanks, all. It was a team effort.*

**Model dialogue.** *Noor's review with Katrin; then the team chat.*

KATRIN: One year. Where do you want to be this time next year?
NOOR: **When I finish** the data course, **I'll ask** to run the dashboards with Jonas.
KATRIN: Good. **As soon as you pass**, **we'll move** you onto the analytics team for one day a week.
NOOR: And **if there's** budget, **I want** to learn the AI tools — the ones for writing product copy.
KATRIN: There is. **When the new tools arrive** in January, **you'll be** the first to test them.
NOOR: Is there a career path from here?
KATRIN: **If you keep going** like this, **there'll be** a senior role in two years.

JONAS: *Everyone — Marco has been promoted! Head of Customer Experience from March.*

AISHA: *So pleased for you. You've earned it.*
MARCO: *Thanks, all. It was a team effort.* **As soon as I start**, **I'll buy** the cakes.
NOOR: *When do you start? Let's celebrate* on Thursday.

**The case.**
- *Speak:* It's your review. Tell your manager three goals with *when*, *as soon as* and *if*. Then a colleague announces a promotion in the chat — congratulate them and arrange to celebrate.
- *Write:* Write your goals for next year in six sentences, each with a future clause — then a two-line congratulations message to a colleague.
