# Book 3 — Taking Charge (B1+/B2)

*Managing and organising.* Read `README.md` first: the world, the cast, the
strands, the grading and the artwork spec all live there. This file is the
fifteen units.

**The company.** Larkline, Copenhagen. Four hundred people. E-bikes: frames
welded in a plant outside Lisbon, batteries from Asia and then from Poland,
assembly and paint in Copenhagen. Sold online, through the company's own
shop, and through retailers across Europe — Tidewater in Rotterdam among
them. The office furniture is from Greenline in Bristol; a pale-wood desk sits
in the background of every office scene.

**The year.** Daniel Mensah arrives from Manchester as the new operations
manager in Unit 1, on a bike, in the rain. He inherits a customer journey
that stops at the shop door, a paint line with one oven, and then a port
strike, a supplier fire and a battery recall. By Unit 15 he has run the
retail-journey project, the recall and the nearshoring of the batteries, and
Beatriz, who talked him into taking a Friday off, is leaving for Berlin. Elin
owns the product, Yusuf owns the creators, Ken owns the numbers, Beatriz owns
the people.

**Grading.** B1+ into B2. Dialogues 160–220 words, 11–15 lines, sentences of
about eighteen words and up to two clauses. Second conditional from Unit 10,
past perfect from Unit 12, reported speech from Unit 13, future perfect and
continuous from Unit 15. No mixed conditionals, no inversion, no participle
clauses, no clefts. See README §5.

**Artwork accent:** amber. Daniel's amber cycling helmet is in every picture
he is in. Stem and slots: README §7.

## Unit 1 · The Hybrid Model
*Two days in, three days out, and a factory that can't work from home.*

**Can do:** introduce yourself to a distributed team; describe how a hybrid arrangement works; make small talk and sort out the tech while people wait.

**Scenario.** Daniel's first all-hands, on video: half the team in Copenhagen, the frame team in Portugal, Yusuf on a train. He introduces himself, learns about hot-desking and overlap hours, and manages a frozen screen and an echo without losing the room.

| strand | |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | hybrid, remote, on-site, hot-desking, hot desk, async, distributed team, all-hands, time zone, headquarters, factory, assembly line, overlap hours · commute, cycle in, book a desk, dial in, share a screen |
| Grammar | present simple for how things work and present continuous for what is happening now, including the temporary: *Most people do two days on-site. I'm still learning the bike lanes. Yusuf is sitting on a train.* |
| Communication | introducing yourself in a distributed team and asking how it works |
| In the Room | small talk that fills the gap, and checking tech without apologising for it |

**Lead-in.** How many days a week are you in the same room as your team? Look at the picture. Who is on-site, who is remote, and who is somewhere in between?

**Grammar in the dialogue.**
- Yusuf, who **is** apparently **sitting** on a train.
- I**'m** still **learning** the bike lanes, honestly. Everybody **cycles** here.
- You **book** a desk when you **come** in. Most people **do** two days on-site.
- We **overlap** from ten to three, and everything else **is** async.

**In the Room — phrase box.**
- *Can you hear me OK?*
- *Sorry, you froze for a second.*
- *Is my screen share showing?*
- *We can see it — go ahead.*
- *How are you finding …?*
- *Don't worry, I'm used to the weather.*
- *You're on two devices.*

**Model dialogue.** *The all-hands, on video. Elin hosts; Daniel, Yusuf and the Portugal team on screen.*

ELIN: Right, everyone's here — Copenhagen, the Portugal team, and Yusuf, who **is** apparently **sitting** on a train.
YUSUF: *Can you hear me OK?* The signal drops in tunnels.
ELIN: Loud and clear. Daniel, over to you.
DANIEL: Thanks. Hi, everyone. I'm Daniel, the new operations manager. I **come** from Manchester, so *don't worry, I'm used to the weather.*
ELIN: *How are you finding* Copenhagen so far?
DANIEL: I**'m** still **learning** the bike lanes, honestly. Everybody **cycles** here — even in this.
YUSUF: *Sorry, you froze for a second.* Did you say you cycle?
DANIEL: Every day now. So — how **does** the hybrid model **work** here? I **see** desks with no names on them.
ELIN: Hot-desking. You **book** a desk when you **come** in. Most people **do** two days on-site, three remote.
DANIEL: And Portugal?
ELIN: The frame team **works** on-site, obviously — you can't weld from home. We **overlap** from ten to three, and everything else **is** async.
DANIEL: That works for me. *Right — is my screen share showing?* I**'m putting** up my first ninety days.
YUSUF: *We can see it.* And I've lost the tunnel. *Go ahead.*

**The case.**
- *Speak:* Introduce yourself to a new distributed team in ninety seconds: who you are, where you're from, how you work, one thing you're still getting used to. Your partner interrupts twice with a tech problem; deal with it and carry on.
- *Write:* Write the "How we work" page for a new starter: office days, overlap hours, what is async, how to book a desk — eight sentences.

**Artwork.**
- `u01-opener` — a wall screen of video windows above a hot-desk office: welders in masks in one window, a train carriage in another with Yusuf's phone on a tripod, and in the room Daniel with his amber helmet under his arm, Elin with her notebook, a pale-wood Greenline desk in front of them; rain on the big window
- `u01-scene` — a bike lane in the rain seen from behind a rider in an amber helmet, a row of cargo bikes at a crossing, the office building ahead

## Unit 2 · Customer Journeys
*From the first ad to the first ride.*

**Can do:** explain a process step by step; describe what changed and when with the past simple, and what has changed so far with the present perfect; check that you have understood and clarify when you haven't.

**Scenario.** Elin walks Daniel along the wall where the customer journey is mapped, sticky note by sticky note. The pain point is app onboarding — a third of new riders lost in week one last year, fixed twice since, a setup call added in March. The shop journey has never been mapped. That is Daniel's first project.

| strand | |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | customer journey, touchpoint, UX, onboarding, churn, funnel, drop-off, retention, test ride, app, pain point, feedback loop · map, track, improve, redesign, solve · since, for, ago, so far |
| Grammar | past simple for a finished moment against present perfect for a result that still counts: *Last year we lost a third of new riders. We've redesigned the onboarding twice since then. In March we added a call.* |
| Communication | explaining a process and mapping the customer experience |
| In the Room | checking understanding and clarifying — the questions that stop you nodding at something you haven't followed |

**Lead-in.** Think of something you bought recently: list every contact you had with the company before and after. Look at the picture. Which note on the wall is the problem?

**Grammar in the dialogue.**
- Last year we **lost** a third of new riders in the first week.
- **We've redesigned** the onboarding twice since then.
- Drop-off **has fallen** to twelve per cent so far this year. In March we **added** a ten-minute setup call.
- **We haven't solved** the retail side, though.

**In the Room — phrase box.**
- *So, if I've understood correctly, …*
- *What do you mean by …?*
- *And has that helped?*
- *Let me make sure I've got this: …*
- *So what you're saying is …*
- *Can you give me an example?*

**Model dialogue.** *Elin and Daniel at the journey wall.*

ELIN: This wall is the customer journey. Every sticky note is a touchpoint, from the first ad to the first ride.
DANIEL: *So, if I've understood correctly*, the amber notes are the problems?
ELIN: Pain points, yes. The biggest is here — app onboarding. Last year we **lost** a third of new riders in the first week.
DANIEL: *What do you mean by "lost"?* They **returned** the bike?
ELIN: No — they **stopped** opening the app. Churn. **We've redesigned** the onboarding twice since then.
DANIEL: *And has that helped?*
ELIN: **It's improved** a lot. Drop-off **has fallen** to twelve per cent so far this year. In March we **added** a ten-minute setup call, and that **made** the biggest difference.
DANIEL: *Let me make sure I've got this:* the call **fixed** most of the churn, and the app changes **did** the rest?
ELIN: More or less. **We haven't solved** the retail side, though. Riders who **buy** in a shop **never get** the call.
DANIEL: *So what you're saying is*, the journey depends on where they buy?
ELIN: Exactly. And **we've only mapped** the online one. The shop journey is your first project.

**The case.**
- *Speak:* Map the journey of a customer of your own company or school on a table with sticky notes, then talk your partner through it: what happened last year, what has changed since, and where the pain point is. They check understanding three times.
- *Write:* Write the project brief for the shop journey: what has been mapped so far, what hasn't, what you did in March and why it worked, and the first three steps.

**Artwork.**
- `u02-opener` — a long wall of sticky notes in a winding line from an advert at one end to a bike at the other, a few notes amber among the pale ones; Elin with her notebook pointing at the amber cluster, Daniel with his helmet hooked on his arm, a pale-wood desk in the foreground
- `u02-scene` — a phone showing a setup screen with a progress bar two thirds full, a bike leaning on a shop counter behind it

## Unit 3 · Agile Projects
*Two hundred bikes by Friday, one oven, and a request from marketing.*

**Can do:** give a quick, precise update in a stand-up; use *will*, *going to* and the present continuous for predictions, plans and arrangements; keep a meeting moving through a frozen screen and an echo.

**Scenario.** The ops stand-up. The sprint goal is two hundred bikes by Friday; the paint line is the bottleneck because one oven is down; Yusuf wants twenty bikes in a new colour for a launch video, which Daniel names as scope creep. Elin de-scopes it. Daniel's screen freezes and then echoes.

| strand | |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | sprint, sprint goal, backlog, bottleneck, scope creep, bandwidth, capacity, velocity, blocker, retro, ticket, shift · prioritise, de-scope, push back, ship, hit (a target) |
| Grammar | the three futures doing three jobs: *We're going to hit it* (a prediction from evidence), *The engineer is coming tomorrow* (an arrangement), *I'll de-scope it* (a decision made now) |
| Communication | giving quick updates in a daily stand-up: goal, status, blocker, ask |
| In the Room | dealing with technical glitches without losing the thread |

**Lead-in.** What is the bottleneck in your work this week — the one thing everything else waits for? Look at the picture. Which machine is the problem?

**Grammar in the dialogue.**
- **We're going to hit** it, but only just.
- The engineer **is coming** tomorrow morning. Until then **we'll run** two shifts on the other one.
- **I'll de-scope** it. Yusuf, **you'll get** ten next sprint.
- **We're going to shoot** the video with those.

**In the Room — phrase box.**
- *Sorry — you've frozen.*
- *Can you turn your camera off and try again?*
- *Is that better?*
- *Your audio is echoing — you're on two devices.*
- *I'll drop the request in the backlog.*
- *Leaving one now.*

**Model dialogue.** *The ops stand-up, on video. Elin runs it; Daniel and Yusuf.*

ELIN: Stand-up. Sprint goal is two hundred bikes out of the door by Friday. Daniel?
DANIEL: **We're going to hit** it, but only just. The paint line **is** the bottleneck — it's running at eighty per cent.
ELIN: Why?
DANIEL: One oven is down. The engineer **is coming** tomorrow morning. Until then **we'll run** two shifts on the other one.
YUSUF: Small thing — marketing **will need** twenty bikes in the new colour for the launch video. By Thursday.
DANIEL: That's not small, Yusuf, that's scope creep. We don't have the bandwidth this sprint.
YUSUF: *Sorry — you've frozen.* Daniel? *Can you turn your camera off and try again?*
DANIEL: *Is that better?* OK. Twenty bikes in a new colour means a paint change, and the oven is already the problem.
ELIN: **I'll de-scope** it. Yusuf, **you'll get** ten next sprint, and **we're going to shoot** the video with those.
YUSUF: Fine. *I'll drop the request in the backlog.*
ELIN: Anything else? No? Then **we're finishing** early. *Daniel, your audio is echoing — you're on two devices.*
DANIEL: Ah. Laptop and phone. *Leaving one now.*

**The case.**
- *Speak:* Run a three-person stand-up for a project you invent: a goal, one bottleneck, one request that is scope creep. Use all three future forms. One person's connection fails halfway through.
- *Write:* Write the stand-up notes for the channel: goal, status, blocker, the de-scoped request and where it went — six lines, three future forms.

**Artwork.**
- `u03-opener` — a paint line seen from the gallery: a row of bare frames on hooks moving towards two ovens, one dark and one glowing amber, a figure in a mask at the sprayer; Daniel in his amber helmet on the gallery with a tablet, Elin's notebook on the rail
- `u03-scene` — a laptop and a phone side by side on a desk both showing the same call, a wavy line drawn between them, Yusuf's tripod in one of the windows

## Unit 4 · Global Logistics
*A strike in Lisbon, a fire in Asia, and thirty customers waiting.*

**Can do:** explain what was happening when something else happened; describe cause and effect through a supply chain; apologise for a delay in a way that keeps a customer.

**Scenario.** Daniel walks Ken through the shortfall: the frames were being loaded in Lisbon when the port strike started; while they waited, the battery supplier had a fire and lead times nearly doubled. Forty bikes built with no batteries. The fix is nearshoring to Poland and a buffer stock. Then a retailer calls with thirty waiting customers.

| strand | |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | supply chain, supplier, disruption, nearshoring, lead time, container, port, quay, customs, shortage, stock-out, buffer stock, shortfall · because of, due to, as a result, lead to, cause, follow · apologise for, make up for |
| Grammar | past continuous for the background action and past simple for the event that cut across it: *The frames were being loaded when the strike started. While we were waiting, the supplier had a fire.* |
| Communication | explaining cause and effect: what happened, what it led to, what follows |
| In the Room | apologising for a delay — the order of sorry, explanation and fix |

**Lead-in.** Where does something you use every day actually come from, and how many countries does it pass through? Look at the picture. What is waiting, and where?

**Grammar in the dialogue.**
- The frames **were being loaded** in Lisbon when the port strike **started**.
- **While we were waiting** for the frames, the battery supplier **had** a fire in one plant.
- A port strike **hit** us while the frames **were sitting** on the quay.
- We **were running** with no buffer stock at all.

**In the Room — phrase box.**
- *I'm sorry — I know how difficult that is for you.*
- *I'd like to explain what happened, and then what we're doing about it.*
- *I apologise for the delay, and for not calling sooner.*
- *We'll make it up to you.*
- *Here's what happens next.*
- *You have my number.*

**Model dialogue.** *Ken's office; later, Daniel on the phone to a retailer.*

KEN: Daniel, walk me through the shortfall. Why **did** we **miss** March?
DANIEL: Two things at once. The frames **were being loaded** in Lisbon when the port strike **started**. Three containers **sat** on the quay for nine days.
KEN: And the batteries?
DANIEL: **While we were waiting** for the frames, the battery supplier in Asia **had** a fire in one plant. Their lead time **went** from six weeks to eleven.
KEN: So the frames **caused** the March gap and the batteries **will cause** April's?
DANIEL: Exactly. One **led to** a delay; the other **led to** a shortage. As a result, we **built** forty bikes with no batteries and **parked** them.
KEN: What's the fix?
DANIEL: Nearshoring the batteries — there's a supplier in Poland. And a buffer stock of frames. We **were running** with none.

*Later, by phone.*

RETAILER: Daniel, I have thirty customers waiting for bikes you promised in March.
DANIEL: *I'm sorry — I know how difficult that is for you. I'd like to explain what happened, and then what we're doing about it.*
RETAILER: Go on.
DANIEL: A port strike **hit** us while the frames **were sitting** on the quay, and a supplier fire **followed**. *I apologise for the delay, and for not calling sooner.* Your thirty ship on Monday, and *we'll make it up to you* with free delivery on the next order.

**The case.**
- *Speak:* Explain a delay you have lived through — a project, a delivery, a train — as a chain of cause and effect, with at least two past continuous sentences. Then call the person it affected and apologise in the right order.
- *Write:* Write the email to all retailers about March: what was happening, what happened, what it led to, what changes, and one line of apology that does not make excuses.

**Artwork.**
- `u04-opener` — a container quay at dawn with three stacked containers and a closed crane, a picket of figures at the gate; in the foreground on a desk a phone, a map with two routes drawn on it, and Daniel's amber helmet; one container amber
- `u04-scene` — forty bikes in a row under a tarpaulin in a warehouse corner, each with an empty slot where a battery should be, one slot marked amber

## Unit 5 · The ESG Mandate
*Lighter, closer, repairable.*

**Can do:** present facts and numbers to people who are not financial; use comparatives and superlatives to make a change vivid; structure a short presentation with signposts and a summary.

**Scenario.** Daniel presents Larkline's carbon numbers to its retailers. Three points, three comparisons: shipping is the biggest part of the footprint; the new frame is fifteen per cent lighter; the batteries now come from Poland by the shortest route the company has ever had, and they are repairable. Elin sums up.

| strand | |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | sustainability, carbon footprint, emissions, circular, lifecycle, recyclable, repairable, supply chain, ESG, target, baseline, offset, stakeholder · reduce, measure, report, source |
| Grammar | comparatives and superlatives carrying the argument: *the biggest part, bigger than the factory and the batteries together, fifteen per cent lighter than, the shortest route we've ever had, the easiest to repair in its class* |
| Communication | presenting facts to non-financial stakeholders: retailers, riders, councils |
| In the Room | structuring a short presentation — the signposts that let a listener relax |

**Lead-in.** If you had to cut your own carbon footprint by a quarter, what would you change first? Look at the picture. Which of the three blocks is shipping?

**Grammar in the dialogue.**
- Shipping is **the biggest** part — **bigger than** the factory and the batteries together.
- The new frame is fifteen per cent **lighter than** last year's, so each bike travels **more cheaply** in carbon terms.
- That's **the shortest** route we've ever had, and the batteries are **more repairable**.
- Our target is a bike that's **the easiest to repair** in its class.

**In the Room — phrase box.**
- *I'll keep this to ten minutes and three points: …*
- *Feel free to stop me.*
- *First, … Second, … Which brings me to my third point: …*
- *Which surprised us.*
- *To sum up: …*
- *That's it. Questions?*

**Model dialogue.** *A retailer briefing. Daniel presents; Elin beside him; a retailer in the room.*

DANIEL: *Thanks for coming. I'll keep this to ten minutes and three points:* where our carbon comes from, what's changed, and what's next. *Feel free to stop me.*
RETAILER: Will there be numbers?
DANIEL: Three, and they're all comparisons. *First*, the footprint. Shipping is **the biggest** part — **bigger than** the factory and the batteries together.
ELIN: *Which surprised us.*
DANIEL: *Second*, what's changed. The new frame is fifteen per cent **lighter than** last year's, so a container carries **more bikes** and each bike travels **more cheaply** in carbon terms.
RETAILER: And the batteries?
DANIEL: **The most important** change. They now come from Poland, not Asia. That's **the shortest** route we've ever had, and the batteries are **more repairable** — five parts instead of one sealed block.
RETAILER: Repairable is **better than** recyclable, for us. Customers ask.
DANIEL: Agreed. *Which brings me to my third point:* next year. Our target is a bike that's **the easiest to repair** in its class. Circular, not just green.
ELIN: *To sum up:* lighter, closer, repairable.
DANIEL: *That's it. Questions?*

**The case.**
- *Speak:* Give a three-point, three-minute presentation on one thing your company or school could do about its footprint, to people who don't read spreadsheets. Signpost every move; use four comparatives or superlatives; take one question.
- *Write:* Write the one-page handout for the retailers: three headings, one comparison under each, one sentence on next year's target.

**Artwork.**
- `u05-opener` — a showroom turned into a briefing: a projected chart of three blocks of different heights with no labels, the tallest amber, a semicircle of retailers' silhouettes on stools; Daniel at the front with his helmet on the table beside a laptop, Elin at his side with her notebook
- `u05-scene` — a battery pack drawn opened into five separate parts laid out on a bench beside one sealed block, the five parts outlined amber

## Unit 6 · AI & Automation
*If we train it on three years of orders …*

**Can do:** discuss whether to adopt a new tool; make and answer predictions with the first conditional; express an opinion, disagree politely and concede a point.

**Scenario.** Elin proposes a pilot of a demand-forecasting tool. Ken is not convinced, Yusuf is keen, Daniel has used one before and knows what makes it work: a human in the loop and a small start. Ken agrees to a pilot with a written stop condition, and declines the writing tool for his own emails.

| strand | |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | machine learning, model, prompt, workflow, automation, prediction, accuracy, data set, training data, human in the loop, pilot, roll-out, drift · adopt, automate, test, trust, sign off |
| Grammar | first conditional for real consequences of a decision: *If we train it on three years of orders, it'll predict next quarter better than we do. If you keep a human in the loop, it'll work. If it fails, we'll stop it.* |
| Communication | discussing the adoption of a new tool: benefit, risk, pilot, stop condition |
| In the Room | expressing opinions and disagreeing politely, including with your boss |

**Lead-in.** What has been automated in your work in the last two years, and did anyone ask you first? Look at the picture. Who is for, and who is against?

**Grammar in the dialogue.**
- **If we train** it on three years of orders, **it'll predict** next quarter better than we do.
- **If the data is** wrong, **the prediction will be** wrong.
- **If you keep** a human in the loop, **it'll work**. **If you don't**, **it'll drift** within a quarter.
- **If it fails**, **we'll stop** it. **If it works**, **we'll roll** it out.

**In the Room — phrase box.**
- *I'm not convinced.*
- *I see it differently.*
- *Can I come in here?*
- *That's fair.*
- *I still have doubts, but I can live with …*
- *With respect, …*
- *Fair enough.*

**Model dialogue.** *A management meeting. Elin, Ken, Yusuf and Daniel.*

ELIN: Proposal: a pilot of the demand-forecasting tool. **If we train** it on three years of orders, **it'll predict** next quarter better than we do.
KEN: *I'm not convinced.* **If the data is** wrong, **the prediction will be** wrong — and we'll trust it more, because it's a computer.
YUSUF: *I see it differently.* We're guessing now. **If the model is** even ten per cent better, **we'll save** a warehouse of parked bikes.
DANIEL: *Can I come in here?* I've used one of these. **If you keep** a human in the loop, **it'll work**. **If you don't**, **it'll drift** within a quarter.
ELIN: *That's fair.* So — a human signs off every forecast?
DANIEL: For the first six months, yes. I'd also say start small. **If we pilot** it on one product line, **we'll learn** fast without betting the company.
KEN: *I still have doubts, but I can live with* a pilot. **If it fails**, **we'll stop** it. Written down, please.
YUSUF: And the writing tool for supplier emails? It drafts, we edit.
KEN: *With respect*, my emails don't need a robot.
YUSUF: *Fair enough.* Not everyone has to use it.
ELIN: Agreed, then. One pilot, one line, a human on the button. **If it works**, **we'll roll** it out.

**The case.**
- *Speak:* In fours, decide whether to adopt a tool that writes your customer emails. Each person takes a position; each uses two first conditionals; one concedes a point; the group writes down the stop condition.
- *Write:* Write the one-paragraph pilot proposal: what the tool does, what will happen if it works, what will happen if it doesn't, and who signs off.

**Artwork.**
- `u06-opener` — a meeting table with a screen at its end showing a rising forecast line, a small silhouette of a person standing between the screen and the table with one hand raised; Ken's calculator watch in the foreground, Elin's notebook, Yusuf's tripod folded, Daniel's amber helmet on the pale-wood desk behind
- `u06-scene` — a single product line on a warehouse shelf under a spotlight, the rest of the shelves in shadow, an amber tag on the lit shelf

## Unit 7 · Data & Privacy
*A small thing, and I'd rather say it now.*

**Can do:** explain what a policy requires with *must*, *have to* and *shouldn't*; give warnings and advice on data protection; give a colleague minor negative feedback without damaging the relationship.

**Scenario.** A data-protection audit finds two things: riders aren't told plainly what the app collects, and a marketing spreadsheet with customer emails sat in a shared folder. Beatriz briefs Daniel; Daniel then tells Yusuf, in a way that separates the mistake from the person.

| strand | |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | cybersecurity, breach, compliance, data protection, personal data, consent, access, encryption, policy, audit, risk, incident, plain language · comply with, restrict, delete, report, anonymise |
| Grammar | modals of obligation and prohibition, and the difference between *mustn't* and *don't have to*: *We must tell riders. It has to be a screen. That shouldn't happen. You don't have to make a thing of it. Personal data mustn't sit in an open folder.* |
| Communication | giving warnings and advice on policies |
| In the Room | delivering minor negative feedback — early, specific, and with the good work named too |

**Lead-in.** What does your phone know about you that you never agreed to tell it? Look at the picture. Which folder should not be open?

**Grammar in the dialogue.**
- We **must** tell riders what we collect, in plain language.
- It **has to** be a screen, not a paragraph. That **shouldn't** happen at all.
- You **don't have to** make a thing of it, but somebody **has to** say it.

DANIEL: Four hundred and twelve. Collection, battery swap, return. I'd like you to handle the whole chain.
CONTRACTOR: We can. **Try to give** us the addresses today and we'll start collecting Thursday.
DANIEL: On a retainer, or per job?
CONTRACTOR: Per job, with milestones: collected, swapped, returned. **Stop to think** about a retainer afterwards — most clients **go on to** book one.
DANIEL: Let's see how this goes first. Second thing. I **tried calling** the courier about weekend slots and got nowhere. Could you **try asking** — you have the relationship?
CONTRACTOR: *I can ask, but I can't promise.* They **stopped doing** weekend collections in the winter.
DANIEL: *I'd really appreciate it.* And *one more favour, if I may:* our freelance writer is doing the rider emails. Could she visit your depot on Friday to photograph the process?
CONTRACTOR: Fine by me. *Just let me know what time.*
DANIEL: *Thank you — I mean that.* I **regret to say** the budget is tight, but I **won't forget** who helped.
CONTRACTOR: *Noted.* We start Thursday.

**The case.**
- *Speak:* Delegate a task you would rather keep — a report, an event, a move — to a contractor: scope, milestones, terms, and two favours. Use five of the meaning-changing verb pairs correctly; your partner refuses one favour politely.
- *Write:* Write the statement of work: deliverables, three milestones, sign-off, and a closing paragraph that asks for one favour without making it a condition.

## Unit 15 · Future Horizons
*By June, we'll have shipped ten thousand bikes.*

**Can do:** summarise and close a major project; talk about what will have happened and what will be happening with the future perfect and future continuous; say goodbye to a colleague and turn a goodbye into a contact.

**Scenario.** The year-end all-hands. Daniel sums up the year in one line and looks ahead: ten thousand bikes by June, building in Poland, selling to cities as well as riders. Beatriz announces she is leaving for a health-tech company in Berlin. Goodbyes, a promise, and drinks on the terrace.

**Lead-in.** This time next year, what will you be doing that you aren't doing now? And what will you have finished? Look at the picture. Who is leaving?

**Model dialogue.** *The year-end all-hands. Daniel, Elin, Beatriz and Yusuf.*

DANIEL: *To sum up the year in one line:* we mapped the shop journey, we survived a strike and a recall, and we're still here. By June, **we'll have shipped** ten thousand bikes.
ELIN: And **we'll have rolled out** the new onboarding to every shop, not just the website.
DANIEL: This time next year, **we'll be building** in Poland as well as Copenhagen. Cities are changing — bike lanes, low-traffic zones, smart-city contracts. **We'll be selling** to councils, not only to riders.
ELIN: And by then, **you'll have stopped** saying "the new ops manager".
DANIEL: I hope so. Now — Beatriz has something to say.
BEATRIZ: I do. I'm leaving Larkline in March. I'm joining a health-tech company in Berlin, as Head of People. By the time I go, **we'll have hired** my replacement, and the wellbeing programme **will be running** on its own.
YUSUF: *We'll miss you.* Who's going to tell Daniel he's allowed a holiday?
BEATRIZ: You are. And *let's stay in touch — properly*, not just a message at Christmas.
DANIEL: *Count on it.* Beatriz — *it's been a privilege. Let me know when you're settled and I'll come to Berlin.*
BEATRIZ: Do that. And **you'll be sending** me the ten-thousandth bike photo, I hope.
DANIEL: *Consider it a promise.* Right — drinks are on the terrace.
