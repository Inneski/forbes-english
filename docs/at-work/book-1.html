Grammar | be in all three forms present simple

I am · you are · he / she / it is · we / they are

Form am / is / are. I am, you are, he / she / it is, we / they are. Short forms: I'm, you're, she's, we're.

Use Say who people are, what their job is, where they are from and where they are now: She's our new assistant. I'm from Hamburg. Tom is in the warehouse.

Watch out Questions put be first: Are you in the office? Negatives add not: Tom isn't here. They aren't remote.

Language point

  1. This is Lena. She's our new marketing assistant.
  2. Are you in the office? — No, I'm at home.
  3. I'm from Hamburg, in Germany.
  4. Tom isn't in the office. He's in the warehouse.

Grammar | present simple with I/you/we/they present simple

I / you / we / they + verb · don't + verb · Do you … ?

Form I / you / we / they + verb: I start, we finish, they work. Negative with don't: I don't have meetings. Questions with do: Do you work at home?

Grammar | *there is / there are present simple

There is + one thing · There are + more than one

Form There is + one thing: There's a printer. There are + more than one: There are two screens. Questions: Is there …? Are there …?

Use Say what exists and where. Use some in positive sentences: There are some cables. Use any in questions and negatives: Is there any paper? There aren't any cups.

Watch out There's is not it's. There's a printer (it exists) — It's a printer (what it is). Any goes with plurals and uncountables: any apps, any paper.

Language point

  1. Is there a light on the laptop? — Yes, there's a green light.
  2. There isn't a picture.
  3. Are there any other problems? — Yes. There are some apps I need.
  4. I don't have any passwords yet.

The rules in full

Unit 1 · People & Places

The verb be. I am, you are, he / she / it is, we are, they are. Short forms are normal in speech: I'm, you're, he's, we're, they're. Negative: I'm not, you aren't, she isn't. Question: Am I …? Are you …? Is she …? Short answers: Yes, I am. No, she isn't. Use be for names, jobs, countries and places: I'm Lena. She's an assistant. He's from Nigeria. They're in the warehouse.

Unit 2 · Work Days

Present simple, I / you / we / they. Form: subject + verb: I work, we start, they finish. Negative: don't + verb: I don't work on Fridays. Question: Do + subject + verb: Do you start at nine? What time do they finish? Use it for routines, timetables and facts. Time phrases: at + clock time, on + day, in + the morning / afternoon, from … to ….

Unit 3 · The Digital Office

There is / there are; some / any. There is (there's) with a singular or uncountable noun: There's a laptop. There's some paper. There are with a plural: There are two screens. Negative: There isn't / There aren't. Question: Is there …? Are there …? Some in positive sentences and offers; any in questions and negatives: There are some cables. Are there any cups? There aren't any.

Unit 4 · On the Move

Present simple, he / she / it. Add -s to the verb: works, leaves, takes. Add -es after -o, -s, -sh, -ch, -x: goes, does, watches. Have becomes has. Negative: doesn't + base verb: It doesn't stop. Question: Does + subject + base verb: Does it stop? Prepositions of place and movement: from, to, at, in, on, near, next to, opposite, across. Go out of the station, turn left at the corner, it's opposite the café.

Grammar reference

The rules in full

Unit 5 · Well-being

Adverbs of frequency. always (100%) · usually · often · sometimes · hardly ever · never (0%). Position: before the main verb (I usually start at nine), after be (She is always early), and between don't / doesn't and the verb (I don't often cook). Sometimes can also start a sentence: Sometimes I cook. Question: How often do you …? — Every day. Once a week. Twice a month.

Unit 6 · Company History

Past simple of be. I / he / she / it was; you / we / they were. Negative: wasn't, weren't. Question: Was he …? Were they …? Short answers: Yes, it was. No, they weren't. There was / there were for the past: There was one desk. There were two people. Years and numbers: two thousand and fifteen / twenty fifteen; nineteen ninety-nine; two hundred and forty; one thousand two hundred.

Unit 7 · Recent Events

Past simple. Regular verbs add -ed: walk → walked, like → liked, want → wanted. Irregular verbs change: take → took, meet → met, give → gave, have → had, come → came, go → went, say → said, do → did. Negative: didn't + base verb: He didn't like it. Question: Did + subject + base verb: Did you send it? Sequence: first, then, next, after that, finally.

Unit 8 · Current Projects

Present continuous. am / is / are + verb + -ing. I'm writing, you're packing, he's calling, we're waiting. Negative: isn't / aren't + -ing. Question: Are you working? What is she doing? Use it for actions happening now and for things that are temporary around now. Spelling: drop -e (come → coming); double a final consonant after one short vowel (sit → sitting).

Unit 9 · Making Choices

Comparatives. One syllable: add -er (cheaper, faster); double the last letter after one short vowel (bigger, hotter); -y → -ier (easier). Two or more syllables: more + adjective (more expensive, more difficult). Irregular: good → better, bad → worse, far → further. Than joins the two things: B is stronger than A. Make it bigger or smaller: much cheaper, a bit faster.

Unit 10 · The Green Office

Can and can't for rules and permission. can + base verb, the same for every person: I can, she can, we can. Negative: can't (cannot). Question: Can I …? Can we …? Short answers: Yes, you can. No, you can't. Can also means ability (I can drive) — see Book 2. For requests, see Unit 11: Can you …? Could you …?

Unit 11 · Simple Problems

Requests with can and could. Can you …? / Could you …? + base verb ask someone to do something; could is softer. Can I …? / Could I …? ask for permission or offer to do something yourself. Answers: Sure. Of course. Yes, one minute. I'm afraid I can't. Apologising: I'm (really) sorry about + noun; I'm sorry for + -ing / noun; I apologise for … Accepting: Don't worry about it. That's fine. No problem at all. These things happen.

Unit 12 · Arrangements

Present continuous for arrangements. am / is / are + -ing with a future time word: We're meeting at nine tomorrow. She's working in London on Friday. Are you coming on the sixth? Use it for plans that are fixed in a diary, with people, times and places. (For intentions with no fixed time, use going to — Unit 15.) Dates: on the fifth of May, on Monday the sixth, next week, next month, in the morning / afternoon.

Unit 13 · Orders & Prices

Countable and uncountable nouns. Countable: a chair / chairs, an invoice / invoices. Uncountable, no plural, no a: money, information, furniture, time, paper, advice. How many + plural countable: How many desks? How much + uncountable: How much money? How much is it? (price). Some in positive sentences and offers; any in questions and negatives.
