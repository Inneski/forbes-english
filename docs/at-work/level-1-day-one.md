# Book 1 — Day One (A1/A2)

*Surviving the modern workplace.* Read `README.md` first: the world, the cast,
the strands, the grading and the artwork spec all live there. This file is
the fifteen units.

**The company.** Greenline Office, Bristol. Thirty people. Desks, chairs and
lamps made from recycled plastic and reclaimed wood, sold online, delivered
in the company's own van. A small office above a warehouse, and one sales
rep who works from Valencia.

**The year.** Lena Hartmann arrives from Hamburg as the new marketing
assistant in Unit 1 and knows nobody. By Unit 15 she has visited a customer,
survived a broken delivery, organised a photo shoot, written the company's
history page and has a plan for next year. Sam runs the place, Tom runs the
warehouse, Ana sells from Spain, Priya fixes what breaks.

**Grading.** A1 into A2. Dialogues 70–110 words, 8–12 lines, a line under
ten words. Present simple and continuous, *can*, *would like*; past simple of
*be* from Unit 6, full past simple from Unit 7, *going to* from Unit 15.
No *will*, no perfect, no conditional, no passive. See README §5.

**Artwork accent:** coral. Lena's coral shoulder bag is in every picture she
is in. Stem and slots: README §7.

## Unit 1 · People & Places
*Who is who, and where they are.*

**Can do:** introduce yourself and a colleague on a video call; say where people are from and where they work today; spell a name and an email address so it is written down right.

**Scenario.** Lena's first morning. Sam opens the Monday video call by introducing her. Tom is in the warehouse, Ana is at home in Valencia, Priya needs Lena's surname spelled to set up her email — and gets it wrong once.

| strand | |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | *jobs:* assistant, manager, founder, sales rep, warehouse, IT support · *nationalities:* Germany/German, Spain/Spanish, the UK/British, Italy/Italian, Poland/Polish · *places:* in the office, at home, remote, on site · colleague, team, boss, surname, first name, email address |
| Grammar | *be* in all three forms — *I'm, she's, we're; isn't, aren't; Are you…? Is she…?* — and the subject pronouns |
| Communication | introducing yourself and your colleagues on a video call: name, job, country, where you are today |
| In the Room | spelling names and email addresses aloud, and checking them |

**Lead-in.** Look at the picture. Who is in the office? Who is at home? Where are you right now?

**Grammar in the dialogue.**
- This **is** Lena. She**'s** our new marketing assistant.
- **Are** you in the office? — No, I**'m** at home.
- I**'m** from Hamburg, in Germany.
- Tom **isn't** in the office. He**'s** in the warehouse.

**In the Room — phrase box.**
- *Can you spell that for me?*
- *It's H-A-R-T-M-A-N-N.*
- *Is that one N or two?* — *Two Ns. Double N.*
- *That's right.* / *No, sorry — it's …*
- *Sorry, can you say that again?*
- *It's lena dot hartmann at greenline dot co dot uk. All small letters.*

**Model dialogue.** *The Monday video call. Sam, Lena and Ana are on screen; Priya joins from the warehouse.*

SAM: Good morning, everyone. This **is** Lena. She**'s** our new marketing assistant.
LENA: Hello! I**'m** Lena. I**'m** from Hamburg, in Germany.
ANA: Hi Lena. I**'m** Ana. I**'m** in sales. Welcome!
LENA: Nice to meet you, Ana. **Are** you in the office?
ANA: No, I**'m** at home, in Valencia. In Spain. I**'m** remote.
SAM: And Tom **isn't** here — he**'s** in the warehouse. Ah, here**'s** Priya.
PRIYA: Hi Lena. I**'m** Priya, IT. *Can you spell your surname for me?*
LENA: Sure. *It's H-A-R-T-M-A-N-N.*
PRIYA: *Is that one N or two?*
LENA: *Two Ns.* Double N.
PRIYA: *Thanks.* So your email **is** lena dot hartman at greenline…
LENA: *Sorry — two Ns.* Hartmann.
PRIYA: *Two Ns. Got it. That's right now.* Welcome to Greenline!

**The case.**
- *Speak:* You are new. Introduce yourself to the team on a video call: your name, your country, your job and where you are today. Then introduce the person next to you.
- *Write:* Fill in Priya's new-starter form for yourself. Then write a two-line message to Ana: your name, your job, where you are, and your email address, written the way you would say it.

**Homework.**
- *Do:* Write five sentences about three people you work or study with: name, job, where they are from, where they are today.
- *Self-check:*
  1. I ___ from Germany.
  2. Ana ___ at home today.
  3. ___ you in the office?
  4. Tom and Sam ___ in Bristol.
  5. My email ___ lena dot hartmann at greenline dot co dot uk.
- *Key:* 1 am · 2 is · 3 Are · 4 are · 5 is

**Artwork.**
- `u01-opener` — a video call as a wall of four big windows on a screen in a small bright office; Lena with her coral bag standing in front of it, Tom in his flat cap beside her, Ana in a headset on the screen, a pale-wood desk in the foreground
- `u01-scene` — Priya with a laptop under one arm leaning over Lena's desk, Lena writing her name on a card in big letters

## Unit 2 · Work Days
*Nine to five, in two time zones.*

**Can do:** say when you start and finish work; describe your working day; find a time that works in two time zones; start and end a video call.

**Scenario.** Lena and Ana need a weekly call. Valencia is an hour ahead of Bristol, Ana has lunch at two, Lena has no meetings on Monday mornings. They test the sound, find the slot, and end the call properly.

| strand | |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | days of the week · *times:* o'clock, half past, quarter past/to, a.m., p.m. · start, finish, get up, have lunch, have a break, go home · early, late · time zone, an hour ahead / behind · meeting, call, schedule, calendar, free, busy |
| Grammar | present simple with I/you/we/they: *I start at nine; we don't have meetings; What time do you finish?* |
| Communication | talking about schedules and finding a meeting time across time zones |
| In the Room | starting and ending a video call |

**Lead-in.** What time do you start work or study? When do you have lunch? Look at the picture: what time is it in Bristol, and what time is it in Valencia?

**Grammar in the dialogue.**
- When **do you start** work?
- I **start** at nine, and I **finish** at half past five.
- We **start** early in Spain.
- I **don't have** meetings on Monday mornings.

**In the Room — phrase box.**
- *Can you hear me?* / *Can you see me?*
- *I can hear you, but I can't see you.*
- *You're on mute.*
- *Shall we start?*
- *OK, I have to go — I have another call.*
- *Thanks, Ana. Bye!* / *See you on Monday.*

**Model dialogue.** *A video call, Bristol to Valencia.*

ANA: Hi Lena! *Can you hear me?*
LENA: Yes, *I can hear you. Can you see me?*
ANA: Yes, perfect. *Shall we start?* So — when **do you start** work?
LENA: I **start** at nine, and I **finish** at half past five. What about you?
ANA: We **start** early in Spain, at eight. But it's nine here now. I'm one hour ahead.
LENA: So nine in Bristol is ten in Valencia?
ANA: Yes. And I **have** lunch at two. So I'm free from ten to one, your time.
LENA: Great. I **don't have** meetings on Monday mornings. Ten o'clock on Monday?
ANA: Ten on Monday, every week. Perfect.
LENA: *OK, I have to go — I have another call.*
ANA: *No problem. Thanks, Lena. Bye!*
LENA: *Bye! See you on Monday.*

**The case.**
- *Speak:* You and a colleague in another country need a weekly call. Say your working times, find a time on Tuesday that works for both of you, and start and end the call properly.
- *Write:* Write your working week for the team page: what time you start and finish, when you have lunch, which days you are in the office and which days you are at home.

**Homework.**
- *Do:* Write your Monday, from getting up to going home, in eight sentences with times.
- *Self-check:*
  1. What time ___ you start? (do / does)
  2. We ___ (not have) meetings on Fridays.
  3. I ___ (finish) at half past five.
  4. ___ they work at home? (Do / Does)
  5. Ana and I ___ (have) lunch at two.
- *Key:* 1 do · 2 don't have · 3 finish · 4 Do · 5 have

**Artwork.**
- `u02-opener` — two rooms side by side: a Bristol office on the left with Lena at a desk and rain on the window, a bright Valencia flat on the right with Ana in a headset and a sea horizon through her window; one large clock face on each wall, hands only, one hour apart
- `u02-scene` — Lena at a laptop with a mug, the screen showing Ana waving, morning light in stripes across the desk

## Unit 3 · The Digital Office
*Everything you need, and what to say when it stops working.*

**Can do:** name the equipment and apps on a desk; say what there is and what there isn't; ask for the things you need; report a simple tech problem and follow a simple fix.

**Scenario.** Lena's laptop arrives, and the screen freezes on day two. Priya talks her through it on the phone, then goes through what else Lena needs: apps, passwords, a second screen.

| strand | |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | laptop, screen, monitor, keyboard, mouse, charger, cable, headphones, printer · app, software, password, login, link, update · frozen, slow, broken, *it doesn't work* · cupboard, drawer, desk |
| Grammar | *there is / there are; Is there…? Are there…?; some / any* — *There are some in the cupboard. There isn't any Wi-Fi in the warehouse.* |
| Communication | asking for the things you need to do your job |
| In the Room | reporting a basic tech problem and following a simple fix |

**Lead-in.** What is on your desk right now? Name five things. Is there anything you need and don't have?

**Grammar in the dialogue.**
- **Is there** a light on the laptop? — Yes, **there's** a green light.
- **There isn't** a picture.
- **Are there any** other problems? — Yes. **There are some** apps I need.
- I don't have **any** passwords yet.

**In the Room — phrase box.**
- *My screen is frozen.* / *It's not working.* / *It doesn't work.*
- *Try this: hold the power button for ten seconds.*
- *It's working now!*
- *Anything else?*
- *Can I have …, please?*
- *One more thing.*

**Model dialogue.** *By phone. Lena at her desk, Priya in the warehouse.*

LENA: Hi Priya. *My screen is frozen.* Nothing works.
PRIYA: OK. **Is there** a light on the laptop?
LENA: Yes, **there's** a green light. But **there isn't** a picture.
PRIYA: *Try this: hold the power button for ten seconds.*
LENA: OK… one, two, three… *It's working now!* Thank you.
PRIYA: Good. **Are there any** other problems?
LENA: Yes. **There are some** apps I need. *Can I have* the design app and the chat app, please?
PRIYA: Sure. **There's** a link in your email. Do you have a password for the printer?
LENA: No, I don't have **any** passwords yet.
PRIYA: OK. Look in your email in five minutes. *Anything else?*
LENA: *One more thing.* **Is there** a second screen for my desk?
PRIYA: **There are some** in the cupboard. Ask Tom.

**The case.**
- *Speak:* You start a new job tomorrow. Tell Priya what there is on your desk and what there isn't. Ask for three things you need.
- *Write:* Write a chat message to IT: one problem with your laptop, what you tried, and one thing you need.

**Homework.**
- *Do:* Write everything on your desk with *there is* and *there are*; then a message to IT asking for two things.
- *Self-check:*
  1. ___ a printer in the office? (Is there / Are there)
  2. There ___ some cables in the drawer.
  3. There isn't ___ paper.
  4. Are there ___ headphones?
  5. There ___ a green light on the laptop.
- *Key:* 1 Is there · 2 are · 3 any · 4 any · 5 is

**Artwork.**
- `u03-opener` — an office desk seen straight on: a laptop with a blank screen, a second monitor still in its box, a coral cable in a tangle, a printer on a shelf; Priya's silhouette with a laptop under her arm arriving from the right
- `u03-scene` — Lena holding her laptop up to a phone propped on the desk so Priya on the phone screen can see it; a small light glowing on the laptop's side

## Unit 4 · On the Move
*Trains, tickets, and turn left at the café.*

**Can do:** say how someone travels to work; ask about a train and say what it costs and how long it takes; give and follow simple directions.

**Scenario.** Lena's first customer visit — a school-furniture buyer, Mr Shaw, in Birmingham. Tom knows the train and the walk from the station. Sam visits the customer every month, so Tom talks about him too.

| strand | |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | train, bus, tram, taxi, van, bike, on foot · station, platform, ticket, single, return, book, direct, change · cost, take (an hour), leave, arrive · turn left / right, go straight on, opposite, next to, near, on the corner, across the road |
| Grammar | present simple with he/she/it — *the train leaves, it takes, it doesn't stop, Does it stop?* — and prepositions of place and movement: *from, to, at, near, opposite* |
| Communication | planning a trip: train times, tickets, cost, and the way from the station |
| In the Room | giving simple directions and repeating them back |

**Lead-in.** How do you get to work or school? How long does it take? Look at the picture. Where is the café? Where is the office?

**Grammar in the dialogue.**
- The train **leaves** from Temple Meads. It **takes** about an hour and a half.
- **Does** it **stop** a lot? — No, it **doesn't**. It **goes** direct.
- A return ticket **costs** about forty pounds.
- Sam **knows** the customer. He **visits** them every month.

**In the Room — phrase box.**
- *How do I get to …?*
- *Go out of the station and turn left.*
- *Go straight on for five minutes.*
- *It's on the right, opposite a big café.*
- *Left, straight on, on the right — got it.*
- *Is it far?* — *No, ten minutes on foot.*
- *You can't miss it.*

**Model dialogue.** *In the office. Lena has her laptop open on a train website.*

LENA: Tom, I have a visit on Thursday. The customer **is** in Birmingham. *How do I get there?*
TOM: By train. It **leaves** from Temple Meads. It **takes** about an hour and a half.
LENA: **Does** it **stop** a lot?
TOM: No, it **doesn't**. It **goes** direct. A return ticket **costs** about forty pounds.
LENA: OK, I can book that. And from the station?
TOM: The office **is** near the station. *Go out of the station and turn left.*
LENA: *Turn left.*
TOM: *Then go straight on for five minutes. It's on the right, opposite a big café.*
LENA: *Left, straight on, on the right, opposite the café. Is it far?*
TOM: *No, ten minutes on foot. You can't miss it.* Sam **knows** the customer — he **visits** them every month.

**The case.**
- *Speak:* A colleague visits your town next week. Tell them how to get from the station to your office: the way, how long it takes, and one thing they can't miss.
- *Write:* Write Mr Shaw a short message: which train you take, what time it arrives, and one question about the visit.

**Homework.**
- *Do:* Write directions from the nearest station to your home or office, six lines.
- *Self-check:*
  1. The train ___ (leave) at eight.
  2. It ___ (not stop) in Bath.
  3. ___ Sam visit the customer every month? (Do / Does)
  4. A ticket ___ (cost) forty pounds.
  5. The office is ___ the café. (opposite / at)
- *Key:* 1 leaves · 2 doesn't stop · 3 Does · 4 costs · 5 opposite

**Artwork.**
- `u04-opener` — a railway station concourse with a blank departure board, Lena with her coral bag walking towards a platform, a long train beyond the barrier; on the far left, through the glass wall, Tom's van at the kerb
- `u04-scene` — a simple street map drawn as a picture: a station, a café on a corner, a dotted line of footprints turning left and going straight, one coral office door on the right

## Unit 5 · Well-being
*What you do when you are not working.*

**Can do:** talk about free time and how often you do things; make small talk before a meeting starts; show interest in what someone says.

**Scenario.** Monday, five minutes early. Sam is late to the call, so Lena and Ana talk about the weekend — running in the rain in Bristol, the beach in Valencia, who cooks and who doesn't. Sam arrives and they start.

| strand | |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | go running, go to the gym, go to the beach, go for a walk, cook, read, watch films, play football, do yoga · weekend, weekday, in the week · relax, tired, busy, healthy · always, usually, often, sometimes, hardly ever, never |
| Grammar | adverbs of frequency and where they go: *I usually go running; I'm always at the gym on Sundays; I hardly ever cook* |
| Communication | small talk before a meeting: the weekend, free time, food |
| In the Room | showing interest — the short replies that keep someone talking |

**Lead-in.** What do you usually do on Saturday morning? Look at the picture. Which of the two would you rather do this weekend?

**Grammar in the dialogue.**
- I **usually** go running on Saturdays.
- I **never** run! I **often** go to the beach.
- I'm **always** at the gym on Sundays.
- I **hardly ever** cook in the week.

**In the Room — phrase box.**
- *How was your weekend?*
- *Oh, really?*
- *That's great.* / *That sounds nice.*
- *I know what you mean.*
- *Same here.* / *Me too.* / *Me neither.*
- *Right, here's Sam. Let's start.*

**Model dialogue.** *On the Monday call, waiting for Sam.*

ANA: Morning, Lena! *How was your weekend?*
LENA: Good, thanks. I **usually** go running on Saturdays. And you?
ANA: *Oh, really?* I **never** run! I **often** go to the beach. It's five minutes from my flat.
LENA: *That sounds nice.* I'm **always** at the gym on Sundays. It's boring, but…
ANA: *I know what you mean.* Do you cook?
LENA: **Sometimes**. I **hardly ever** cook in the week. Too tired!
ANA: *Same here.* We **often** eat late in Spain. At nine or ten.
LENA: *Really? Ten?*
ANA: Yes! Oh — *here's Sam. Let's start.*

**The case.**
- *Speak:* You are early for a call with a colleague you don't know well. Talk for one minute about your weekend, ask two questions, and show interest three times.
- *Write:* Write six sentences about your week for the team's well-being board: how often you exercise, cook, relax and sleep well.

**Homework.**
- *Do:* Write eight sentences about your free time, using every adverb of frequency once.
- *Self-check:*
  1. I go running on Saturdays. (usually)
  2. She is late. (never)
  3. We eat late. (often)
  4. I am at the gym on Sundays. (always)
  5. They cook in the week. (hardly ever)
- *Key:* 1 I usually go running on Saturdays. · 2 She is never late. · 3 We often eat late. · 4 I am always at the gym on Sundays. · 5 They hardly ever cook in the week.

**Artwork.**
- `u05-opener` — one picture, two weathers: a seafront promenade in Valencia at sunrise on the right with Ana's silhouette walking, a rainy Bristol park on the left with Lena running under trees, one coral sun over the middle

## Unit 6 · Company History
*Where Greenline came from.*

**Can do:** ask and say when a company started; talk about the past with *was* and *were*; say years, prices and big numbers clearly.

**Scenario.** Lena is writing the "Our story" page for the website. Sam tells her the history: 2015, a garage, a table from a school, ninety-nine pounds, and the year everybody was at home and needed a desk.

| strand | |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | year, date, founder, history, milestone, the first office, product, customer, price, sales · garage, warehouse, shop · *numbers:* hundred, thousand, million · ago, in 2015, at first, now, today |
| Grammar | past simple of *be*: *was / were, wasn't / weren't, Was it…? Were you…?* |
| Communication | telling the story of a company: when it started, who, where, what |
| In the Room | saying numbers, prices and years so they are heard right, and checking them |

**Lead-in.** When did your company, school or town start? Look at the picture. Where was Greenline's first office?

**Grammar in the dialogue.**
- The first office **was** my garage!
- How many people **were** there? — Two.
- Everybody **was** at home.
- **Were** you at home too? — Yes, I **was** a student.

**In the Room — phrase box.**
- *Two thousand and fifteen.* / *Twenty fifteen.*
- *Nineteen ninety-nine.*
- *Ninety-nine pounds — nine, nine.*
- *Two hundred and forty.*
- *Sorry — how many?* / *Sorry, how much?*
- *So that was the big year.*

**Model dialogue.** *Sam's desk. Lena has a notebook.*

LENA: Sam, when **was** the start of Greenline?
SAM: In *two thousand and fifteen*. I **was** thirty-one. The first office **was** my garage!
LENA: *Twenty fifteen.* And how many people **were** there?
SAM: Two. Me and Tom. Tom **was** the driver — and the warehouse.
LENA: Ha! And the first product?
SAM: A desk. It **was** a table from a school. The price **was** *ninety-nine pounds*.
LENA: *Sorry — how much? Nine, nine?*
SAM: *Yes, nine, nine.* Today the same desk is *two hundred and forty*.
LENA: And *twenty twenty*?
SAM: *Twenty twenty* **was** the big year. Everybody **was** at home. Our sales **were** very big.
LENA: *So that was the big year.*
SAM: Yes. **Were** you at home too?
LENA: Yes! I **was** a student in Hamburg.

**The case.**
- *Speak:* Tell a colleague about a company you know: when it started, who the founder was, where the first office was and what the first product was. Say every year and number in words.
- *Write:* Write the "Our story" box for the Greenline website: five sentences, with at least four dates or numbers written as words.

**Homework.**
- *Do:* Write the history of your school, company or town in six sentences with *was* and *were*, every number in words.
- *Self-check:*
  1. The first office ___ a garage.
  2. There ___ two people.
  3. ___ you at home in twenty twenty?
  4. The price ___ (not) high.
  5. Tom and Sam ___ the first team.
- *Key:* 1 was · 2 were · 3 Were · 4 wasn't · 5 were

**Artwork.**
- `u06-opener` — a garage with its door up, one wooden desk inside under a bare bulb, a younger Sam in round glasses in the doorway, a small van in the drive, a coral sunset behind the roofs
- `u06-scene` — a timeline drawn as a road with three small buildings along it — a garage, a small unit, a warehouse — and one van driving from the first towards the last

## Unit 7 · Recent Events
*What happened, in order.*

**Can do:** report on a trip or a past event; use regular and irregular past forms; show the order in which things happened.

**Scenario.** The Monday after Birmingham. Lena reports to the team: the train, the walk, Mr Shaw, the chairs he liked, the catalogue he asked for. Sam asks what she did next; she has not sent the email yet.

| strand | |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | take (a train), arrive, walk, meet, visit, talk, ask, answer, like, want, give, have lunch, come back, send · catalogue, visit, meeting, trip, customer · first, then, next, after that, finally, on time, late |
| Grammar | past simple, regular (*arrived, walked, liked*) and irregular (*took, met, gave, had, came*); negatives and questions with *did* |
| Communication | reporting on a past event or trip to the team |
| In the Room | showing sequence: *first, then, after that, finally*, and asking *and then?* |

**Lead-in.** Think of a trip you made for work or study. What did you do first? Look at the picture: put the four scenes in order.

**Grammar in the dialogue.**
- I **took** the train at eight. It **arrived** on time.
- I **met** the manager, Mr Shaw.
- What **did** he **say**? — He **liked** the new chairs.
- **Did** you **give** him one? — Yes, I **did**.

**In the Room — phrase box.**
- *First, …* / *Then …* / *After that, …* / *Finally, …*
- *And then?*
- *What happened next?*
- *How was it?*
- *Not yet.*

**Model dialogue.** *The Monday call.*

SAM: So, Lena — Birmingham. *How was it?*
LENA: Good! *First*, I **took** the train at eight. It **arrived** on time.
TOM: *And then?*
LENA: *Then* I **walked** to the office. Your directions **were** perfect, Tom. I **met** the manager, Mr Shaw.
SAM: What **did** he **say**?
LENA: He **liked** the new chairs. He **asked** about prices, and he **wanted** a catalogue.
ANA: **Did** you **give** him one?
LENA: Yes, I **did**. *After that*, we **had** lunch. *Finally*, I **came** home at six.
SAM: Great work. **Did** you **send** him an email?
LENA: *Not yet.* This afternoon.
SAM: Good. That's the next step.

**The case.**
- *Speak:* Tell the team about a trip or a visit you made. Use *first, then, after that* and *finally*, and at least six past-tense verbs.
- *Write:* Write Sam a six-line report on the Birmingham visit: what you did, what Mr Shaw said, and the next step.

**Homework.**
