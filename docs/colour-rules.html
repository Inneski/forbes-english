The Colour Rules

Every value below was read out of the published decks, not remembered. Counts are marked spans on display across the whole line. Specimens sit on a dark plate because that is the only ground these inks ever appear on.

The whole system, in three sentences

1
The be that MAKES the tense wears the tense. is/are/am + -ing → present continuous pink. was/were + -ing → past continuous yellow. Same for the passive: is being pink, was being yellow.
2
The be that IS the verb wears its own tense. Brown in past simple — I was there. Slate in present simple.
3
Green is the helper that carries no meaning of its own. Do-support: do / does / did. And — still unruled — have / has / had in the perfects.

The token is the system value. The ink is a measurement.

A deck may paint a tense in a different hex, because contrast belongs to that camp’s surfaces and artwork, not to the token. Keep the published token so the TOKENS gate holds the line together; add --<tense>-ink beside it, derived mechanically — L* raised in Lab with hue and chroma held — until the ratio measured off the render clears 5:1.

Never measure a colour against an opaque surface. Every colour defect this system has shipped was invisible to a token-against-token check, because the cards are translucent and sit on artwork.

The standing rules

  • Always split. The negator comes out of the contraction — is + n’t. won’t is the one exception: it splits into “wo”, which is not a word.
  • A question splits the chain, it does not change it. Is … raining keeps the colours it had in the statement.
  • going to splits three ways — am/are/is blue, going to lime, infinitive gold. Splitting the be shows how a question inverts, and leaves room for was/were going to.
  • was/were are never brown in a past continuous sentence. is/are are never blue in a present continuous sentence.
  • A negator is never anything but magenta. The NEGCOLOUR gate found eleven wearing .aux or .modal.
  • Headings run in the aux green — eyebrow, para-head, card lead-ins — on the eight descent decks, to set them apart from the station’s own tense.
  • A struck-through error stays --text-dim, auxiliary or not.
  • Never hardcode rgba(0,0,0,…) or rgba(255,255,255,…). Use the theme primitives.
  • Palettes derive mechanically from the hero. Never hand-pick; extract-palette.py does it and every row must PASS.
  • Learner-facing text never mentions a previous version of the lesson. That belongs in the builder docstring and the commit message.

one decision, not yet made

Does the perfect’s have go turquoise?

If the continuous’s be is half the tense, then has been and have seen work the same way — and by rule 1 the have should wear the tense, not the helper green. That is roughly 148 spans across six decks, and it is the last green that is not do-support.

Decide it once, not one deck at a time.

And the be inside an illustrative sentence?

Not a taught form — just a sentence that happens to contain one. 33 cases; check-unmarked.py --review lists them. It is a teaching call, not a defect, and until it is made the same report keeps coming back one slide at a time.
