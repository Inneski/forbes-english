# Brief for ChatGPT — the pictures for The Climb, from your character deck

**How to use this file.** Start a new ChatGPT chat. Attach your character
deck (the sheet with the team on it), then paste everything between the two
rules below as the first message. ChatGPT makes scene 1; type **next** for
each scene after that. Fifteen scenes, then (optionally) five portraits.

Save each picture into **`incoming/sherpa-climb/`** as you go, named
`camp-01.png` … `camp-13.png`, `summit.png`, `summit-top.png` (and
`cast-tensing.png` … if you make portraits). Other names are fine, since
Claude can sort them by eye, but these save a step. Then tell a local Claude
session: *"The Climb pictures are in incoming/sherpa-climb — put them in."*
The steps for that session are at the bottom of this file.

**Check the deck against the cast before you start.** All 117 lines of the
game are written for these five people, and the pronouns are fixed:
**Tensing** (the guide), **Myra** (she, the planner), **Otto** (he, the old
hand, the oldest), **Sam** (he, young, first big climb) and **Navya** (she,
base-camp radio), plus **Momo**, a yak. If your deck has different names, a
different number of people, or Sam or Otto as a woman, tell Claude first:
the lines can change, and it is quicker before the pictures exist than after.

**Why the pictures follow the lines.** Every camp's picture sits behind that
camp's eight lines, so the picture should show what the lines are about:
camp six's lines are the storm on the ridge, so camp six's picture is the
team roped together in the hail. The scene list below is taken from the
game's own lines (`lesson-template/build/sherpa-climb/content.py`).

---

I'm making the pictures for an English-learning game called **The Climb**. A
small team climbs a mountain in thirteen camps, then makes a summit push.
The attached sheet shows the team. Please make **fifteen scenes**, one at a
time: make scene 1 now, and the next one each time I say "next".

**The team (from the attached sheet).** Keep every person exactly as drawn
on the sheet in every scene: the same face, hair, build, clothes, pack and
colours. Nobody changes outfit during the climb, except for adding a hat,
goggles or a head torch higher up.
- **Tensing**: the mountain guide. Calm, experienced, always at the front.
- **Myra**: the planner. Brisk; carries the map and the schedule.
- **Otto**: the old hand. The oldest; has climbed for forty years.
- **Sam**: young, on his first big climb. Keen, sometimes exhausted.
- **Navya**: runs the radio at base camp. She stays at the bottom and is
  never on the mountain.
- **Momo**: a yak who lives at base camp with Navya. Never on the mountain.

**The style for every scene.** Match the attached sheet's drawing style.
The palette is soft pink and slate blue with snow white and dark rock: dawn
and dusk light, pink-lit snow, pale blue skies. Use a flat, matte, painted
look with a little paper grain, and simple shapes rather than photographic
detail. Keep it calm, never gloomy. No text, no letters, no logos and no
watermark in any picture.

**The frame for every scene: landscape, 1536 × 1024.**
- **Put the people and the action in ONE half** (left or right; the scene
  says which). Keep the other half calm: sky, snow, mist or open water. In
  the game a text card covers most of that calm half.
- Keep faces and anything important out of the top and bottom tenth: the
  game trims a little off both on wide screens.
- People should read clearly at phone size. Make them a good size in their
  half of the frame, not specks, except where a scene says "small" or "far".

**The fifteen scenes.**

1. **Camp one, the lake at dawn (day one).** The sun is coming up over a
   still mountain lake. Otto and Myra are cooking breakfast on a small stove
   by the tents; Sam is tying his boots; Tensing stands holding a radio to
   one ear, listening to the weather report. The team is on the LEFT; the lake and
   the rising sun fill the right.
2. **Camp two, the foot of the rock.** Early morning at the base of a huge
   granite cliff above a meadow. Tensing stands alone on a boulder, looking
   up at the sky; further back the tents are still closed, and the others
   are asleep. Tensing on the RIGHT; the open meadow and haze on the left.
3. **Camp three, evening after a long day.** A campsite on a grassy shoulder,
   the trail behind them. Sam sits on a rock writing in his diary; an eagle
   flies overhead; Otto, behind him, is already half asleep against his pack.
   People on the LEFT; the valley and the evening sky on the right.
4. **Camp four, the rope.** A steeper slope with a small lower camp. A single
   fixed rope runs from the camp up the slope; Otto is small, high up the
   rope, going to look at the ice; Sam and Myra watch from the tents. A small
   pile of stones (a cairn Otto built two years ago) stands near the tents.
   Action on the RIGHT; the calm slope and sky on the left.
5. **Camp five, the plan.** A high meadow with a ridge ahead and dark clouds
   gathering beyond it. Myra holds out a map and points at the ridge; Sam
   leans in to look; Otto unpacks a big bag of food for dinner. People on the
   LEFT; the ridge and the gathering weather on the right.
6. **Camp six, the storm on the ridge.** A narrow rocky ridge in sudden
   slanting hail. The four climbers are roped together, heads down; Sam
   grips the rope; a small rock tumbles past just below him. The team on the
   RIGHT; the storm cloud on the left. Dramatic, but not frightening.
7. **Camp seven, the weather you can't see yet.** A rocky camp under a huge
   wall of cloud building behind the mountain. Otto takes a heavy pack from
   Sam's shoulders to carry it for him; Tensing studies the sky. People on
   the LEFT; the wall of cloud on the right.
8. **Camp eight, the long wet day.** Wet snow and meltwater beside a glacier
   stream. A line of fresh boot tracks leads back down the path; the team,
   tired and soaked, trudges on; Otto is singing, mouth open, while Sam
   covers his ears. Team on the RIGHT, the tracks leading in from the left.
9. **Camp nine, the ledge at dusk.** One small tent on a rock ledge, glowing
   from inside; beyond it the glacier they will cross tomorrow, lit pink by
   the last light. Sam, outside the tent, looks across at it. Tent and Sam on
   the LEFT; the glacier on the right.
10. **Camp ten, steps in the ice.** A steep ice slope where another team has
    already cut a staircase of steps. The team stands at the bottom looking
    up at the steps; far off, a small stone hut with its door shut. People on
    the RIGHT; the ice and the far hut on the left.
11. **Camp eleven, the hut at last.** Twilight on deep snow. A line of
    footprints leads to a small stone hut; Tensing has just got the door
    open and warm light spills out; the others, shivering and snowy, crowd
    in behind. The hut and the team on the LEFT; the snowfield on the
    right.
12. **Camp twelve, the cairn.** A tall stone cairn on a snowy plateau below
    the summit, pale clear sky. Myra checks her watch and points up; the
    others rest against the cairn. Cairn and team on the RIGHT; the sky on
    the left.
13. **Camp thirteen, four in the morning.** Night, stars, a long snowfield
    rising to the summit. A line of four head torches climbs it in single
    file, small and far. The line of lights on the LEFT; the dark snow and
    stars on the right.
14. **The summit push, sunrise.** The last narrow snow ridge to the top, the
    sun coming up. The four climbers, one behind the other, nearly there.
    The team on the RIGHT; the sunrise and the sea of cloud on the left.
15. **The summit.** All four on the very top, seen from behind, arms raised
    or arms round each other, looking down at the whole route: the camps are
    tiny below, with clouds in between. Centre-right; the view fills the
    rest.

That is all fifteen. If I then ask for **portraits**, make one per person
(Tensing, Myra, Otto, Sam, Navya), portrait format 1024 × 1536: head and
shoulders to the waist, plain pale background, the same light for all
five, each exactly as on the sheet. Give Navya her radio handset and a mug
of coffee.

## For the Claude session that receives the pictures

Read `docs/ARTWORK-sherpa-climb.md` first. It holds the file names, what
replaces what, and how the game picks each picture up with no code change.

1. **Sort.** Look at every file in `incoming/sherpa-climb/` and name them by
   scene, using the list above (camp-01 … camp-13, summit, summit-top,
   cast-<who>). Check each picture's people against the deck. In particular,
   are Navya and Momo off the mountain? Are the people in the half the scene
   asked for?
2. **Scenes:** `py tools/prep-artwork.py incoming/sherpa-climb/<file>
   --into SherpaClimb --names camp-06 --width 1600 --quality 82`. It flags a
   3:2 source as "not 16:9": expected and harmless, because the game crops
   with `object-fit: cover`. It refuses a duplicate of an existing repo
   image, which is right. Then make the 960 px `-sm` copy (the one-liner in
   `docs/ARTWORK-sherpa-climb.md`). `summit-top` goes in the same way.
3. **Portraits:** `--width 800 --names cast-ana` etc. Without separate
   portraits, crop each person from the deck instead (4:5, head to waist,
   about 800 × 1000) into `SherpaClimb/cast-<who>.jpg`.
4. **Build and check:** `py lesson-template/build/sherpa-climb/build.py`
   measures each new scene's quieter half and moves the card. Then run
   `node lesson-template/build/sherpa-climb/test_climb.js` (the no-scroll
   gate) and look at a screenshot of every camp. If the card covers a face,
   set `'side'` on that camp in `content.py`.
5. **Commit** the new `SherpaClimb/` files by name together with the page.
   Commit the old landscapes' replacements in the same commit, with the
   old-to-new mapping in the message.
