# Grammar page: improvement plan

Innes, 2026-11-20: "create a plan for improving this page". The page is
forbesenglish.com/grammar (`grammar.html`). It is generated by
`tools/build_grammar_hub.py`, with the chart rack from `tools/grammar_maps.py`
and the topics from `tools/topics.py`; never edit `grammar.html` by hand.

## What it is now (measured live, 2026-11-20)

| | 1920 × 1080 | Phone (Pixel 7) |
|---|---|---|
| Page length | 9,036 px = 8.4 screens | 18.6 screens |
| Topic shelves start at | screen 3.9 | screen 6.4 |
| Topic shelves take | 3,147 px | 9.7 screens |
| Grammar maps take | 1,802 px | 2.8 screens |
| First-load weight | 2.0 MB, 51 requests (images 1.6 MB; three.js 196 KB; hero.jpg 543 KB) | same, plus a WebGL canvas |
| Load event | 3.4 s | 0.9 s |

The order is:

1. hero: the 3D relief of the Gerundia chart;
2. Grammar maps: a rack of 25 rolls beside one open chart;
3. Tense code: twelve coloured squares, plus going to;
4. Shelves: 19 topic cards in three groups (Tenses, Grammar, Skills);
5. Two routes: Sherpa Tensing and Block Camp;
6. two notes;
7. the Level Check close.

The page has no footer, and nor do index, library, ielts or block-camp.

### What works

- The tense code. It is the clearest thing on the page, and it ties every lesson to the Sherpa colours.
- The rack idea, and the charts themselves.
- The counts in the hero (345 lessons, 19 topics, 90 free) and the "free first" promise.

### What does not

1. **The page answers its own headline late.** The H1 is "English grammar, by
   topic", but the topics start 3.9 screens down on a desktop and 6.4 on a
   phone. Before them come the maps, whose names are places rather than grammar
   points, and the tense code. A learner who arrives from Google for "passive
   voice" has to scroll past 25 fanciful names to find it.
2. **The rack is a list in the order the charts were built.**
   - The tags name places (*The Bridges*, *Statia & Actia*, *The Backshift
     Current*), not grammar points. Only the open sheet says what a chart
     teaches.
   - One tag is cut short: "The -ER Coast & the MORE…".
   - At 1920 the open chart and its words fill about 450 px, while the rack
     runs about 1,150 px. That leaves roughly 700 px of empty column beside the
     lower rolls.
3. **The hero copy sits on busy lettering.** Map names ("Try Tarn", "Remember
   Rock", "Go-On Gill") show through the paragraph and the stats. The halo
   softens this but does not remove it.
4. **The hero is heavy for what it does.**
   - three.js and a 543 KB JPEG load on every visit, phones included, for a
     relief the reader scrolls past.
   - The whole page is 2.0 MB before anyone taps anything.
5. **Two topic cards share a picture with another card.** Future Tenses has
   the same image as Tense Review and Grammar Tests, and Conditionals the same
   as Business English (`grammar-hub/*-1b321f2e.jpg`, `*-17f226f3.jpg`).
6. **The shelf pictures are four styles at once:** voxel game art, pixel
   posters, film-poster stills and a diagram screenshot (Used To). On a phone
   each card is a full-width picture, which is most of the 9.7 screens.
7. **"Skills" is not grammar.** Business English, IELTS Academic and
   Vocabulary (186 lessons) make the grammar page longer without serving its
   reader.
8. **One thing appears twice.** Tense review shows as a card on the Tenses
   shelf and again as the black "All of them at once" bar under the tense code.
9. **No way to search.** The page claims 345 lessons but offers no filter; the
   library's filters are only linked at the very bottom.
10. **On a phone the top bar shows only Library, the language and Go Pro.**
    Check that RPGs, Level Check and the rest are reachable from it on this page.

## The plan

The phases are in order of value per hour. Each ends in something shippable.

### Phase 1: lead with the promise (one session)

1. **A topic index straight under the hero.**
   - It holds the 19 topics as compact chips in three rows (Tenses, Grammar,
     Beyond grammar), each with its colour swatch, lesson count and free count.
   - It fits one screen on a desktop and about 1.5 on a phone, and each chip
     links to its topic page.
   - The data is already in `topics.py` and the catalogue.
2. **A filter box on the index.** Typing narrows the chips, and the chart rack
   with them ("passive" leaves Passive Voice and the Passive Ferry). It is
   client-side, with no new data.
3. **New section order:**
   1. hero;
   2. topic index;
   3. tense code;
   4. grammar maps;
   5. full shelves;
   6. routes;
   7. close.
4. **The primary hero button becomes "Find your grammar point ↓",** pointing at
   the index. "Find your level" stays as the second button.
5. **Quick fixes:**
   - the two duplicate pictures (item 5);
   - one of the two tense-review entries (item 8);
   - the truncated tag.
   - Add a check to `build_grammar_hub.py` that refuses the same picture on two cards.

### Phase 2: make the rack a guide (one session)

6. **Group the rolls by grammar area,** with a small heading over each group:
   - Tenses and time: Regularia, Finished & Open Time, Statia & Actia, the Passive Ferry, the Backshift Current, the Conditional Quadrants, Sherpa Tensing.
   - Verb patterns: Gerundia, the Particle Ferries, Makeland & Doland.
   - Nouns and articles: Countania, the Atlas of THE.
   - Adjectives and adverbs: the Adjective Canal, Soundings of Frequency, the -ER & MORE Coasts.
   - Prepositions: the Harbour of Scale, the Voyage.
   - Modals and certainty: the Grammar Forecast, Soundings of Certainty.
   - Sentences: the Return Voyage, the Inversion Maelstrom, the Bridges.
   - Words and register: the Prefix Archipelago, the Heights of Register, the Atlantic.

   The groups live in `grammar_maps.py`, as a field on each map.
7. **Each tag names its grammar point** under the place name ("The Bridges ·
   linking words"), keeping the level.
8. **Use the empty column at 1920.**
   - The open chart becomes sticky, staying beside the rack as the reader moves
     down it.
   - Under it go two or three lessons from the same topic, free ones first.
9. **Cross-link both ways:** each topic page links its chart or charts, and each
   chart's sheet links its topic page.

### Phase 3: a lighter, more legible hero (half a session)

10. **Lay the copy on a parchment panel,** the card colour at about 85% with a
    blur, the same treatment as the chart pages. Do not lighten the whole map.
11. **The 3D relief on large screens only.**
    - Phones, `prefers-reduced-motion` and Save-Data get the still picture.
    - three.js loads after first paint.
    - `hero.jpg` is served as WebP at 1920 and 1080 widths.
    - Targets: under 1 MB first load on a phone, and the load event under 2 s on a desktop.

### Phase 4: shelves that read as one set (needs Innes's call on pictures)

12. **On phones, the shelves become compact rows:** swatch, name, counts and
    free. They drop from 9.7 screens to about 4. Desktop keeps the cards.
13. **Move "Skills" off the grammar shelves** into one line of links: "Beyond
    grammar: Business English · IELTS · Vocabulary".
14. **Pictures on desktop: one style for all 19.** Either each topic's sea
    chart or tense colour, or a matched set made for the shelves. This is a
    HOUSE-STYLE §14 call (art direction), so ask with both versions rendered.

### Not this page, but found here

- No footer on any main page (index, library, ielts, block-camp, grammar): no
  contact, legal or sitemap links. Worth one site-wide pass.

## How to check each phase

Measure live at 1920 and on a phone (Pixel 7), with
`scratchpad/audit.js`-style captures:

- page length in screens;
- where the topic index starts;
- first-load KB;
- no duplicate pictures;
- every chip, roll and card hit by `elementFromPoint`.

Then run `py tools/build_hubs.py`, then `py tools/seo.py`, and push. Check that
GitHub's check runs show a "Workers Builds: forbes-english" run; a Pages
success alone does not update the live site.
