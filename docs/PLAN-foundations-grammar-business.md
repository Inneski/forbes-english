# Plan — A2 Foundations, B1–B2 Grammar, and the Business collection

Written 2026-09-08 from `docs/SYLLABUS-GAPS.md`. Three series, 22 lessons
and one hub page. This file is the brief for the sessions that build them.
The Midjourney shopping list is at the bottom; Innes generates the pictures
and drops them in `incoming/`, then `/publish` does the rest.

## Rules that apply to every lesson here

- **Read `CLAUDE.md`, then `lesson-template/HOUSE-STYLE.md`, then
  `docs/HANDOFF.md` before building anything.** Every deck is generated by a
  builder in `lesson-template/build/`; copy the nearest recent one
  (`build_flow.py` + `i18n_flow.py` is the cleanest small pair,
  `build_nature2.py` the cleanest large one). `deck.py` and `chrome_i18n.py`
  are shared — use them.
- **Format: a 16:9 deck, light theme.** The whole family is
  `data-theme="light"`; the palette comes from `extract-palette.py <hero>
  --light` and every row must PASS. Slide budget is HOUSE-STYLE §6: ~55
  words a slide, nothing scrolls, split rather than shrink.
- **Languages: `langs=('en', 'de', 'es')`**, teach cards in the six-item
  form. Spanish is a floor, not an option.
- **The activation stage is required** (HOUSE-STYLE §10b): a speaking task
  and a writing task that use the point, not a quiz about it.
- **Answer keys:** never the only longest or the only shortest option, never
  first by default, never in a different grammatical shape from the
  distractors (`check-lesson.js` measures length; shape it does not).
- **Pipeline, always in this order:** palette → builder → `check-lesson.js`
  clean → catalogue row → `tools/seo.py` last. New rows get `sort_order`
  null; the shelf sorts newest first on its own.
- **Titles carry the category.** `library.html` derives categories from the
  title and filename by regex. Series A and B need a new category — see
  "Library changes" — and every Series C title must contain the word
  *Business* so it lands in the Business collection without a regex edit.
- **Learner-facing text never mentions the old version of anything**, and
  nothing here is a rebuild, so there is no old version to mention.
- **Level is the level of the grammar, not of the story.** An A2 lesson is
  told in A2 English. If a sentence needs the point being taught to be
  understood, it is on the wrong slide.

## Library changes (one session, before or with the first lesson)

1. Add a **`Foundations`** category to `detectCategories()` in
   `library.html`: `if (/^foundations-/.test(item.file)) cats.push('Foundations');`
   and add it to `categories`, `catLabels` and `COLLECTIONS` with the hero
   `foundations-articles.html` and the blurb *"The A2 grammar under
   everything else: articles, quantities, comparisons, frequency, ability.
   One point per lesson, told in A2 English."*
2. Add an **`Exam Grammar`** category the same way on the file prefix
   `exam-grammar-`, with the hero `exam-grammar-reported-speech.html` and
   the blurb *"The B1–B2 grammar the exams test: reported speech, relative
   clauses, verb patterns, question tags, register."* The existing topical
   categories still apply on top (Reported Speech exists and is empty;
   the B1 lesson fills it by title).
3. When the Business hub exists, give the Business collection
   `hub: 'business.html'` so the header offers it.
4. `check-library.js --vs-origin` before every push that touches
   `library.html`. Re-read the file from `origin/main` first.

## Series A — A2 Foundations (7 lessons)

Files `foundations-<point>.html`, folders `Foundations/<point>/`. Titles
`Foundations — <Point>: <hook>`. All A2, all Pro except the first, which is
free so the series has a door.

| # | File | Title | Scope | The trap for DE / ES speakers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | `foundations-articles.html` | Foundations — Articles: A, An, The, or Nothing | a/an with sounds not letters; the for known and unique; zero article with plurals, uncountables, meals, languages, most place names | DE: *the* before languages and abstract nouns (*the life is hard*); ES: *the* before generalisations (*the dogs are loyal*), *a* before professions dropped (*she is teacher*) |
| A2 | `foundations-countables.html` | Foundations — Countable & Uncountable: Some, Any, a Piece Of | count vs mass; some/any/no; a piece/bottle/slice of; plurals that are not (*information, advice, furniture, news*) | DE: *informations*, *advices*; ES: *furnitures*, *a bread* |
| A3 | `foundations-quantifiers.html` | Foundations — Much, Many, Few, Little, Plenty | much/many, a lot of, (a) few / (a) little and the meaning of the article, too much/many, enough, plenty | both: *much people*; DE: *a little* vs *little* |
| A4 | `foundations-comparatives.html` | Foundations — Bigger, Better, the Best | -er/-est vs more/most, irregulars, *than*, *as … as*, *the same as*, *much/far/a bit* + comparative | DE: *more big*, *as good like*; ES: *more better*, *the most big* |
| A5 | `foundations-frequency.html` | Foundations — How Often? Adverbs of Frequency | always → never scale; position with *be* and with other verbs; *every day* vs *everyday*; *once a week*; questions with *how often* | DE: adverb before *be* (*I always am*); ES: adverb after verb (*I go always*) |
| A6 | `foundations-can-could-may.html` | Foundations — Can, Could, May: Ability, Permission, Requests | can/can't ability now, could/couldn't past, *be able to* for other tenses, can/could/may for permission and requests with politeness scale | DE: *I can English*; ES: *I can to swim*, *can you to help* |
| A7 | `foundations-say-tell.html` | Foundations — Say, Tell, Speak, Talk | tell + person, say + words, say to; speak a language / speak to; talk about; the fixed pairs (*tell the truth, tell a story, say sorry*) | both: *he said me*, *tell to him*; ES: *speak about* vs *talk about* |

Deck shape for the series, so the seven feel like one thing:

1. Cover. 2. Where you meet it (one scene, four sentences with the point
in bold). 3–5. The rule in three cards. 6. Spot the mistake (six items,
the L1 trap in every one). 7–9. Gap-fill in the scene. 10. Sort (two
bins). 11. Activation: speak — describe the picture using the point four
times; write — a short message that needs it. 12. Results.
About 20 slides once the budget has its way.

## Series B — B1–B2 Exam Grammar (8 lessons)

Files `exam-grammar-<point>.html`, folders `ExamGrammar/<point>/`. Titles
`<Point>: <hook> (B1)` or `(B2)`. These are the points a Cambridge or IELTS
candidate is tested on and asks for by name.

| # | File | Title | Level | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | `exam-grammar-reported-speech.html` | Reported Speech: What She Said (B1) | B1 | say/tell, backshift table, pronouns and time words (*today → that day*), reported questions with *if/whether* and word order, when not to backshift |
| B2 | `exam-grammar-reporting-verbs.html` | Reporting Verbs: Suggest, Deny, Admit, Refuse (B2) | B2 | verb + -ing (*deny, admit, suggest*), verb + to (*refuse, offer, promise*), verb + object + to (*advise, warn, remind*), verb + that; the five that take two patterns |
| B3 | `exam-grammar-relative-clauses.html` | Relative Clauses: The One Who, the Place Where (B1) | B1 | who/which/that/whose/where/when; when *that* can replace; when the pronoun can be dropped (object relatives); prepositions at the end |
| B4 | `exam-grammar-non-defining.html` | Non-Defining Relative Clauses: The Comma Changes Everything (B2) | B2 | the comma test, no *that*, no dropping, *which* referring to a whole clause, *whom* and *of which* in writing |
| B5 | `exam-grammar-verb-patterns.html` | Verb Patterns: Stop To, Stop Doing (B2) | B2 | the meaning-changing pairs: *stop, remember, forget, try, regret, go on, mean, need*; *like doing* vs *would like to do*; *-ing* after prepositions |
| B6 | `exam-grammar-question-tags.html` | Question Tags: It's Cold, Isn't It? (B1) | B1 | positive → negative, negative → positive; auxiliary choice; *I'm … aren't I*; *let's … shall we*; imperatives; intonation and what it does to meaning |
| B7 | `exam-grammar-indirect-questions.html` | Indirect Questions: Could You Tell Me Where…? (B1) | B1 | *Could you tell me / Do you know / I wonder* + statement word order; *if/whether* for yes-no; no *do*; the politeness it buys |
| B8 | `exam-grammar-register.html` | Register: Formal, Neutral, Informal (B2) | B2 | the same message three ways; contractions, phrasal verbs vs Latinate verbs, *I'd be grateful if* vs *can you*, passives and nominalisation for distance, when formal is wrong |

Deck shape: cover; the point in a real text (an email, a message thread, a
short article) before any rule; the rule in cards; transform exercises
(direct → reported, two sentences → one relative clause, formal ↔ informal);
error correction; activation. B1 and B8 want a real email as the running
text; B6 and B7 want dialogue.

lessons that were never designed as a route. This series adds the missing
rungs, then `business.html` puts old and new in order, the way `ielts.html`
does for IELTS.

starts with **Business English —** so the regex shelves it. A2/B1 first,

| C2 | `business-emails-1.html` | Business English — Emails 1: Subject Line to Sign-Off (B1) | B1 | the five parts; greetings by relationship; *I am writing to* / *just a quick note*; requests and attachments; closing lines; the register ladder |
| C3 | `business-emails-2.html` | Business English — Emails 2: Bad News, Chasing and Saying No (B2) | B2 | softening (*I'm afraid, unfortunately, it seems*), chasing without accusing, declining with an alternative, apologising once, tone repair |

| C5 | `business-meetings.html` | Business English — Meetings: Agenda, Opinion, Interruption, Action (B2) | B2 | opening and the agenda; giving and asking for opinions; agreeing and disagreeing at three strengths; interrupting and holding the floor; summarising and action points |
| C6 | `business-interviews.html` | Business English — Job Interviews: Tell Me About Yourself (B2) | B2 | the six standard questions and the shape of a good answer; STAR for competency questions; strengths and weaknesses without clichés; questions to ask; follow-up email |
| C7 | `business-complaints.html` | Business English — Complaints: Making One and Handling One (B1) | B1 | both sides in one lesson: stating the problem, what you want, escalating politely; acknowledging, apologising, offering, closing; the phrases that inflame |

- **Start here (B1):** small talk → emails 1 → phone calls → complaints
- **Working (B2):** meetings → emails 2 → interviews → *Feedback That
  Lands* → *Dailies Review* (both existing)
- **Leading (C1):** *C1 Negotiation Language* → *Negotiations & Handling
  Objections* → *Escalating a Complaint* → *Decision-Making Under
  Uncertainty* → *FireShield* (all existing)

Front page: a fourth feature panel beside IELTS, Sherpa Tensing and Block
Camp, once the hub exists.
