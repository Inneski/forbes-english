Finished Time & Open Time

I saw her yesterday, but I have seen her today. The action is the same. What changes is the time: yesterday is over, and today is still open. Finished time takes the past simple; open time takes the present perfect. Read the time word, find its shore, and the tense is decided.

Reading the two shores

Is the time over?

Do not ask how long ago it happened. Ask whether the time you are talking about has finished. If it has, you are on the west shore.

Finished: a named past time

Yesterday, last week, in 2019, on Monday, at six: the time is over.

I saw her yesterday.

Finished: ago

Ago counts back from now to a finished point.

He called two days ago.

Finished: when + a past time

A when clause about the past closes the time.

I learned to sail when I was ten.

Open: your life so far

Ever and never mean at any time up to now.

Have you ever sailed?

Open: up to now

Yet, already, so far, recently, just: the time reaches this moment.

We have already eaten.

Open: since + a starting point

Since names where the time began; it runs to now.

They have lived here since 2019.

Twofold Isle, the Shallows and the False Cape

Where the situation decides

Two time words can be finished or open. The answer is not in the word: it is in what is true now.

Twofold Isle

Read each row as a pair.

Time wordFinishedOpen
for five yearsI lived in Rome for five years. (I left)I have lived in Rome for five years. (I still do)
this morningI saw her this morning. (it is now afternoon)I have seen her this morning. (it is still morning)
todayI called her today. (the working day is over)I have called her today. (the day is still going)
this weekWe met this week. (and will not again)We have met twice this week. (so far)

I lived in Rome for five years, and I have lived here for two.
