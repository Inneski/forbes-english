Past Perfect

The Past Perfect is the past of the past. When a story is already in the Past Simple and you need to reach further back, had + past participle does it: When we arrived, the film had started. The arriving is past; the starting is earlier still.

It is only needed when the order matters and is not obvious. I had breakfast and left is fine in the Past Simple because the order is clear. By the time I got there, she had left needs the Past Perfect because the two events are being compared.

Signals: by the time, before, after, already, until, as soon as, and reported thoughts and speech (He said he had seen it). The continuous form, had been doing, gives the earlier action duration: She had been waiting for an hour when the bus came.

The mistake is overuse: putting everything old in the Past Perfect. It marks the earlier of two events, and once that is established the story drops back into the Past Simple.
