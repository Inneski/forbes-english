Useful Language

  • From an aesthetic sensibility standpoint, the film demands...

  • 02The Nietzsche-Wagner relationship is the most dramatically rich material in the story. How do you prevent it from becoming a conventional bromance-to-rivalry narrative? What can the form do that the content cannot?

  • 05Who do you cast as Nietzsche, and what does that casting decision say about your interpretation of the man?
Section 7 · Input Task

Reading: The Impossible Philosopher

Read the extract, then answer the comprehension questions. scored

When the German director Werner Herzog was once asked whether he would ever make a film about Friedrich Nietzsche, he reportedly replied: "To dramatise philosophy, you must first decide whether you are serving the ideas or the man. With Nietzsche, the two are almost impossible to separate — and that, precisely, is the problem." The remark captures a dilemma that has deterred even the most ambitious filmmakers. Nietzsche is not merely a biographical subject; he is a style, a provocation, a way of seeing.

A straightforward biopic risks becoming hagiography — an uncritical celebration of a tormented genius who strode across the Alps composing aphorisms and denouncing God. Yet a film that dwells too insistently on his madness risks something equally distorting: the reduction of a complex thinker to a cautionary tale about intellectual excess. The challenge is to grapple with a man who was simultaneously ahead of his time and catastrophically misunderstood by it.

Any honest film must contend with the figure of his sister, Elisabeth Forster-Nietzsche, who systematically misappropriated his work following his collapse in 1889. Her editorial interventions sanitised, distorted, and eventually aligned his philosophy with a nationalism he would have despised. A director who ignores this story sanitises it in a different way — by pretending that ideas exist in pure form, untouched by the forces that interpret and transmit them.

Perhaps the most compelling cinematic approach would be one that embraces perspectivism as a structural principle — not just a theme. Such a film might deliberately show Nietzsche through multiple incompatible lenses: the tender friend, the ruthless critic, the solitary wanderer, the man dissolving into silence. Not a single truth, but a collision of truths. Not biography, but biography as a philosophical act.

Section 9 · Deeper Discussion

Going Further

Discuss or reflect on these higher-level questions. Draw on the article and your own professional experience.

  • 01The article says a conventional biopic risks becoming hagiography. Do you think that is inevitable — or is hagiography a failure of directorial nerve rather than a structural problem with the genre?
  • 02Herzog's remark implies that serving the ideas and serving the man are in tension. Is that tension unique to philosophical biography, or does it apply to any film about a real person?
  • 03The article proposes perspectivism as a structural principle, not just a theme. Think of films that make their form enact their argument. What makes that work? What are the risks?
  • 04Elisabeth's interventions mean any film about Nietzsche is also, inevitably, about the politics of interpretation. Is that a problem to solve or a subject to explore?
  • 05"Not biography, but biography as a philosophical act." Could that be your elevator pitch? What would it mean for the film you want to make?
Section 10 · Final Productive Task

The Pitch & The Statement

Speaking Task — The Pitch

You are pitching your Nietzsche film to a panel of European co-production partners. Two minutes. They have heard three other pitches today. Make yours the one they remember.

What to cover
  • Genre, tone, and structural approach — and why it is right for this material
  • Which period of his life, and what that choice reveals about your interpretation
  • How you represent the ideas on screen without dramatising them into cliche
  • How you handle Elisabeth and the question of misappropriation
  • Your casting argument
Useful Phrases
  • Rather than producing a hagiography, the film would...
  • The structural principle is perspectivism itself, meaning...
  • We grapple with Elisabeth's distortions by...
  • The film's aesthetic sensibility draws from...
  • To avoid anything anachronistic in the treatment of the ideas...
  • We dramatise the rupture with Wagner not through dialogue but through...

Success criteria: clear vision, at least three expressions from the lesson used naturally, two key challenges from the article addressed, approximately two minutes.
