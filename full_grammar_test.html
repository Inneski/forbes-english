Possibility — maybe

might

might

Something is possible but not certain. I might make pasta. It might rain later.

One form for every subject. He mights does not exist.

might not

The negative of a possibility. She might not come — she has a cold.

No to after the modal, and the verb keeps its base form.

Obligation — not allowed

must / mustn’t

must

An obligation, often one the speaker feels strongly about. You must be home before ten.

Subject + must + base form, identical for everybody.

mustn’t

A prohibition — the thing is forbidden. You mustn’t feed the animals.

mustn’t and don’t have to are opposites, not alternatives.

must / not necessary

have to

have to / has to

An obligation that comes from outside: a rule, a timetable, a job. He has to wear a uniform.

An ordinary verb, so it has a past: I had to wear a uniform.

don’t have to

No necessity at all. He works from home, so he doesn’t have to drive.

Questions and negatives take do or does: Do you have to work?

Predictions & decisions

will / won’t

will

A prediction, an offer, or a decision made as you speak. I think she’ll win.

will + base form, the same for every subject.

won’t

The contraction of will not. Don’t worry — I won’t forget.

Both are correct English; won’t is the usual one in speech.

indefinite quantities

some / any

some

Positive sentences, and offers or requests. Would you like some tea?

A question does not change this: an offer still takes some.

any

Negatives and open questions. We haven’t got any milk left.

Uncountable nouns take no plural: some information, never informations.

Substitute for a noun

Expressing possession

whose / possessive ’s

whose

Asks who something belongs to. Whose bag is this? — It’s Sarah’s.

who’s is who is, and means something else entirely.

’s and s’

One owner: Tom’s keys. Several owners: my parents’ cars.

The apostrophe goes before the s for one, and after it for more than one.

Adjective → Adverb

Adverbs of manner

Regular

quick → quickly. dangerous → dangerously. Adjectives in -y: happy → happily.

Irregular

good → well. fast → fast. hard → hard.

fastly and goodly are not English words.

me too / me neither

So / Neither

So + auxiliary + I

Agreeing with a positive statement. I went to bed late. — So did I.

Copy the tense: went comes back as did.

Neither + auxiliary + I

Agreeing with a negative statement. I can’t stand horror films. — Neither can I.

The verb comes in front of I, as it does in a question.

🗣Discussion · in pairs

Take a prompt each and keep going until your partner has used the same structures back at you.

  • Your flatmate has left the kitchen in a state again. Tell them what they must do, what they mustn’t do, and what they don’t have to bother with.
  • Plan next Saturday together, disagreeing at first. Say what you are going to do, what you think will happen, and what you might do instead if it rains.
  • Compare your lives. Three things you have already done, three you have never done — and agree each time with So have I or Neither have I.
