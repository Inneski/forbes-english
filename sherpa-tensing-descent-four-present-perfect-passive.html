Descent four · present perfect passive

It has been done

The tense of announcements. Something happened, it is finished, and it matters now — and the passive lets you say so without naming whoever did it. The decision has been taken. By whom? The sentence declines to say.

Descent briefing

When to use it

The passive is not a different tense. It is the present perfect with the doer let go — and the reason you let them go is always one of these six.

You do not know who

Nobody can tell you the doer, so the sentence does without one.

"My bike has been stolen."

The doer does not matter

The event is the news; who did it is a footnote.

"The bridge has been closed since Tuesday."

The doer is obvious

Saying it would be a waste of everyone's time.

"He has been arrested."

You would rather not say

The passive is how English removes a person from a sentence.

"A decision has been taken."

Putting the important thing first

Whatever you put at the front is what the sentence is about.

"Three hundred homes have been flooded."

Putting the doer back with by

When the doer is the point, it goes at the end.

"The report has been signed off by the board."
Building the passive

How it's built

Affirmative

subject + has / have + been + past participle

"The forms have been sent."

Negative

the not goes after the first auxiliary

"It hasn't been decided yet."

Questions

the first auxiliary moves to the front

"Has the parcel been delivered?"

With the doer

… + by + doer, at the end

"It has been approved by the committee."

Turning an active sentence around

Three moves, always the same: the object comes to the front, the verb becomes has / have + been + participle, and the old subject either disappears or goes to the back behind by. Only the middle step changes from camp to camp.

ActivePassiveWhat moved
Someone has stolen my bike.My bike has been stolen.The bike came first.
They have closed the bridge.The bridge has been closed.They was nobody.
The board has signed off the report.The report has been signed off by the board.The board stayed, at the end.
They have flooded three hundred homes.Three hundred homes have been flooded.Plural subject, so have.

Conjugation chart

Been is fixed and the participle is fixed. Only has or have changes — and note that been and the participle are two different words doing two different jobs.

Three mistakes worth naming

Almost every error with this one is one of these three.

Not thisThisWhy
My bike has been stole.My bike has been stolen.After been comes the third form. Stole is the second.
My bike has stolen.My bike has been stolen.Without been the bike is the thief.
It has been happened.It has happened.Happen takes no object, so it has no passive.
The fork in the path

Active or passive?

Neither is better. The question is only what the sentence is about — because whatever you put at the front is what the reader will think it is about.

QuestionPassiveActive
What is the sentence about?The thing it happens to.
"My bike has been stolen."		The one who does it.
"Someone has stolen my bike."
Do you know the doer?Often not — and you do not need to.Yes, and you are naming them.
Where does the doer go?At the end with by, or nowhere at all.At the front, as the subject.
Which is shorter?Longer by two or three words.Usually shorter. Prefer it when both work.

This is the sentence a company writes when something has gone wrong: an error has been identified, your account has been suspended, the changes have been made. Finished, relevant now, and nobody at the wheel. Being able to hear the missing subject is half the value of learning it.

Trail markers

Signal words

Reach for the passive when

  • the doer is unknown
  • the doer is irrelevant
  • the doer is obvious
  • the process is the point
  • a report needs distance
  • the object is the topic

Verbs with no passive at all

  • happen · occur
  • arrive · go · come
  • die · live
  • exist · belong
  • seem · appear
  • become · fall

The test for whether a verb can go passive: does it take an object? They built it has one, so it turns. It happened has none, so it cannot.
