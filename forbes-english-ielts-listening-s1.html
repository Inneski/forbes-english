Section 1 — the everyday conversation

Two speakers, one form to fill in, and a recording that plays exactly once

Before you listen

The easiest section, and the one people throw marks away on

What Section 1 always is

Two speakers, an everyday situation, and a form or a set of notes to complete. Enrolling, booking, reporting something lost. It is the gentlest English on the paper.

Which is why a lost mark here costs the same as a lost mark in the lecture, and hurts more.

It tests writing, not understanding

You will understand nearly every word. The marks go on whether you can write a spelled surname, a phone number and a price down accurately while someone keeps talking.

Spelling counts. A correctly heard word spelt wrong scores nothing.

Read the form first

You get time before the recording starts. Use it to see what KIND of answer each gap wants — a day, a number, a name — so you are waiting for the right thing instead of listening to everything.

A gap after "£" wants a number. You can know that before you hear a word.

After the recording

The four places this section takes its marks

The correction

A speaker says one thing and immediately changes it — Tuesday… sorry, that’s the children, the adult class is Thursday. The answer is always the second one. This is the commonest mistake on the whole section.

Listen for sorry, actually, I mean, I beg your pardon. Every one of them is a warning that the answer is about to change.

The spelled word

When a speaker starts giving letters, an answer is being dictated. In Section 1 it usually happens once, and it is never repeated more than the speakers would naturally repeat it.

Know the letters that sound alike in English: A, E and I; G and J; M and N.

The number and the distractor

British speakers say double oh for two noughts and oh for one. And there are nearly always two prices or two times — one of them labelled as the wrong one, quickly.

Thirty-five was the monthly rate for non-members. She was joining, so the answer was forty-two.

    Take the call

    Use at least three: read the form first wait for the letters double oh = 00 take the correction two prices, one label spelling is marked
    🗣Discussion · in pairs

    In pairs, with a form each. One of you is the receptionist at a centre, a hotel or a garage; the other is enquiring. The receptionist must spell one name, give one phone number, and correct themselves once. The caller fills in the form and then reads it back.

    • Speller: give the surname once, at speaking speed. Do not slow down and do not repeat it unless you are asked.
    • Caller: read the whole form back at the end. Every wrong letter is a mark, so say the letters, not the word.
    • Somewhere in the call, change one detail after you have already given it. See whether your partner catches it.
    ✍️Writing · 100–150 words

    Write the six lines of a form for a booking of your own choosing — a course, a delivery, a repair — and beside each one write what KIND of answer it needs: a name, a day, a number, a price. That is the thirty seconds of preparation the test gives you, done in advance.
