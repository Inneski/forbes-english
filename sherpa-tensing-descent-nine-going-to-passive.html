Descent nine · going to passive

It is going to be done

Already decided, already in motion, and the decider is nowhere in the sentence. This is the one you meet in the letter that tells you what is about to happen to your street, your contract or your account.

Descent briefing

When to use it

The passive is not a different tense. It is the going to future with the doer let go — and the reason you let them go is always one of these six.

You do not know who

Nobody can tell you the doer, so the sentence does without one.

"The shop is going to be demolished."

The doer does not matter

The event is the news; who did it is a footnote.

"The road is going to be widened."

The doer is obvious

Saying it would be a waste of everyone's time.

"He is going to be charged."

You would rather not say

The passive is how English removes a person from a sentence.

"Some of these posts are going to be cut."

Putting the important thing first

Whatever you put at the front is what the sentence is about.

"The whole street is going to be resurfaced."

Putting the doer back with by

When the doer is the point, it goes at the end.

"The work is going to be done by the council."
Building the passive

How it's built

Affirmative

subject + am / is / are + going to be + past participle

"The shop is going to be demolished."

Negative

the not goes after the first auxiliary

"The road isn't going to be widened."

Questions

the first auxiliary moves to the front

"Is the fence going to be replaced?"

With the doer

… + by + doer, at the end

"It is going to be done by the council."

Turning an active sentence around

Three moves, always the same: the object comes to the front, the verb becomes am / is / are + going to be + participle, and the old subject either disappears or goes to the back behind by. Only the middle step changes from camp to camp.

ActivePassiveWhat moved
They are going to demolish the shop.The shop is going to be demolished.The shop came first.
Someone is going to charge him.He is going to be charged.Someone said nothing.
The council is going to do the work.The work is going to be done by the council.The council stayed, at the end.
They are going to cut some posts.Some posts are going to be cut.Plural subject, so are.

Three mistakes worth naming

Almost every error with this one is one of these three.

Not thisThisWhy
The shop is going to be demolish.The shop is going to be demolished.After be comes the participle, every time.
The shop is going be demolished.The shop is going to be demolished.The to is part of the form and cannot be dropped.
The shop will going to be demolished.The shop is going to be demolished.Pick one future. Will and going to do not stack.
The fork in the path

Active or passive?

Neither is better. The question is only what the sentence is about — because whatever you put at the front is what the reader will think it is about.

QuestionPassiveActive
What is the sentence about?The thing it happens to.
"The shop is going to be demolished."		The one who does it.
"They are going to demolish the shop."
Do you know the doer?Often not — and you do not need to.Yes, and you are naming them.
Where does the doer go?At the end with by, or nowhere at all.At the front, as the subject.
Which is shorter?Longer by two or three words.Usually shorter. Prefer it when both work.

Camp five is the tense of things already decided, and in the passive that is exactly what makes it uncomfortable to read: your contract is going to be reviewed tells you the decision has been taken and declines to say by whom. The evidence is there; the decider is not.

Trail markers

Signal words

Reach for the passive when

  • the doer is unknown
  • the doer is irrelevant
  • the doer is obvious
  • the process is the point
  • a report needs distance
  • the object is the topic

Verbs with no passive at all

  • happen · occur
  • arrive · go · come
  • die · live
  • exist · belong
  • seem · appear
  • become · fall

The test for whether a verb can go passive: does it take an object? They built it has one, so it turns. It happened has none, so it cannot.
