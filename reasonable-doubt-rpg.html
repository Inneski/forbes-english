How the deliberation works

Every word you say has a price.

Points · A careful answer earns +1 point.

Patience · The room starts with five. A careless answer costs one. At zero, nobody will follow your vote.

Files · Three pieces of evidence, in any order. The jury reconvenes when all three are closed.

Before the first vote · your toolkit · 1 of 2

The language of doubt.

You can question an argument without attacking the person making it. Choose the strength of your language carefully.

MIGHT / MAY / COULD HAVE + PP
He might have been mistaken. · A possibility in the past.

CAN’T HAVE + PP
The design can’t have been unique. · A strong negative deduction.

EVEN IF / ALTHOUGH + CLAUSE · DESPITE + NOUN
Even if he owned one, it proves nothing. · Concede a point, then challenge it.

Before the first vote · your toolkit · 2 of 2

The language of doubt.

IF + HAD + PP, COULD(N’T) HAVE + PP

SAID (THAT) + BACKSHIFT
“I heard it.” → He said he had heard it. · Report what someone said.

I’D RATHER WE + PAST · UNLESS
I’d rather we voted in secret. · Propose, or set a condition.

    🗣Speaking

    In pairs or threes. Claim, reason, and an honest limit.

    • Change roles: one of you defends the reliability of a witness, the other questions it. Ninety seconds each, then swap.
    • Give a 60-second closing statement to the jury: two pieces of evidence, one concession, and what you still do not know.
    • Think of something you are sure about. What would make you reconsider? Begin: “I would reconsider if …”.
