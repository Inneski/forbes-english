The Locked Study · B1

Adjective Order: Escape the Study

You wake up in Professor Whitlock’s study. The door is locked. On the desk there is a note: “Only a person who puts adjectives in the correct order can leave this room.” Three locks stand between you and the exit.

The Professor’s rule: When we use more than one adjective before a noun, they follow this order:
#CategoryExamples
1Opinion / Valuebeautiful, priceless, strange
2Sizebig, small, tall, narrow
3Ageold, new, ancient, modern
4Shaperound, oval, spiral, rectangular
5Colourred, dark, yellowed
6Origin / PeriodFrench, Egyptian, Victorian
7Materialwooden, silver, leather, iron
8Purpose / Type / Stylehunting, dining, Impressionist
—Nounkey, painting, lodge …

e.g. “a priceless old round Victorian silver dining table” — but native speakers rarely stack more than three!

The door swings open!
