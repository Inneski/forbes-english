Camp three · past simple

The camp behind you

Camp two was the bedrock — solid ground, always true. Camp three is the ground you've already crossed. Past simple describes finished actions: tents struck, terrain covered, a chapter closed off from where you're standing now.

Examples in
Basecamp briefing

When to use it

A completed action

One finished event at a specific point in the past.

"I finished the report yesterday afternoon."

A past habit or routine

Something you regularly did, but no longer do.

"We climbed every weekend when we lived in the Alps."

A sequence of events

Telling a story: one thing happened, then the next.

"She packed her bag, closed the door, and left."

A historical fact

Something true for a period that has since ended.

"The Ottoman Empire lasted six centuries."

A past state or feeling

A condition or opinion that no longer applies.

"I didn't like coffee when I was a child."

A specific past moment

Anchored to a stated or clearly implied time.

"They arrived at 6pm last night."
Building the tense

How it's built

Affirmative

subject + past form (regular: base + -ed · irregular: its own form)

"She worked late." · "She went home."

Negative

subject + did not (didn't) + base verb

"They didn't finish on time."

Yes / No questions

Did + subject + base verb?

"Did you call her?"

Wh- questions

question word + did + subject + base verb?

"Where did you go?"
