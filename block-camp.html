III. Adventures

click-through · branching story · multiple endings
  • The Last Train Home A cyberpunk megacity, a curfew closing in, and one train left before the checkpoints seal the district. Every route out is a prediction about what will happen next. Start hereFuture SimpleA1–A2Pro
  • Grammar Stoker’s Blocula Bram Stoker’s castle as a branching grammar nightmare. The west door is locked, the iron key hangs on the Count’s coat, and the last act asks whether you go down to the crypt or out through the courtyard — every route runs on a conditional, every report comes back in the passive. NewConditionalsPassiveB2Pro
  • The Long Way Home Homer’s Odyssey rebuilt block by block. Thirty-six scenes of storm and monster where the tense you choose decides what happened first — and losing the thread costs you the crew. NewNarrative TensesB1Pro
  • The Lost Yellow Road A voxel Oz. The Witch has scattered four tiles of the yellow road, and every one comes back as a question about what was happening at that moment — click the glowing object in each scene to read. NewPast ContinuousA1–A2Pro
  • Frankenstein Part I: Ambitions Mary Shelley’s laboratory rebuilt block by block. Victor has the parts, the storm and the nerve, and every question asks what he is going to do with them — up to the moment the eyes open and the plan stops being his. NewGoing ToA2Pro
  • Frankenstein Part II: Consequences The eyes are open and Victor has to live with it. Five branching choices decide what the Creature becomes — whether Victor speaks or runs, keeps his promise or breaks it — and the last asks Walton to choose between his crew and the glory that killed Victor. NewGoing ToA2Pro
  • Sherlock Holmes: The Blue Hour The Blue Star is gone from its case and a guard faces prison for it. Two branching routes through a painted, gaslit London — the shops or the stables, the rooftops or the river — where every question asks what somebody does, every day, and four evidence tiles decide whether Holmes can prove it before the nine o’clock bell. NewPresent SimpleA2Pro
  • The Last Bounty You rode in for a five-hundred-dollar outlaw and found the man who killed your brother. Two story choices decide who has to find the courage — and a wrong answer never ends the run here: it explains the rule and lets you put it right, with the points going to whoever gets it first time. NewPast SimpleA2Pro
  • A Fistful of Lies Fifty dollars for one hour at a saloon piano, and nobody minds that you cannot play. Two branching trails through a robbery that runs on finished past actions — question the dealer or search the office, save your friend or stop the wagon — and four clue tiles that decide whether the case closes. NewPast SimpleA1–A2Pro
  • Wonderland: The Stolen Now The last afternoon is looping and the palace clock is counting down. Two branching acts, a pink-or-blue cake trial that splits what is happening now from what always happens, and three endings. NewPresent ContinuousA1–A2Pro
  • Nautilus: The Black Archive A signal under the Atlantic, a city built from forgotten blocks, and something vast waking beneath it. Two branching descents — the crystal trench or the ruined temple, the engine room or the open water — and every question asks the same thing: a finished result, or an activity still running. NewPresent PerfectPresent Perfect ContinuousB1Pro
  • Twenty Thousand Leagues: The Sealed Log The same voyage, painted, and told from the ship’s log after the Nautilus came home. Every entry looks back from a later moment — what had already happened, what had still been going on — through the crystal trench, the coral city and the squid that was waiting beyond both. NewPast PerfectPast Perfect ContinuousB1–B2Pro
  • Nautilus: The Black Archive (Deep-Sea) The Black Archive again, painted: the same signal under the Atlantic and the same drowned city, lit by lamps rather than built from blocks. Two branching descents — the crystal trench or the ruined temple, the engine room or the open water — and every question asks the one thing: a finished result, or an activity still running. NewPresent PerfectPresent Perfect ContinuousB1Pro
  • Frostbound: The River Remembers A voice under the ice, two sisters who follow it north, and a dam their kingdom is proud of for the wrong reasons. Four branching choices through a frozen forest — the ridge or the trail, the fire spirit or the stone giants — where every question asks what somebody does every time, and your score alone decides which of three endings the flood leaves behind. NewPresent SimpleA1–A2Free
  • The Last Night at the Grand Hotel A mountain hotel on its last night, an owner who vanishes in eleven seconds of darkness, and a deadline at midnight. Twenty-eight time checks across every tense — five of them clues that break an alibi — and three decisions about who to trust, what to save first and who gets to hear the truth. NewMixed TensesB1–B2Pro
