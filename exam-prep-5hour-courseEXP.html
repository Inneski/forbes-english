What is in the test?

Structure & Reading

Find the keywords in the instruction. Then True/False, completing sentences, and answering in full sentences.

Getting this wrong costs marks you already knew how to earn.

Words & Grammar

Unit 8 space words and Unit 9 food words, used correctly in a sentence. Present simple, present progressive, past simple, some/any, one/ones.

This is the largest part of the paper.

Writing

One text of 100–120 words, with a beginning, a middle and an end, joined with linking words.

Part II covers this in full.

Hour 1 · Test strategy

Read the instruction like a sat-nav

Step 1 — find the verb

complete · choose · write · answer · match

The verb tells you what to do. Nothing else in the instruction does.

Step 2 — find the target

past simple · some/any · one/ones

The target tells you what the examiner is looking for. Miss it and a correct sentence still scores nothing.

Worked example

“Complete the sentences with the past simple.”

Verb: complete. Target: past simple. Now you know both what to do and what to use.

Hour 2 · Present simple

Habits, and things that are always true

Positive

I play tennis. · She plays tennis.

She, he and it add -s. Nothing else does.

Negative

I don't play. · She doesn't play.

The verb after don't / doesn't is always basic.

Question

Do you play? · Does she play?

Signal words: usually, often, sometimes, never, every day, on Mondays.

Hour 2 · Present progressive

Happening right now

Positive

I am eating. · She is watching.

am / is / are, then the verb with -ing.

Negative

I'm not eating. · They aren't playing.

Only am / is / are changes. The -ing word never does.

Question

Am I eating? · Is she watching TV?

Signal words: now, at the moment, today, right now, Look!, Listen!

Hour 2 · The decision

Which tense, and how you know

Again and again → simple

She plays tennis on Fridays.

A habit, a routine, or something always true. It does not matter what is happening as you read.

At this moment → progressive

Look! She is playing tennis.

It is happening as you speak. Look! and Listen! are the clearest signals in the exam.

Find the signal first

usually, every day → simple  |  now, Look! → progressive

Read the sentence for a time word before you look at the verb. The time word decides, every time.

Hour 2 · Spelling

How she / he / it gets its -s

Most verbs: add -s

play → plays · eat → eats · meet → meets

After -o, -ch, -sh, -ss: add -es

go → goes · watch → watches · finish → finishes

Consonant + y: -y becomes -ies

fly → flies · study → studies

But play → plays: there is a vowel before the y, so nothing changes.

Hour 2 · Spelling

Making the -ing form

Most verbs: add -ing

watch → watching · play → playing

Ends in -e: drop the -e

make → making · write → writing

Never makeing. This one is tested.

Short word, one consonant: double it

sit → sitting · run → running

Hour 3 · Past simple

Most verbs simply take -ed

The rule

watch → watched · look → looked

This covers most verbs in the language. Learn it first, then learn the exceptions.

Small spelling changes

like → liked · stop → stopped · study → studied

Ends in -e: add only -d. Short word with one consonant: double it.

Some verbs change completely

go → went, not goed

These are the irregular verbs. There is no rule for them — the next two slides are the ones to learn.

Hour 3 · Negatives and questions

After did and didn't, the verb goes basic

Positive

I watched a film.

Negative

I didn't watch a film.

Not didn't watched. The past is already inside didn't.

Question

Did you watch a film?

Not Did you watched? — same reason. This is the single most tested rule in Hour 3.

Hour 3 · Time markers

The words that put a sentence in the past

Pointing back from now

yesterday · last week · two days ago

German: gestern, letzte Woche, vor zwei Tagen.

Moving the story on

later · the next morning · a week later

These count from the last event, not from now.

suddenly = unexpectedly

Suddenly, he heard a noise.

German plötzlich. It does not mean quickly — it means nobody saw it coming.

🗣Speaking · in pairs

One of you saw something strange last night. The other is the reporter.

  • Reporter: ask four questions with Did you…? Every verb after did stays basic.
  • Witness: tell what happened, in order. Use suddenly once and the next morning once.
  • Witness: say one thing you do every day, and one thing that is happening right now.
  • Both: agree on one sentence for the newspaper, in the past simple, under twelve words.
