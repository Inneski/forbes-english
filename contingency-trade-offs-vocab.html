Plans and Outcomes

Part 1 · eighteen words
Plans and outcomes

Plan A, plan B

Every project runs on an assumption or two. When one fails, you either reach for the contingency plan you wrote in advance, or you improvise something ad hoc — and explain it post hoc.

The gap between what you planned and what you got is the shortfall. What you give up to close it is the trade-off.

Plans and outcomes

Planning ahead

contingency plan

A plan that can be followed if the original plan is not possible.

ad hoc

Arranged specifically as needed, not planned in advance.

post hoc

After the event; done or reasoned after the fact.

in parallel

Happening at the same time and connected.

trade off

Verb: give up one thing to gain another, as part of a compromise. The noun is "a trade-off".

shortfall

A deficit of something required or expected.

Plans and outcomes

Facts and checks

unintended

Not planned or meant.

actual

Existing in fact, as opposed to what was intended, expected or believed.

current

Happening or existing now.

requirement

Something that is necessary.

assumption

Something accepted as true or certain to happen, without proof.

reassess

Consider or assess again, especially in the light of new information.

Limits and safety

threshold

The level that must be exceeded for a reaction or effect to occur.

precaution

A measure taken in advance to prevent something unpleasant.

reliable

Able to be trusted to work well, consistently.

primarily

For the most part; mainly.

delay

A period by which something is late or postponed.

trial

A test of the performance, quality or suitability of someone or something.

On Site

Part 2 · eighteen words
On site

What the building is made of

On site the vocabulary turns physical: moisture in the walls, the firmness of a membrane, a dense and homogeneous concrete mix, a fire-retardant coating.

And when an old building lacks something, you do not rebuild it. You retrofit.

On site

Materials

moisture

Water or other liquid present in small amounts in a solid or on a surface.

firmness

The quality of having a solid, almost unyielding surface or structure.

dense

Closely compacted in substance.

homogeneous

Of the same kind all the way through; alike.

resilient

Able to withstand difficult conditions or recover quickly from them.

fire-retardant

A substance or treatment that slows or stops the spread of fire.

On site

Condition and repair

mouldy

Covered in or affected by mould.

hazardous

Risky; dangerous.

width

How wide something is.

retrofit

Add a component to something that did not have it when it was built.

remove

Take something away from where it was.

take off

Deduct part of an amount: "take 10% off the price".

On site

Around the site

chain

A connected, flexible series of metal links for fastening or pulling loads.

carpet

A floor or stair covering made of thick woven fabric.

stuck

Past of "stick": pushed something sharp into something. Also: unable to move.

locate

Find the exact place or position of something.

audible

Able to be heard.

appropriate

Suitable for the situation.

People and Pressure

Part 3 · twenty-two words
People and pressure

The human side

Projects are run by people, and people get tired. An arduous week ends in exhaustion; a monotonous one in boredom. A client who is agitated needs a persuasive answer, not an exaggerated one.

Some of these words have two meanings. Watch "concern" and "concerns", and "compromised".

People and pressure

Reading people

deserve

Have earned a reward or punishment by your actions or qualities.

tell

In poker: an unconscious action that gives away an attempt to deceive.

take for granted

Fail to appreciate someone or something because you are so used to it.

concern

Noun: anxiety; worry.

concerns

Verb: relates to; is about. "This email concerns the delay."

competitor

Another business offering a similar product or service.

People and pressure

Effort

exhaustion

A state of extreme physical or mental tiredness.

arduous

Needing a lot of effort; difficult and tiring.

monotonous

Boring and repetitive.

manageable

Able to be organised or completed without too much difficulty.

agitated

Troubled or nervous, and showing it.

nightmare

A frightening dream — or, informally, a very difficult situation.

People and pressure

Reactions and argument

startle

Give a person or animal a sudden shock or fright.

provoke

Cause a reaction, often a strong or angry one.

exaggerate

Describe something as bigger, better or worse than it really is.

persuade

Convince someone to do or believe something.

persuasive

Good at persuading people, through reasons or temptation.

opinion

A view or judgement, not necessarily based on fact.

People and pressure

Money, deals and leading

compromised

1. Settled a dispute by each side giving something up. 2. Weakened or put at risk: "a compromised structure".

affordable

Reasonably priced.

advert

An advertisement.

conduct

1. Organise and carry out: "conduct a survey". 2. Lead or guide someone.

    Use at least four: contingency plan trade-off shortfall ad hoc precaution reassess take for granted
    🗣Speaking

    In pairs or small groups. Give real examples, not one-word answers.

    • Describe a project — at work, at home or in your studies — where the original plan failed. Was there a contingency plan, or was the fix ad hoc?
    • What trade-offs does your job or course force on you? Time against quality, cost against safety? Which one would you reassess?
    • What do people in your field take for granted — a tool, a colleague, a material — that would be a nightmare to lose?
    ✍️Writing · 150–200 words

    You manage a building project. A supplier has delivered 30% less fire-retardant board than ordered. Write an email to the client: explain the shortfall, the precautions you are taking, and your contingency plan.
