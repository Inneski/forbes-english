The four words that get swapped

Hazard, risk, exposure, issue

hazard

The thing that could do the damage: a sole supplier, an unpatched server, a currency.

It exists whether or not it ever reaches you.

risk

Likelihood combined with impact. Always future, always uncertain.

The moment it becomes certain, it stops being a risk.

exposure

How much of you sits in its path: €4m, three sites, 60% of revenue.

A quantity, not a judgement.

issue

A risk that has already materialised. It has an owner and a fix.

Calling it a risk after the fact sounds like hoping.

Three fixed patterns

a risk of, a risk to, at risk of

a risk of —

names the event: a risk of slippage, of contamination, of a strike.

Ask yourself: what is the event?

a risk to —

names what is threatened: a risk to margin, to the schedule, to our licence.

Ask yourself: what gets hurt?

at risk of —

promotes the threatened thing: the launch is at risk of slipping.

Use it when the thing threatened matters more than the risk does.

all three

There is a risk of a strike; it is a risk to the launch, which is now at risk of slipping.

All three, in the order a reader expects them.

The four responses

What you can actually do about a risk

treat

Reduce the likelihood, the impact, or both: mitigate, control, hedge.

Before controls it is inherent risk; what is left after them is residual risk.

transfer

Move the financial consequence: insurance, an indemnity, a fixed-price contract.

The event still happens. Only the bill moves.

tolerate

Accept it consciously and say so: we are accepting this within appetite.

Tolerating is a decision on the record. Ignoring is not.

terminate

Stop doing the thing. The only response that removes the risk itself.

The only response that can honestly use eliminate.

Calibration

Saying how likely it is, without hiding it

raising it

may well · stands to · a distinct possibility · all but certain

We may well breach the covenant is a warning, not a maybe.

lowering it

might just · a remote possibility · an outside chance · conceivably

Might just is weaker than might, not stronger.

litotes

not inconceivable · a not insignificant exposure · far from ideal

Understatement a C2 reader hears as emphasis — and a B2 reader may miss entirely.

Three words a board does not confuse

Appetite, tolerance, capacity

risk appetite

How much risk you choose to take in pursuit of a return. A statement of intent.

Chosen deliberately, and minuted.

risk tolerance

The acceptable variation around that appetite before someone has to act.

A threshold that obliges someone to act, not a preference.

risk capacity

The most you could absorb and still survive. A fact about the balance sheet.

Not chosen. You can exceed your capacity once.

Register

What the passive may hide — and what it may not

legitimate

The control was bypassed on 14 March. The event is established; the agent is genuinely not yet known.

Honest work: reporting what happened before you can say who.

evasive

It was felt that the review could be deferred. A decision, with nobody attached to it.

A decision with no decider, so there is nobody to ask.

the test

Can a reader ask a person about this sentence? Mistakes were made → we got the supplier assessment wrong.

If you know who, and the sentence hides them, that is the tell.

🗣Discussion · in threes

Ten minutes. One chairs, one owns the risk, one is the finance director who signs the decision.

  • You own the cobalt exposure: cause, event, consequence, then one recommended response and one you rejected.
  • Finance director: the recommendation costs €2m a year. Push back using appetite, tolerance and capacity.
  • The event has now happened. Same room — every risk is now an issue. Redo it in ninety seconds.
  • Argue the other side: when is tolerate the professional answer rather than the lazy one?
✍️Writing · 180–220 words

Write the escalation email to the sponsor. Name the cause, the event and the consequence; calibrate the probability instead of hedging it; give the response you recommend and the one you rejected; end with a named owner and a date.
