What is a Gerund?

A gerund is a verb + –ing functioning as a noun. It can occupy any noun slot: subject, object, or complement. German uses nominalized verbs (das Lesen, das Schreiben) — English does this with gerunds, constantly, and without an article.

① Gerund as Subject
Reading novels improves your vocabulary.
Swimming every morning takes discipline.
Rule A gerund subject is singular — it always takes a third-person singular verb. "Running is fun." Never "Running are fun."
② Gerund as Direct Object

Certain verbs require a gerund as their object. The infinitive is not possible here.

She enjoys hiking in the Alps.
He finished writing the report.
Gerund-only verbsCorrectWrong
enjoy, avoid, finish, consider,
suggest, keep, mind, deny,
admit, practice, postpone, risk		 I enjoy swimming. ✗ I enjoy to swim.
③ Gerund after a Preposition

After any preposition, a following verb must be a gerund — never an infinitive.

I look forward to seeing you.
She is good at solving problems.
⚠ German Speaker Trap "I look forward to see you" — the to here is a preposition, not an infinitive marker. Always: look forward to + –ing.

More examples: instead of doing · tired of waiting · without saying · before leaving · after arriving

Possessive before a Gerund

In formal English, the doer of a gerund is expressed with a possessive — not an object pronoun. German has no equivalent structure.

Formal vs Informal Informal: "I don't mind him leaving early." ✓
Formal: "I don't mind his leaving early." ✓

Formal treats the phrase as a noun: his leaving = sein Weggehen.

Advanced Notes
Gerund vs. Present Participle

Same –ing form, completely different function. This is a classic C1-level confusion point.

FunctionTest
Gerund Acts as a noun "Swimming is fun." → Can replace with noun? ✓
Participle Modifies a noun; forms continuous tenses "The swimming coach arrived." → Modifies noun ✓
Complex Gerund Phrases as Subjects

English comfortably places long gerund phrases as subjects. German speakers often reach for a dass-clause instead.

Deutsch (Nebensatz)
Dass wir uns täglich unterhalten, ist wichtig.
English (gerund phrase)
Talking to each other every day is important.

Structure ✓ "Not knowing the answer is completely fine."

The Perfect Gerund

Use having + past participle to show the gerund action happened before the main verb.

Having studied in Berlin, she speaks flawless German.
Deutsch
Da sie in Berlin studiert hatte, spricht sie fehlerfreies Deutsch.
English
Having studied in Berlin, she speaks flawless German.
The Passive Gerund
being + past participle "She hates being interrupted." → Sie hasst es, unterbrochen zu werden.
"He feared being recognised." → Er fürchtete, erkannt zu werden.
