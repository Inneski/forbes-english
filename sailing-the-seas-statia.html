Statia & Actia

Some verbs name a state: you know, you want, you own. Others name an action: you run, you cook, you sail. States usually stay in the present simple; actions take IS/ARE + -ING for now. Read the two shores, then cross to Twofold Isle, where the sentence decides.

Reading the two shores

A state stays, an action happens

The shores are a guide, not a law. The test at the bottom of the chart is what decides: does IS/ARE + -ING work in this sentence?

Statia: knowing and believing

Verbs of the mind name a state, not something you do. They usually take the present simple, even for now.

I know the way. (not: I am knowing)

Statia: wanting and needing

So do verbs of wanting, liking and needing.

She wants a coffee right now.

Statia: owning and belonging

Possession is a state too.

This bag belongs to me.

Actia: things you do

An action in progress now takes IS/ARE + -ING.

We are sailing home at the moment.

Actia: the same verb, as a habit

An action verb takes the present simple for a habit, like any verb.

We sail every weekend.

The test

Do not ask “is this verb on the list?” Ask whether IS/ARE + -ING works in this sentence.

I am thinking about it. (an action: it works)

Twofold Isle and the Shallows

Where the sentence decides

These verbs live on both shores. As a state they stay simple; as an action they take -ING, and the meaning changes with the form.

Twofold Isle: one verb, two meanings

Read each row as a pair. The verb is the same; the meaning is not.

VerbA stateAn action
thinkI think so. (my opinion)I'm thinking about it. (I am considering it)
haveI have a boat. (I own it)I'm having lunch. (I am eating)
seeI see what you mean. (I understand)I'm seeing Ana tonight. (I am meeting her)
beHe is silly. (always)He's being silly today. (he is acting that way)
tasteThe soup tastes good.I'm tasting the soup.
weighThe bag weighs ten kilos.She's weighing the bag.

I have a boat, and today I'm having lunch on it.

The Shallows: either form, same meaning

With these two, both forms are right and the meaning does not change.

SimpleContinuousNote
I feel tired.I'm feeling tired.both are natural
My leg hurts.My leg is hurting.both are natural
You look well.You're looking well.both are natural

How do you feel? How are you feeling? Both are fine.

        Signal words

        You are in Statia

        • the mind: know, believe, understand
        • wanting: want, need, prefer
        • owning: own, belong
        • no end and no start you can watch
        • present simple, even for now
        • I think so, I have a boat

        You are in Actia

        • something you can watch happening
        • now, at the moment, Look!
        • IS/ARE + -ING
        • it starts and it stops
        • I'm thinking about it
        • I'm having lunch

        One test: can you watch it happening? You can watch someone run. You cannot watch someone know.
