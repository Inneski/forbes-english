Modal Verbs

can · could · must · should · might · will · would — learned on a mountain

B1 · Grammar Modal verbs 20 slides
What each one does

Seven modals, four jobs

Ability

can now · could in the past

“She can ski black runs.” “When she was younger she could ski perfectly.”

Obligation & advice

must = a rule · should = advice

Must not is forbidden. Should not is only a bad idea. The gap between them is the whole point.

Certainty

will = sure · might = maybe

“You will improve.” “The lift might not open.”

Politeness

could you…? · I would like…

These two carry almost every polite request you will ever need to make.

The form — it never changes

Modal, then the bare verb. Always.

Statement

You must check the forecast.

Never to check, never checking. The modal takes the plain verb.

Negative & question

Beginners should not attempt…
Could you show me…?

Negative: modal + not + verb. Question: the modal jumps to the front. No do, ever.

Talking about the past

might have left · should have booked

Modal + have + past participle. Might leave and should book point at the future instead.

🗣Roleplay · in pairs

One of you works at the ski school. The other has never skied.

  • Give three pieces of advice, and one absolute rule. Make the difference audible.
  • Ask politely for three things: a lesson, boots in your size, and the forecast.
  • Something went wrong yesterday. Say what you should have done differently.
  • Speculate about why your friend is late. Use might have twice.
