Before you start

It is not an accent test. It never was.

What is actually scored

The descriptors ask how easily you can be understood, whether you control the features that carry meaning, and how much effort the listener has to make. Sounding British is on none of those lists.

Candidates lose marks here for flat stress far more often than for an accent.

Stress marks the meaning

English puts the beat on the words that carry the point and lets the rest run light. Flatten that and every word arrives with equal weight, so the listener has to work out the point unaided.

Two syllables, two words: RECord is the noun, reCORD is the verb.

Six sentences, six words

“I didn’t say she stole the money” means six different things depending on which word takes the beat. The words never change; only the stress does.

Try it aloud on each of the six. Every version denies something different.

Before you start

Speech arrives in groups, not in words

The thought group

Fluent English comes in short runs of words said as one unit, with a small break between them. The break is not a hesitation — it is the punctuation of spoken language.

Roughly three to seven words is the natural size of a group.

The boundary carries meaning

Move the break and you move the sense. The group boundary is doing the same job a comma does on the page, and putting it in the wrong place misleads the listener in the same way.

Read a written sentence aloud and the commas usually show you where the groups end.

Inside a group, keep going

A break inside a group is the one that reads as trouble: pausing in the middle of a noun phrase tells the examiner you have lost the word, not that you are weighing an idea.

Pause at the joins, not inside the pieces.

Before you start

The pause, and what you put in it

Silence is allowed

A short silence at the end of a group reads as thinking. The same length of silence in the middle of a phrase reads as a search for a word. The pause is not the problem — its position is.

Two seconds at a join is unremarkable. Two seconds inside a phrase is audible.

Fillers are worse than silence

Errrm and a repeated first word draw attention to the trouble. A clean break does not. If you need a moment, take it quietly — or say that you are taking it.

That is a good question, actually buys the same time and costs nothing.

Talk around the word you lost

If the word will not come, describe what the thing does and keep the turn. Paraphrase is scored under Lexical Resource; stopping dead is scored under Fluency, and not kindly.

Never stop to ask the examiner for a word. They will not give you one.

🗣Discussion · in pairs

In pairs, with the long-turn card from Part 2. Read your partner a four-line answer, deliberately flat, then read it again in thought groups with the beat on the content words. Your partner says what changed.

  • Say “I didn’t say she stole the money” six times, one beat each. Your partner names what you denied.
  • Take a Part 3 question and answer it in groups of three to seven words, pausing only at the joins.
  • Have your partner interrupt with a word you do not know. Talk around it and keep the turn — no stopping, no asking.
✍️Writing · 150–200 words

Write out a two-minute answer, then mark it up: a slash at every group boundary and capitals on the word that takes the beat in each group. Read it back and check the beats land where the meaning is.
