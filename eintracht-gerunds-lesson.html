What is a gerund?

The -ing form as a noun

A gerund is a verb with -ing that works as a noun. It names an activity or concept. Unlike a continuous tense, it is not a verb here — it is a thing.

In English you cannot always replace it with zu + Infinitiv as you would in German. The gerund fills that noun slot.

Role 1 — Subject

Gerund as Subject

When the -ing form is the grammatical subject — what the sentence is about, what comes before the main verb:

Pressing high up the pitch  is  Eintracht's style.
Subject: "Pressing high up the pitch" Gerund phrase
What is Eintracht's style? → Pressing high up the pitch. That answers the question, so it's the subject.
Winning the Bundesliga  is  the dream of every Frankfurt fan.
Subject = gerund phrase
Not conceding in the first ten minutes  matters  enormously.
Negated gerund phrase as subject not + gerund
To negate a gerund, put not directly before the -ing form: not conceding, not pressing.
Role 2 — Object

Gerund as Object

When the -ing form is the grammatical object — what the main verb acts on. Many English verbs only take a gerund here, never an infinitive.

The manager  suggested  changing the formation.
Object: "changing the formation" Gerund phrase
❌ suggested to change — wrong in English. Always: suggest + gerund.
The striker  kept  running in behind the defence.
Object: "running in behind"
The club  considered  signing a new central midfielder.
Object of "considered" Gerund phrase

In context — Football examples

Full sentence analysis

Every colour-coded element is annotated. All sentences are from the world of football.

Scoring in the Europa League final  was  the highlight of his career.
Subject = gerund phrase"Scoring" = gerund
Compare German: Das Erzielen eines Tores... war der Höhepunkt... — German nominalises with das Erzielen. English uses the gerund naturally without an article.
The fans  enjoyed  watching the team defend so well.
Object = gerund phrase
❌ enjoyed to watch — never correct. Enjoy demands a gerund.
Playing with only ten men  forced  them to sit deeper.
Subject = gerund phrase
The whole phrase Playing with only ten men is the subject — it includes the gerund + adverbial phrase.
He  admitted  handling the ball deliberately.
Object of "admitted"gerund + adverb
German: Er gab zu, den Ball absichtlich mit der Hand gespielt zu haben. — English: simpler gerund phrase, no zu.
The referee  stopped  playing  after the flare landed on the pitch.
Object = gerund
Stopped playing = the match ended. Compare: stopped to look = paused in order to look. The gerund vs infinitive distinction after stop changes meaning entirely.
Not pressing the ball carrier  gave  them too much time.
Negated gerund phrase as subject
Negation before the gerund: not pressing, not marking, not tracking — the not always comes first.
Kontrastive Grammatik

German infinitive → English gerund

Where you use zu + Infinitiv in German, English almost always needs a gerund.

DeutschEnglishRole
Tore schießen ist das Schönste. Scoring goals is the best feeling. Subject
Er liebt es, Freistöße zu schießen. He loves taking free kicks. Object
Sie vermied es, den Gegner zu foulen. She avoided fouling the opponent. Object
Der Trainer schlug vor, früher zu pressen. The coach suggested pressing earlier. Object
Er bereute, den Elfmeter verschossen zu haben. He regretted missing the penalty. Object
Sie hörten auf, in der zweiten Hälfte zu verteidigen. They stopped defending in the second half. Object
Key insight: German uses es + zu + Infinitiv or a Nominalisierung (das Schießen). English replaces both with a gerund — simpler, but the pattern must be learned.
Feine Unterschiede

When the choice changes meaning

Some verbs in English take either a gerund or an infinitive — but the meaning shifts. These are the hardest cases.

With gerundWith infinitive
The striker stopped shooting.
He no longer shoots.		 The striker stopped to shoot.
He paused (e.g. dribbling) in order to shoot.
I remember winning this trophy.
I recall that moment (past memory).		 I remember to collect my ticket.
I don't forget to do it (future task).
He tried tackling from behind.
He experimented with the approach.		 He tried to tackle but missed.
He made an effort, possibly unsuccessfully.
She regrets missing the match.
She missed it and is sorry now.		 She regrets to inform you...
Formal only — fixed expression.

QUESTION 01
_______ in the Bundesliga requires incredible physical fitness.
Playing is the subject of the sentence — gerund needed. "To play" is possible but less natural as a subject in modern English.
QUESTION 02
Eintracht Frankfurt's fans love _______ in the Deutsche Bank Park.
Love + gerund. "Love to sing" is also used in American English, but the gerund is the standard form after love in British/European English.
QUESTION 03
The coach avoided _______ his best players before the cup final.
Avoid always takes a gerund. ❌ avoided to tire is ungrammatical.
QUESTION 04
The midfielder denied _______ the opponent intentionally.
Deny + gerund. German: Er bestritt, den Gegner absichtlich mit dem Ellenbogen getroffen zu haben.
QUESTION 05
_______ the transfer window wisely is what separates top clubs from the rest.
Using the transfer window wisely is the subject — a gerund phrase with adverb. The whole phrase functions as a noun.
QUESTION 06
The referee kept _______ play when the ball went out.
Keep always takes a gerund. He kept stopping play = Er unterbrach das Spiel immer wieder.
