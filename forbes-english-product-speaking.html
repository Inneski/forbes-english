Warm-Up: Talk About Your Work

Speaking fluency · 5–8 minutes · No preparation needed

Teacher: Ask these questions naturally — one at a time. Don't rush. Listen for grammar patterns, hesitation, and vocabulary gaps. Make notes but don't correct yet. Your goal is to get the student speaking freely and to understand how he talks about his job right now.
Student speaks first

Tell me about your role and your company.

Answer as naturally as you can — imagine you're meeting a new business contact for the first time.

🎙 Conversation Questions — take your time
  • Q1 Can you describe your role? What are you responsible for day to day?
  • Q2 What kinds of products do you manage? Who makes them and who buys them?
  • Q3 Who do you usually speak to in English — customers, suppliers, retailers, colleagues?
  • Q4 What is the most difficult part of speaking English at work for you?
  • Q5 Can you describe a recent conversation in English that was difficult? What made it hard?
👂 Teacher — listen for these
❌ Watch for

"I make market research"
"responsible to develop"
"We discuss about suppliers"
"I am working since five years"

✓ Target forms

"I conduct market research"
"responsible for developing"
"We discuss this with suppliers"
"I have been working for five years"

Note patterns — bring them back later in the lesson as natural corrections, not a list of errors.

Say It Better

Spoken upgrade challenge · 8–10 minutes · the student speaks each improved version aloud

Teacher: Read the weak sentence aloud. Ask the student: "How could you say this more professionally?" Give him 10–15 seconds to think. Then reveal the strong version together. He must say the strong version aloud — ideally twice, once slowly and once at normal speed. The goal is muscle memory, not just recognition.
🔄 Upgrade 1
❌ Weak — teacher reads this

"I make market research with customers in England."

✓ Strong — student says this

"I conduct market research with customers in England."

Why? We "conduct" research, surveys, interviews and meetings. "Make" is for physical things. Say it twice — once slowly, once naturally.

🔄 Upgrade 2
❌ Weak

"I am responsible to develop the product strategy."

✓ Strong — say this aloud

"I am responsible for developing the product strategy."

Why? "Responsible for + -ing" is fixed in professional English. "Responsible to do" sounds unnatural to native speakers.

🔄 Upgrade 3
❌ Weak

"Customers have problems with the membrane when it is wet."

✓ Strong — say this aloud

"A common pain point is handling the membrane in wet conditions."

Why? "Pain point" is standard product management vocabulary. It sounds professional to customers and suppliers alike.

🔄 Upgrade 4
❌ Weak

"Can you make the membrane more lighter?"

✓ Strong — say this aloud

"Would it be possible to reduce the weight of the material?"

Why? "Would it be possible to…?" is the professional way to ask a supplier about changing a product. It is polite but direct. Also note: "more lighter" is a double comparative — always avoid this.

🔄 Upgrade 5
❌ Weak

"We discuss with suppliers about the new product."

✓ Strong — say this aloud

"We are discussing the new product with our suppliers."

Why? "Discuss" does not take "about" — we discuss something, not "about something". This is one of the most common errors for non-native speakers in business English.

🎯 Quick Drill — say these from memory

Teacher reads the situation — the student responds with the correct phrase without looking.

  • 1 "You want to study your customers' needs." → I need to conduct…
  • 2 "Your job is to manage the product range." → I am responsible for…
  • 3 "Roofers complain about slippery surfaces." → A common pain point is…
  • 4 "You want the supplier to change the weight." → Would it be possible to…
  • 5 "You are talking to a supplier about a new idea." → We are discussing… with…

Market Research Roleplay

Roleplay conversation · 12–15 minutes · Teacher plays a UK roofer

Teacher: You are a roofer in England — straight-talking, experienced, slightly impatient. the student must ask you questions and follow up naturally. Do not volunteer information — make him dig for it. If he asks a closed question like "Is price important?", answer only yes or no and wait. This forces him to ask better follow-up questions. Correct his questions only after the roleplay ends.
🎙 the student — Product Manager
Your goal today:

You are conducting market research with a roofer in England. You want to understand how he chooses membranes, what problems he has, and what would make his job easier. Ask open questions and follow up on every answer.

🔨 Teacher — UK Roofer
Character notes:

You've been roofing for 18 years. You care about speed, reliability, and availability. You don't like membranes that tear or are slippery. You decide which products to buy, not the contractor. Be helpful but brief unless pushed.

💬 the student — useful questions to practise

Use these as a guide — don't read them. Try to ask naturally, and always follow up.

Opening "What type of projects do you usually work on?"
Discovering needs "What do you look for when choosing a membrane?"
Finding problems "What problems do you usually have during installation?"
Digging deeper "Can you give me an example of that?"
Buying decisions "Who usually decides which product to buy on site?"
Improvement "What would make the product easier to use?"
📋 Teacher — roofer answers (use freely, mix these up)
  • "To be honest, price matters — but if something tears easily on a windy day, it's a nightmare."
  • "Availability is a big thing. If my supplier doesn't stock it, I won't use it. Simple as that."
  • "Some membranes are too slippery when the roof is wet. That's a safety issue."
  • "The instructions are sometimes unclear — especially when it's raining and you're trying to read them."
  • "I care about breathability, durability and ease of installation. In that order."
  • "I usually decide what we buy. The contractor leaves it to me."
⚡ After the roleplay — correction focus

Ask the student to repeat these common weak questions as strong versions:

❌ Closed / weak questions

"You like this product?"
"Is price important?"
"You have problems?"
"What you need?"

✓ Open / strong — say these

"What do you like about it?"
"How important is price?"
"What kind of problems…?"
"What do you need from…?"

Supplier Discussion

Roleplay conversation · 10–12 minutes · Teacher plays a supplier

Teacher: You are a technical supplier — professional, busy, and numbers-focused. the student must relay what the roofer told him (from Activity 3) and negotiate a product improvement. Push back on cost and timeline. Don't make it easy. Listen for whether he uses the summary phrase "Based on our research, we have noticed that…" and whether he asks structured feasibility questions.
🏭

The Scenario

the student is meeting a supplier to discuss improving the membrane product based on market feedback.

The situation: After talking to roofers in England, you have clear feedback: the membrane is strong, but it is difficult to install quickly in wet or windy weather and sometimes slippery. Your job is to explain this to your supplier and explore whether the product can be improved. You need to ask about feasibility, cost impact, and timeline.

🎙 the student — Product Manager
Your goals in this conversation:

Share the customer research findings. Ask if the product can be improved. Discuss specifications, cost, and timeline. Summarise what was agreed.

🔩 Teacher — Supplier
Character notes:

You are interested in the feedback but cautious about cost. Reducing weight is technically possible. Adding grip coating adds 8–12% to cost. Development takes 4–6 months. Ask for written specifications before committing.

💬 the student — key phrases to use in this conversation
Sharing research "Based on our customer research, we have noticed that…"
Introducing requirement "One important requirement from the market is…"
Asking feasibility "Would it be possible to adjust the…?"
Asking about cost "How would this affect the production cost?"
Asking about timing "What would be a realistic timeline for development?"
Checking understanding "So, if I understand correctly…"
⚡ After the roleplay — spoken summary challenge

Ask the student to summarise what was agreed in the roleplay — spoken, without notes. He should cover:

  • 1 What did the customers say? (the feedback)
  • 2 What did you ask the supplier to change?
  • 3 What did the supplier say about cost and timeline?
  • 4 What is the next step?

This practises reporting and summarising — essential for meetings and internal updates.

Product Pitch — Speak for 2 Minutes

Fluency task + self-assessment · 10–12 minutes

Teacher: Tell the student you are a new retailer in Ireland who knows construction but not his product range. He must pitch one product for 2 minutes — no notes. Use the timer below. After he speaks, ask him to self-assess using the checklist, then give your own feedback. End with the 3-part teacher feedback: one thing he did well, one correction, one upgrade phrase.
2-Minute Speaking Timer Pitch one product — no notes allowed
2:00
The scenario

"I'm a new retailer in Ireland. I understand construction but I don't know your products. Tell me about one of your membranes."

Speak for 2 minutes. Cover: what it is, who uses it, what problem it solves, why it is better than alternatives.

🏗️ Structure guide — keep this in your head, not on paper
  • 1st Introduce the product — "This product is designed for…"
  • 2nd Explain the purpose — "Its main purpose is to…"
  • 3rd Describe the benefit — "The main advantage is that…"
  • 4th Compare — "Compared with standard options, this…"
  • 5th Summarise simply — "In simple terms, it helps…"
✅ Self-Assessment — the student ticks these after speaking

Click each point if you feel you did it well. Be honest.

  • ✓ I explained what the product is and who uses it.
  • ✓ I explained the main purpose clearly.
  • ✓ I described at least one key benefit or advantage.
  • ✓ I compared it to an alternative or cheaper option.
  • ✓ I used professional vocabulary (not "problems" or "things").
  • ✓ I spoke for most of the 2 minutes without stopping.
  • ✓ My sentences were complete — subject + verb + object.
  • ✓ I finished with a clear summary sentence.

Ticked: 0 of 8

💡 Teacher — 3-part feedback model
  • ① Praise: Name one specific thing he did well — e.g. "You explained the purpose very clearly and your structure was logical."
  • ② Correct: Give one correction from what you heard — e.g. "Instead of 'we make research', say 'we conduct research'."
  • ③ Upgrade: Give one stronger phrase — e.g. "Instead of 'it is better', you could say 'the key advantage is its durability in wet conditions'."
