Camp one · present continuous

The ripple

Every route starts from where you're standing. This camp is about now — and everything close enough to now that it's still moving.

Examples in
Basecamp briefing

When to use it

Happening right now

Something is in progress at this exact moment.

"I'm watching a film right now."

A temporary situation

True for now, but not permanent — it's set to change.

"She's staying with her cousins this month."

A trend

Something is changing over a stretch of time.

"House prices are rising rapidly."

A fixed future plan

A personal arrangement already set for a specific time.

"We're meeting the Berlin team tomorrow."

Describing a photo

What's visibly happening in an image, frozen in the moment.

"The children are building a sandcastle."

An annoying habit

A repeated action that irritates the speaker — always plus continuous.

"He's always borrowing my things!"
The elastic moment

How long is now?

Now is not a point on the line. It is elastic — it stretches to whatever the sentence needs, and the present continuous stretches with it. That is why the same tense covers this second and this century.

This second

The narrowest now: it is happening as you speak.

"I'm reading this sentence."

This week, this month

You are not doing it at this instant, but the activity is open and unfinished.

"I'm reading a long novel at the moment."

This era

Now can be decades wide. Nothing here is happening in the room you are sitting in.

"The climate is changing."

So the test is not "is it happening this second?" but "is it unfinished?" If the thing is still going on in any sense the speaker cares about, the present continuous is right.

Building the tense

How it's built

Affirmative

subject + am / is / are + verb-ing

"I am reading a great book."

Negative

subject + am / is / are + not + verb-ing

"She isn't listening to me."

Yes / No questions

Am / Is / Are + subject + verb-ing?

"Are you coming to the party?"

Wh- questions

question word + am / is / are + subject + verb-ing?

"What are you doing?"

A ripple further along

The arrangement you've already made

Present continuous is not only about now. Move the same ripple along the line and it becomes an arrangement: something already fixed between people, sitting in future time and behaving exactly as if it were under way. The dashed rings are the difference — the event is settled, but it has not started.

It has to be arranged

Somebody has agreed it. There is a time, and usually a place.

"We're meeting the Berlin team at nine on Thursday."

Not for predictions

If nobody arranged it, the present continuous cannot carry it.

"It's raining tomorrow." ✗  "It's going to rain." ✓

The diary test

If you could write it in a diary, this tense will take it.

"I'm flying to Oslo on the 14th."
