What separates C1 from B2 here

At B2 you can state a position. At C1 you can state it while leaving the other party room to move — and you can hear what their wording commits them to.

Three things are tested here: register (how formal), precision (what a term contractually means), and diplomacy (firm without confrontation).

Phrase bank

Language worth keeping — 1/2

subject to
Introduces a condition: "Delivery by October, subject to receipt of drawings by June."
we are not in a position to…
Diplomatically signals a constraint without confrontation.
whole-life cost
Total cost of an asset across its lifespan, including maintenance and operation.
force majeure
Contract clause excusing performance when extraordinary external events occur.
liquidated damages
Pre-agreed financial penalties for specific breaches — most often late completion.
novation
Legal transfer of all contractual rights and obligations to a new party.
pain/gain share
Mechanism splitting cost savings and overruns between client and contractor.
Phrase bank

Language worth keeping — 2/2

retention
Percentage withheld from payments as security against defects.
due diligence
Thorough investigation of a party or proposal before entering a commitment.
mitigation
Duty to take reasonable steps to minimise loss following a breach.
we hereby reserve our right to claim…
Preserves a legal position without immediately escalating.
prescriptive
Specifying exactly how something must be done — contrasted with outcome-based.
inequitable allocation of risk
Formal way to describe an unfair distribution of risk in a contract.
proportionate risk allocation
Risk should sit with the party best placed to manage it.
