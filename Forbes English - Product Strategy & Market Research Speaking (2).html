Product Strategy & Market Research

A speaking lesson — roofing, suppliers, and the conversations that actually shape a product line

C1 Advanced Speaking 8 questions · 12 stations
How this works

Mostly talking, some checking

Eight quick questions first, on the technical vocabulary a roofer or supplier would actually use. Those are scored.

Then twelve speaking stations. Those are not scored and have no right answers — they exist to get you talking at length about work you already know.

Take the stations at whatever pace suits the conversation. Skipping some is expected — nobody finishes all twelve in one session.

Vocabulary check — 1/8

During a site visit, a roofer tells you: "This underlay is fine, but it does not breathe — moisture gets trapped under the tiles." Which term best describes the property he is missing?

Choose the term that captures the roofer concern.

Vocabulary check — 2/8

You are summarising feedback from Irish retailers for your supplier. Which sentence most accurately reports a complaint without exaggerating it?

Pick the most precise, professional phrasing.

Vocabulary check — 3/8

A carpenter says your vapour barrier is "a nightmare to tape in cold weather." In a follow-up call, how would you best probe for the root cause?

Choose the most effective open question.

Vocabulary check — 4/8

In a product roadmap meeting, you want to propose delaying a new cladding membrane launch by one quarter. Which phrasing is most appropriately diplomatic and persuasive for a stakeholder audience?

Select the strongest professional phrasing.

Vocabulary check — 5/8

Complete the sentence with the single most appropriate word.

Type one word.

"Before we can sell the membrane in the UK, it needs to pass fire testing under the relevant building regulations."

Vocabulary check — 6/8

A retailer mentions a competitor breather membrane "undercuts us by 15% but the roofers do not trust it." How should you most usefully frame this in your strategy notes?

Choose the analysis that captures the real strategic insight.

Vocabulary check — 7/8

You are negotiating with a raw material supplier about a lead time increase. Which response best balances firmness with maintaining the relationship?

Pick the most professionally balanced response.

Vocabulary check — 8/8

Complete the sentence with the single most appropriate word.

Type one word.

"We ran a small trial with three roofing contractors before committing to a full product launch."

Speaking stations — 1/6

Warm-Up: Setting the Scene

A few minutes · No right answer, just talk
  • Tell me about a conversation you had recently — with a carpenter, a roofer, a retailer, anyone — that's stuck with you for some reason. What was it about?
  • And what made it stick? Was it something they said, or how they said it?
Speaking stations — 2/6

Vocabulary Toolkit

Sitting here in case you want it — never required
  • No need to force these in. Glance at it once, then forget it's here — if a word is genuinely useful while you're talking, it'll be waiting.
Speaking stations — 3/6

Voices From the Field

Let one question lead naturally into the next
  • Picture a carpenter or roofer you've actually spoken with. What do they care about most when they're choosing between products — even if they'd never put it in those words?
  • Has anyone ever pushed back on something you said about a product? What happened next?
  • If you could ask every installer in England and Ireland one honest question, no filter, what would it be?
Speaking stations — 4/6

The Supplier Side

Talk it through as if explaining it to a friend
  • Think of a time a supplier told you something you didn't want to hear — a delay, a price rise, a quality issue. How did that conversation actually go?
  • Looking back, is there anything you'd say differently if you had that conversation again today?
  • What does a genuinely good working relationship with a supplier feel like, day to day?
Speaking stations — 5/6

The Bigger Picture

No structure to follow — just think out loud
  • If you had to guess, what's the one thing your market research keeps telling you that leadership doesn't fully want to hear?
  • Where do you think this product line is heading over the next year or two — and how confident are you in that guess?
  • Is there a part of your job you wish more people understood properly? Tell me about it.
Speaking stations — 6/6

Winding Down

A gentle close, nothing to tick off
  • Of everything we talked about today, what's still sitting with you?
  • Is there anything you wish you'd said earlier, or said differently?
  • Anything on your mind you'd like to keep talking about, even briefly, before we finish?
Going deeper — 1/6

The Hardest Moment

One scenario — take your time with it
The situation

You are in a meeting in English — with a customer, a supplier, anyone. Something goes wrong. Maybe you misunderstand something important, or you say something that lands differently than you intended, or the other person says something and you are not sure whether to push back or let it go.

Has something like that actually happened to you? Tell me about it.

  • What did you do in the moment — and what do you wish you had done?
Going deeper — 2/6

Two Languages

Not a test — genuine curiosity
  • Is there something you can say in German or French that you genuinely cannot say as well in English?
  • When you are negotiating or disagreeing in English, does it feel different from doing the same thing in your own language? In what way?
Going deeper — 3/6

Something That Failed

Failure is more interesting than success — talk about it plainly
  • Tell me about a product decision, a market research finding, or a customer conversation that did not go the way you expected.
  • What did you actually learn from it — not the official lesson, the real one?
  • Is there anything about that failure that you have not really talked about with anyone at work?
Going deeper — 4/6

English at Work

Two sides of the same experience
  • Which of those two situations comes up more often in your work?
Going deeper — 5/6

How Others See You

You do not have to agree with any of this
  • How do you think a roofer in England sees you when you walk onto a site? What does he assume about you?
  • How do you think your Swiss colleagues see the way you work — your style, your priorities?
  • Is there a version of yourself at work that you perform — a role you play — that is not quite the whole picture?
Going deeper — 6/6

The Culture Gap

England and Ireland — not the same place
Something to react to

A lot of people treat England and Ireland as essentially similar markets — same language, similar construction habits, broadly the same customer expectations.

In your experience, is that actually true?

  • Where have you noticed the biggest difference — in how people communicate, what they care about, how they push back?
  • Is there something that works well in conversations with English customers that would not land the same way with Irish ones, or the other way around?

🗣Discussion · in pairs

Pick any station you did not reach and run it properly — five minutes, no notes.

  • Sell a more expensive membrane to a roofer who only cares about price per roll.
  • Explain a six-week delay to a customer who has already booked the scaffolding.
  • Argue that the market research is wrong — then argue that leadership is.
