Descent ten · past perfect passive

It had been done

By the time you got there, it was already over — and nobody in the sentence did it. This is the tense of arriving too late, told from the point of view of the thing it happened to.

Descent briefing

When to use it

The passive is not a different tense. It is the {TENSE} with the doer let go — and the reason you let them go is always one of these six.

You do not know who

Nobody can tell you the doer, so the sentence does without one.

"The safe had been emptied before the police arrived."

The doer does not matter

The event is the news; who did it is a footnote.

"The bridge had been closed for months."

The doer is obvious

Saying it would be a waste of everyone's time.

"He had been arrested twice already."

You would rather not say

The passive is how English removes a person from a sentence.

"The decision had been taken long before the meeting."

Putting the important thing first

Whatever you put at the front is what the sentence is about.

"All the files had been deleted."

Putting the doer back with by

When the doer is the point, it goes at the end.

"The report had been written by an intern."
Building the passive

How it's built

Affirmative

subject + had been + past participle

"The safe had been emptied."

Negative

the not goes after the first auxiliary

"The forms hadn't been signed."

Questions

the first auxiliary moves to the front

"Had the room been cleaned?"

With the doer

… + by + doer, at the end

"It had been approved by the board."

Turning an active sentence around

Three moves, always the same: the object comes to the front, the verb becomes had been + participle, and the old subject either disappears or goes to the back behind by. Only the middle step changes from camp to camp.

ActivePassiveWhat moved
Someone had emptied the safe.The safe had been emptied.The safe came first.
They had closed the bridge.The bridge had been closed.They was nobody.
An intern had written the report.The report had been written by an intern.The intern stayed, at the end.
They had deleted all the files.All the files had been deleted.Convenient, and no deleter.

The shortest chart on the descent. Had been is the same for everybody — no was and were, no third-person -s — and the participle never moves.

Three mistakes worth naming

Almost every error with this one is one of these three.

Not thisThisWhy
The safe had been empty.The safe had been emptied.Without the participle you have written an adjective: the safe simply had nothing in it.
The safe had emptied.The safe had been emptied.Without been the safe emptied itself.
Yesterday the safe had been emptied.Yesterday the safe was emptied.With no second past event to stand behind, the past simple passive is the tense.
The fork in the path

Active or passive?

Neither is better. The question is only what the sentence is about — because whatever you put at the front is what the reader will think it is about.

QuestionPassiveActive
What is the sentence about?The thing it happens to.
"The safe had been emptied."		The one who does it.
"Someone had emptied the safe."
Do you know the doer?Often not — and you do not need to.Yes, and you are naming them.
Where does the doer go?At the end with by, or nowhere at all.At the front, as the subject.
Which is shorter?Longer by two or three words.Usually shorter. Prefer it when both work.

Two past moments, and the passive removes the person from the earlier one. By the time we got there the files had been deleted tells you the deletion came first and refuses to say who did it — which, in that particular sentence, is usually the whole story.

Trail markers

Signal words

Reach for the passive when

  • the doer is unknown
  • the doer is irrelevant
  • the doer is obvious
  • the process is the point
  • a report needs distance
  • the object is the topic

Verbs with no passive at all

  • happen · occur
  • arrive · go · come
  • die · live
  • exist · belong
  • seem · appear
  • become · fall

The test for whether a verb can go passive: does it take an object? They built it has one, so it turns. It happened has none, so it cannot.
