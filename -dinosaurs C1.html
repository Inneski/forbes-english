C1 · Advanced Placement briefing 23 slides
Before the questions

Stating what the science accepts

The consensus passive

English reports settled findings in the passive and the simple past: the T. rex was considered an apex predator. The agent is left out because the point is the finding, not who found it.

Perfect and continuous forms sound like an unfinished process, not a conclusion.

Hedging a claim

Science rarely asserts flatly. Widely believed to have been, is thought to be, appears to have — the hedge marks how firmly the field holds the claim.

Believed to have been reports a present belief about the past.

The register carries it

A theory gains traction; a discovery transforms our understanding; a habitat is disrupted. At C1 the fixed collocation is the difference between fluent and merely correct.

Every one of these is a set phrase. Change one word and it stops sounding native.

The unreal past

A past that did not happen

Third conditional

An unreal past condition takes an unreal past result: if the asteroid had not struck, the dinosaurs would have survived. Both halves have to be in the past.

A present result (would survive) turns it into a mixed conditional — a different sentence.

How sure are you?

Swap the modal to change the confidence: would have (certain), might or could have (possible), may have (possible, more formal).

All of them keep have + past participle. That part does not move.

Inversion drops the if

Had the asteroid not struck Earth… is the formal equivalent of if the asteroid had not struck Earth. Invert the auxiliary and the subject, and delete if.

Written register only. It is common in academic prose and rare in speech.

The trap

Participle clauses, and what goes wrong with them

What it does

Having mined the amber block, the player… compresses after the player had mined into three words. It signals that one action finished before the next began.

Having + past participle for a completed action; -ing alone for a simultaneous one.

The subject rule

The participle has no subject of its own, so it borrows the subject of the main clause. Whoever did the first action must be the one doing the second.

This is the whole rule. Everything below follows from it.

The dangling participle

Having mined the amber block, the velociraptor was discovered. The main clause subject is the velociraptor, so the sentence says the velociraptor did the mining.

It is the commonest C1 writing error, and the writer almost never hears it.

Activation

Brief the expedition

Use at least four: was considered is thought to be believed to have been gain traction might have evolved had the asteroid not struck having + past participle contrary to popular belief by and large it was not until… that
🗣Roleplay · in pairs

You are the expedition scientist; your partner is the sceptic who wants the evidence. Three minutes each, then swap.

  • Explain what the feathered fossils found in China changed, and how confident the field is about it.
  • Argue what might have happened to the dinosaurs if the asteroid had missed. Stay in the unreal past throughout.
  • Correct a popular misconception about dinosaurs — the size of a velociraptor, or their colour — without saying “that is wrong”.
  • Your partner claims the Pteranodon was a dinosaur. Put them right, politely, in one turn.
✍️Writing · 200–250 words

Write the briefing note the expedition team reads before it deploys. Set out what is established, what is still contested, and what the team should not assume. Hedge the claims that deserve hedging.
