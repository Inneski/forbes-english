Reading — The Galápagos Islands

Read the passage carefully. Hover over highlighted words to see their definitions. Then answer the comprehension questions below.

The Galápagos archipelago lies about 1,000 kilometres west of mainland Ecuador in the Pacific Ocean. These remote islands are famous for their extraordinary biodiversity and the role they played in Charles Darwin's theory of evolution.

The islands are home to species found nowhere else on Earth. The giant tortoise, the marine iguana, and the blue-footed booby are just a few examples. Because the islands were so isolated, animals evolved in unique ways without the threat of human predators.

Today, the Galápagos face serious challenges. Many animals are endangered due to deforestation, introduced species, and climate change. The Ecuadorian government and international organisations work hard to protect this natural wonder.

Visitors to the islands must follow strict rules to preserve the fragile ecosystem. Only a limited number of tourists are allowed each year. Despite this, tourism remains an important source of income for local communities.

Grammar — Comparatives & Superlatives

Ecuador is a land of extremes! We use comparatives (to compare two things) and superlatives (to describe the most extreme version) to talk about it. Study the patterns below, then complete the sentences.

Comparative & superlative patterns
tall → taller → tallest
Cotopaxi is taller than most volcanoes. It is one of the tallest active volcanoes.
large → larger → largest
The Amazon is larger than any other rainforest. It is the largest in the world.
biodiverse → more… → most…
Ecuador is more biodiverse than most countries. It is one of the most biodiverse nations.
good → better → best
Ecuador's chocolate is better than many others. Some say it is the best in the world.
far → farther → farthest
The Galápagos is farther from the coast than most island chains.
