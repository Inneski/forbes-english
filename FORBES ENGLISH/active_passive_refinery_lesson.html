Active & passive voice

Grammar skills for clear communication on the fireground, in radio calls, and in formal incident reports.

01 What is it 02 Form it 03 When to use 04 Practice 05 Quiz

Why this matters: As a deputy lead, your words direct people in high-risk situations. Active voice delivers clear commands and assigns accountability. Passive voice is the standard for formal reports and procedures. Knowing both — and when to switch — is a mark of a professional communicator.

The core difference
Active voice

The subject performs the action. Clear, direct, accountable.

Active
The team leader isolated the valve.

We know exactly who did what.

Passive voice

The object receives the action. The doer may be hidden or unimportant.

Passive
The valve was isolated.

Who did it? Not stated here.

Colour key
Subject — who / what acts Verb — the action word Object — what receives the action
More examples — your working environment
Active
The incident commander ordered an evacuation.
Passive
An evacuation was ordered by the incident commander.
Active
Maintenance repaired the pump seal this morning.
Passive
The pump seal was repaired this morning.
Active
Our team tested the deluge system last week.
Passive
The deluge system was tested last week.
Building active sentences
Subject + verb + object
The person or thing doing the action comes first. Verb follows. Then what was acted on.
Active — Subject → Verb → Object
I submitted the incident report.
The crew deployed hose line 3.
She confirmed the ESD actuation.
Building passive sentences
Object + [to be] + past participle (+ "by" + agent)
The object moves to the front and becomes the new subject. The verb is a form of to be plus the past participle. Add "by + doer" at the end — or leave it out if the doer is unimportant.
Tense Formula Refinery example
Present simpleis / are + past participleThe alarm is tested daily.
Past simplewas / were + past participleThe valve was closed immediately.
Present perfecthas / have been + past participleThe pump has been repaired.
Future (will)will be + past participleAll staff will be briefed tomorrow.
Modalmust / should be + past participleEquipment must be inspected before use.

Past participles: Regular verbs add -ed: test → tested, seal → sealed, inspect → inspected. Irregular verbs change form: write → written, do → done, give → given. Quick test — if the word fits naturally in "The report was ___", it is the past participle.

Use active voice when...
  • Giving orders or commands on the fireground
  • Assigning clear responsibility to a named person
  • Speaking on radio — short, direct, no ambiguity
  • Delivering verbal briefings to your team
  • Recording who did what in shift handovers
Use passive voice when...
  • Writing formal incident reports or investigation logs
  • The action matters more than who performed it
  • The doer is unknown or not relevant
  • Drafting standard operating procedures
  • Maintaining an objective tone in sensitive matters
Same situation — different voice
Fireground radio call
Active — preferred
"Alpha team, advance hose line 3 to the north bund wall."
Passive — too slow for radio
"Hose line 3 is to be advanced to the north bund wall by Alpha team."
Incident report writing
Passive — preferred
"The isolation valve was confirmed closed at 14:32. All personnel were evacuated from the risk zone."
Active — less formal
"I confirmed the isolation valve closed at 14:32. We evacuated all personnel from the risk zone."
Standard operating procedure
Passive — traditional SOP style
"All breathing apparatus sets must be checked before each shift. Defects must be reported immediately."
Active — modern SOP style
"Check all breathing apparatus sets before each shift. Report any defects immediately."

Deputy lead rule of thumb: When you speak — use active. When you write a formal report — use passive. Commands must always be active. The passive voice in reports creates an objective, professional tone that is the industry standard.

Active to passive — conversion examples

Study each transformation. Notice what changes: word order, verb form, and the placement of "by".

Active
The safety officer filed the near-miss report.
→
Passive
The near-miss report was filed by the safety officer.
Active

→
Passive
The flange fire was extinguished by the crew.
Active
Engineers have completed the fireproofing inspection.
→
Passive
The fireproofing inspection has been completed.
Active
The shift commander will update the emergency plan next month.
→
Passive
The emergency plan will be updated next month.
The three steps
  • Move the object to the front — it becomes the new grammatical subject
  • Change the verb to the correct form of to be + past participle, keeping the same tense
  • Add "by + original subject" at the end — or omit it entirely if the doer is not important
