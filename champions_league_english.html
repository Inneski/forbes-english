🎙️ Kick-off — Quarter-Final First Leg
"And we are underway here at the Bernabéu! The atmosphere is absolutely electric as Real Madrid kick off this titanic clash under the European floodlights."
underway (adv/adj) — already started and happening now.
"Preparations are underway." / "The project is underway."
electric (adj, figurative) — thrillingly exciting; charged with intense energy.
"The electric atmosphere in the boardroom was palpable."
kick off (phrasal verb) — to begin something. Literal in football; figurative in daily life.
"Let's kick off the meeting at 9am." / "The festival kicks off on Friday."
titanic (adj) — of colossal size, power or importance. From the Titans of Greek mythology.
"They faced a titanic struggle to secure funding."
⚽ 34th Minute — Goal!
"GOAL! The prolific striker latches onto a through ball and slots it coolly past the keeper — a composed finish from a player who never looks rattled!"
prolific (adj) — producing a large amount. In business: "a prolific writer." In sport: "a prolific goalscorer."
latches onto (phrasal verb) — to seize an opportunity immediately and with purpose.
"She latched onto the idea and ran with it."
through ball (noun) — a pass played into space behind defenders for a teammate to run onto. Figuratively: "a perfect through-pass of information."
slots (verb) — to place something neatly and precisely into position.
"He slotted the final piece into the puzzle."
coolly (adverb) — in a calm, unflustered manner, showing no nerves under pressure.
"She coolly declined the aggressive offer."
composed (adj) — calm and in full control of one's emotions under pressure.
"The composed CEO delivered the difficult news without hesitation."
🔴 Post-Match Analysis
"That was a dominant display. The manager's shrewd substitutions in the second half completely turned the tie on its head — an extraordinary tactical masterclass."
dominant (adj) — having power and control over everyone else; clearly superior.
"The company holds a dominant position in the market."
display (noun) — a public performance or demonstration of skill or behaviour.
"The team put on a dazzling display of creativity."
shrewd (adj) — having sharp powers of judgement; very clever in practical matters.
"It was a shrewd investment decision that paid off handsomely."
substitutions (noun) — replacing one person or thing with another. Widely used in business, chemistry and sport.
"Staff substitutions were made during the restructure."
turn sth on its head (idiom) — to completely reverse or invert a situation.
"The new evidence turned the entire investigation on its head."
Grammar in Action
Three key grammar structures taught through Champions League examples. Practise with fill-in-the-blank exercises and get instant feedback.
Past Perfect — "What had happened before"
Use had + past participle to describe an action that was completed before another past action. It creates a clear timeline in the past.
By the time the referee blew the final whistle, the striker had already scored a hat-trick.
→ The hat-trick was completed before the whistle — past perfect shows what came first.
The manager had never won a European trophy before last season.
→ Covers all time up to a specific point in the past.
Fill in: "Before the final, the club ___ (never / lose) a Champions League knockout tie at home."
Passive Voice — Focus on the action, not the actor
Use be + past participle when the action is more important than who performed it, or when the actor is unknown or obvious.
The trophy was lifted by the captain in front of 80,000 fans.
→ The focus is on the trophy being lifted, not on who lifted it.
A controversial penalty was awarded in the dying minutes.
→ We don't need to say "the referee awarded" — the action is the focus.
Fill in: "The match ___ (postpone) due to heavy snowfall across the city."
Third Conditional — Imaginary past situations
Use If + past perfect, would have + past participle to speculate about how a past situation could have been different.
If the goalkeeper had saved that penalty, they would have progressed to the final.
→ He didn't save it, so they didn't progress. It's an imaginary past scenario.
Fill in: "If the manager ___ (make) that substitution earlier, we might have won."
