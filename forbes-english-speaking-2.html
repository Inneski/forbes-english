Warm-Up: Your Products and Your People

Speaking fluency · 6–8 minutes · No preparation needed

Teacher: The goal here is to hear how the student currently explains their products and audiences in English. Ask one question at a time. Do not correct — just listen and note patterns. Pay special attention to how they distinguish between the two product types (vapour barriers vs roofing/cladding membranes) and the three audiences (carpenters, roofers, retailers).
🏗️ Vapour barriers — interior use 🏠 Roofing membranes — exterior 🧱 Cladding membranes — exterior
Start here — student answers first

Tell me what your company makes and who buys it.

Imagine you are speaking to a new business contact who does not know your industry. Explain it simply and clearly.

🎙 Conversation questions
  • Q1 What is the difference between a vapour barrier and a roofing membrane? Explain it to me as if I have never worked in construction.
  • Q2 Who are your three main types of customer? What does each one care about most?
  • Q3 Why is it important for you to speak English with customers in England and Ireland specifically?
  • Q4 What is harder for you — speaking with a roofer on site, or presenting a product strategy to a retailer? Why?
  • Q5 Tell me about one recent conversation in English that was difficult. What happened? What would you do differently now?
👂 Teacher — listen for these patterns
❌ Common errors

"We produce membranes for protect buildings"
"I am responsible to develop"
"We discuss about the product"
"I make researches with carpenters"
"The membrane is more better"

✓ Target forms

"We manufacture membranes to protect buildings"
"I am responsible for developing"
"We discuss the product with suppliers"
"I conduct market research with carpenters"
"The membrane offers greater protection"

Note which errors appear naturally — save them for Activity 2. Don't correct mid-answer unless it causes genuine confusion.

🧩 Follow-up: Three audiences — speak about each one

Ask the student to describe each customer type in their own words. Then ask: "What is different about how you speak to each of them?"

🪵

Carpenter

Interior work. Fitting vapour barriers. Cares about ease of installation and airtight seals.

🏠

Roofer

Exterior work. Roofing membranes under tiles or slates. Cares about durability, breathability, speed.

🏪

Retailer

Sells to tradespeople. Cares about margin, availability, brand trust, and easy product explanation.

Say It Better

Spoken upgrade challenge · 8–10 minutes · Student says each improved version aloud

Teacher: Read the weak sentence. Pause. Ask: "How would you say that more professionally?" Give 10 seconds to think, then reveal the strong version together. Student says the strong version aloud — once slowly, once at natural speed. End with the no-notes drill at the bottom. Use errors you noticed in Activity 1 as bonus items.
🔄 Upgrade 1 — Describing your product
❌ Weak — teacher reads

"We make a special plastic sheet that stops water going into the building."

✓ Strong — student says this

"We manufacture a roofing membrane designed to prevent moisture ingress while allowing the structure to breathe."

Why: "Manufacture" is the correct verb for physical products. "Moisture ingress" is standard building industry language. "Designed to" sounds far more professional than "that stops".

🔄 Upgrade 2 — Explaining your role
❌ Weak

"My job is to think about what products we need and then talk to customers and suppliers about it."

✓ Strong — say this

"I am responsible for developing a structured product strategy, which involves conducting market research with customers and coordinating with suppliers on product development."

Why: Three key collocations here — responsible for + -ing, conducting market research, coordinating with. All are standard in B1+ professional English.

🔄 Upgrade 3 — Describing customer needs
❌ Weak

"Carpenters say the vapour barrier is sometimes hard to put in and it takes too long."

✓ Strong — say this

"A common pain point for carpenters is that installation can be time-consuming, particularly when achieving an airtight seal in complex areas."

Why: "Pain point" is essential product management vocabulary. "Installation" and "airtight seal" are the correct technical terms carpenters and roofers use. Using their language builds trust immediately.

🔄 Upgrade 4 — Talking to a retailer about benefits
❌ Weak

"Our product is good because it is strong and roofers like it."

✓ Strong — say this

"The key benefit for your customers is its durability in exposed conditions. Roofers consistently report that it holds up well in wind and rain, which reduces callbacks for your trade customers."

Why: When speaking to a retailer, connect product benefits to their business — fewer returns, happier trade customers. "Reduces callbacks" is powerful retailer language.

🔄 Upgrade 5 — Asking a supplier about changes
❌ Weak

"Can you make the membrane a bit easier to cut? Customers don't like how hard it is."

✓ Strong — say this

"Based on our customer research, one important requirement is improved workability — specifically, easier cutting on site. Would it be possible to adjust the material composition to address this?"

Why: Opening with "Based on our customer research" immediately gives credibility. "Workability" is the industry term. "Would it be possible to…?" is more professional than "Can you…?" when speaking with a supplier.

⚡ No-notes drill — student responds without looking

Teacher gives the situation — student responds with the professional phrase from memory.

  • 1 "What does your company do?" → We manufacture… designed to…
  • 2 "What is your job?" → I am responsible for developing… which involves conducting…
  • 3 "What do carpenters complain about?" → A common pain point is… particularly when…
  • 4 "Why should I stock your product?" → The key benefit for your customers is…
  • 5 "You want the supplier to change the product." → Based on our customer research… would it be possible to…?

Market Research: Interview a Carpenter

Roleplay conversation · 12–15 minutes · Teacher plays a UK carpenter

Teacher: You are a carpenter in England who fits vapour barriers in loft conversions and timber-frame houses. You are experienced but not interested in technical jargon — you care about what works on site. Answer questions briefly at first. If the student asks a closed question (yes/no), give only yes or no and wait. This forces better follow-up questions. After the roleplay, use the correction panel below.
🎙 Student — Product Manager
Your goal today:

You are conducting market research with a carpenter in England. You want to understand how he chooses vapour barriers, what problems he encounters during fitting, and what would make his job easier. Ask open questions and always follow up.

🪵 Teacher — UK Carpenter
Character notes:

20 years in the trade. You work mainly on loft conversions and timber-frame builds in the Midlands. You care about products that cut cleanly, seal well, and don't slow you down. You find some European products hard to source locally.

💬 Student — useful question structures

Use these as a guide — not a script. Try each type of question naturally during the conversation.

Opening "What type of projects do you mainly work on?"
Choosing products "What do you look for when selecting a vapour barrier?"
Finding problems "What challenges do you typically face during fitting?"
Digging deeper "Can you give me an example of that?"
Buying decisions "Who usually decides which brand to use on a project?"
Improvement "What would make this product easier to work with?"
Availability "How easy is it for you to source this type of product locally?"
Comparison "How does our product compare to what you normally use?"
📋 Teacher — carpenter's answers (use freely, mix these up)
  • "Mainly loft conversions and the odd new-build. Timber frame, mostly. Cold roofs."
  • "It needs to cut cleanly. If it tears when I score it, that's time wasted and money lost."
  • "The tape is sometimes the problem — if it doesn't bond properly to the barrier, you lose your airtight seal."
  • "I tend to stick with what I know. Changing products means a learning curve on site."
  • "Availability matters. I can't be waiting a week for materials when I've got a customer waiting."
  • "The instructions are sometimes in German or French. I just throw them away and figure it out."
  • "Mostly it's me. The builder trusts me on this stuff. I recommend what I use."
⚡ After the roleplay — question upgrade

Ask the student to convert these weak questions into strong open questions — spoken, without looking:

❌ Weak questions

"Is the product easy to use?"
"Do you have problems with it?"
"You like this brand?"
"Is price important for you?"
"Can you get it locally?"

✓ Open questions — say these

"How easy is it to work with on site?"
"What kind of problems do you encounter?"
"What do you like about this brand?"
"How important is price in your decision?"
"How easy is it to source locally?"

Retailer Pitch: Ireland

Spoken product presentation · 10–12 minutes · Teacher plays an Irish builders' merchant

Teacher: You are a buyers' manager at a builders' merchant in Munster, Ireland. You stock membranes from two existing suppliers and are cautious about adding new brands. You care about price, availability, and whether your trade customers will actually buy it. Push back with practical objections. Listen for whether the student connects product benefits to your business, not just their product.
🏪

The Scenario

Student is visiting a builders' merchant in Ireland to present two products and discuss stocking them.

You are visiting a builders' merchant in Munster, Ireland. They currently stock membranes from two other brands. Your goal is to explain the vapour barrier and the roofing membrane clearly, highlight their key benefits, and persuade the retailer that their customers — carpenters and roofers — will prefer your product.

Product A: Interior Vapour Barrier Product B: Roofing Membrane
🎙 Student — Product Manager
Your goals:

Explain both products clearly to a non-expert. Highlight the benefits for carpenters and roofers. Handle objections about price, availability, and brand recognition. Ask what the retailer needs from a supplier.

🏪 Teacher — Irish Retailer
Character notes:

Your trade customers are mostly roofers and carpenters in Munster. You need products that sell themselves — your staff can't explain technical specs. Price must be competitive. Delivery lead times matter a lot in Ireland.

💬 Student — key phrases for this conversation
Introducing a product "This product is designed for use in…"
Explaining the benefit "The key benefit for your trade customers is…"
Handling an objection "That's a fair point. What I can tell you is…"
Comparing products "Compared with standard alternatives, this offers…"
Asking what they need "What would you need from us as a supplier to make this work?"
Summarising "So in simple terms, what we're offering is…"
📋 Teacher — retailer objections (use 2 or 3)
  • "I've never heard of your brand. My roofers are loyal to what they know."
  • "What's the lead time from Switzerland? I can't have gaps in my stock."
  • "My staff won't understand the technical side. Is this easy to explain to a carpenter who just wants to get the job done?"
  • "I've got two suppliers already. Why would I add a third and complicate things?"
  • "Can you give me a sample pack? I'd want my best customer to try it before I commit."
⚡ After the roleplay — spoken summary

Ask the student to summarise the conversation in 4–5 sentences — no notes. They should cover:

  • 1 Which products did you present and to whom?
  • 2 What objections did the retailer raise?
  • 3 How did you respond to the objections?
  • 4 What is the next step?

This summarising skill is essential for reporting back to your manager or team after a customer visit.

Product Strategy Update

Fluency task · 2-minute spoken presentation + self-assessment · 10–12 minutes

Teacher: You are a senior manager from Swiss HQ visiting for a strategy update. The student must brief you on the UK and Ireland market — what they have learned from customers, what product opportunities they have identified, and what the next steps are. No notes allowed. Use the 2-minute timer. After they speak, work through the self-assessment together, then deliver 3-part feedback.
2-Minute Strategy Update No notes · Speak continuously · UK & Ireland market
2:00
The scenario

"Tell me what you've learned from the UK and Ireland market, and what your product recommendations are."

Give a 2-minute spoken update covering market findings, customer needs, product opportunities, and next steps. No notes.

🏗️ Structure to keep in your head
  • 1st Market situation — "At the moment, we see strong demand for… in England and Ireland."
  • 2nd Customer research findings — "Based on conversations with carpenters and roofers, the main pain points are…"
  • 3rd Product opportunity — "This creates an opportunity to develop / improve…"
  • 4th Retailer insight — "Retailers in Ireland have told us that… which means we need to…"
  • 5th Next steps — "The next step is to discuss technical specifications with our supplier and…"
✅ Self-Assessment — student reviews their own performance

Click each point if you feel you achieved it. Be honest — this helps identify what to practise next.

  • ✓ I described the market situation clearly — not just my products.
  • ✓ I used customer research language — "based on our research… / we have noticed that…"
  • ✓ I mentioned at least two customer types and what they care about.
  • ✓ I identified a product opportunity — not just a problem.
  • ✓ I used professional vocabulary — not everyday words like "things" or "problems".
  • ✓ I finished with a clear next step — not a vague statement.
  • ✓ I spoke for most of the 2 minutes without long pauses.
  • ✓ My sentences had a clear subject, verb, and object — no incomplete ideas.

Ticked: 0 of 8

💡 Teacher — 3-part feedback
  • ① Praise specifically — e.g. "You connected the customer research to the product opportunity very clearly. That structure is exactly right for a strategy meeting."
  • ② One correction — e.g. "You said 'we make researches' — the correct phrase is 'we conduct market research'. No plural."
  • ③ One upgrade — e.g. "Instead of 'the product is good for wet weather', say: 'the membrane performs consistently in exposed conditions'. That's the level of language for a strategy presentation."
