The words that carry weight

Five phrases you will hear in every status meeting

behind schedule

Milestones have slipped against the plan.

Not a pause, and not a renegotiated date. Those are said differently, and the difference matters in a report.

scope of works

What the project includes — its boundary.

Revised upward means more work. It is rarely announced as a schedule or budget change, but it is one.

to flag a risk

To identify it formally and put it in front of decision-makers.

Flagging is a written act. If it was only said in a corridor, it was not flagged.

contingency budget

Reserve held against what nobody planned for.

Deliberately not tied to any line item. Spending it on a known cost defeats the point.

Two sequences worth knowing cold

The lifecycle, and the risk process

Project lifecycle

1. Concept & feasibility assessment
2. Front-end engineering design (FEED)
3. Procurement & contracting
4. Construction & installation
5. Commissioning & testing
6. Operations & handover

Risk management

1. Identify potential risks across all project areas
2. Assess the likelihood and impact of each risk
3. Develop mitigation and contingency strategies
4. Assign risk owners and response actions
5. Monitor and review the risk register regularly
6. Report residual risks to the steering committee

🗣Roleplay · in threes

One project manager, one client, one finance lead. Fifteen minutes, one agenda.

  • Report that the project is behind schedule. Do not use the word problem.
  • The scope has been revised upward. Explain what that does to the budget.
  • Flag one critical risk, and propose who should own it.
  • Close by confirming what goes to the steering committee, and by when.
