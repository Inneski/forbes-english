Hour 4 · some or any

The rule, and then the exception

Positive → some

There are some apples.

This one never changes.

Negative → any

There aren't any plums.

After not, isn't, don't — always any.

Question → any

Have you got any money?

When you genuinely do not know the answer. Keep reading — the next slide is the case that catches people out.

Hour 4 · The case that catches people out

Offers and requests take some — even as questions

An offer

Would you like some tea?

You are giving, not asking. Would you like…? is always an offer.

A request

Can I have some water, please?

You are asking for a thing, not asking whether it exists.

The overlap

Have you got any / some apple juice?

In a café this is really a request, so both are natural. In the test, any is the safe answer to a plain question — but you now know why you hear both.

Hour 4 · one or ones

So you do not have to say the noun twice

one — singular

I like the red jacket. → I like the red one.

One thing.

ones — plural

I want the blue shoes. → I want the blue ones.

More than one thing. Shoes, glasses, trousers and jeans are always plural in English.

How to decide

Look back at the noun in the question.

The question tells you. Which shoes…? → plural → ones. Nothing else decides it.

Hour 4 · Reading method

Before you read the questions (1 of 2)

1 · Read the title

It tells you the topic before you meet a single sentence.

2 · Look at any picture

Free information, and it is usually about the main event.

3 · Underline the facts

Names, days, times, places, numbers.

Almost every True/False item is decided by one of these. Underline them and the answers are already on the page.

Hour 4 · Reading method

Then answer (2 of 2)

4 · Read the question twice

Small, large, Friday, Saturday — one word decides it.

5 · Find the line

Go back and point at the sentence that proves it.

If you cannot find the line, your answer is a guess. An answer that "sounds right" is the classic trap.

6 · Full sentence if asked

Where did Mia go? → Mia went to a restaurant.

If the instruction says in full sentences, one word scores nothing — however right it is.

Hour 4 · Full sentences

What a full answer looks like

Where did Mia go yesterday?

Mia went to a restaurant yesterday.

The answer reuses the words of the question. That is the whole technique.

What did her brother eat?

Her brother ate chicken and potatoes.

Past question → past answer. Ate, not eat.

What did they buy later?

They bought some ice cream later.

Not "ice cream". A full sentence needs a subject and a verb.

Hour 5 · Guided writing

The shape of a story that scores

Beginning

When? Who? Where?

Two sentences is enough. Start with a time marker and the examiner knows immediately that you can use one.

Middle, then the problem

What happened? What went wrong?

A story with no problem is a list. The problem is what makes the reader keep going.

Ending

What happened in the end?

Finish it. An unfinished story loses marks even if every sentence in it is correct.

Hour 5 · Guided writing

Phrases to join it together

To begin

Last weekend · One day · Yesterday

To go on

First · Then · After that · Later

For the surprise, and the end

Suddenly · But then · In the end · Finally

Use three or four across the whole text. Ten is worse than three — it stops being a story and becomes a list of connectors.

Hour 5 · Model text

A story that does the job (1 of 2)

Last Saturday, Mia and her brother went to the space centre. They looked at a big spaceship and took some photos. Suddenly, they heard a strange noise. An alien came out of the spaceship! At first Mia was scared, but the alien was friendly.

Bold marks every checklist item as it happens: a time marker to open, some, and suddenly for the surprise.

Hour 5 · Model text

A story that does the job (2 of 2)

He was hungry, so they gave him some pancakes and some strawberries. He didn't like the pancakes, but he loved the strawberries. He wanted more, but there weren't any left. Later, the alien went back into the spaceship. Before he left, he gave Mia two small stones — a green one and a blue one. In the end, Mia was happy, because she had an exciting story to tell at school.

112 words in total. Note any in the negative, one twice, and three irregular pasts — gave, went, had. The old model was 99 words and never showed one at all.

Hour 5 · Before the mock test

The five mistakes the error-correction section tests

The -s, and don't / doesn't

She doesn't eat meat.

she / he / it → doesn't. Everyone else → don't.

The base verb after did

Did she go? · I didn't see it.

Never did she went. The past is inside did.

any in negatives, ones for plurals

There isn't any milk. · The red ones.

And Look! always forces the progressive. Five rules — that is the whole section.

Activation

The Alien in the Restaurant

Use at least four: some any one / ones suddenly In the end went had
🗣Speaking · in pairs

One of you is the waiter. The other has brought an alien to dinner.

  • Waiter: offer four things, every offer with some. Two of them have run out — say so with any.
  • Customer: order using one and ones at least once each. Do not repeat the noun.
  • Both: the alien does not like the first dish. Sort it out without either of you being rude.
  • Both: tell the story afterwards in the past simple, in six sentences, starting with a time marker.
✍️Writing · 100–120 words

Write "The Alien in the Restaurant". Check it against the model: a time marker to open, some and any used correctly, one or ones once, three irregular past verbs, and a real ending.
