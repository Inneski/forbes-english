The bike, decoded

Where you sit, and what holds you

Saddle & seatpost

The saddle is the seat. The seatpost holds it, and slides into the seat tube to set your height.

Adjust the seatpost, not the saddle, for height.

Top tube, down tube, seat tube

The three main tubes of the frame. The down tube is the big diagonal one; the top tube runs along the top.

Together they are the frame — never "the body".

The bike, decoded

What you steer with

Drop handlebars

Curved bars giving several hand positions. The curved lower section is the drops.

“She moved her hands onto the drops.”

Stem & fork

The stem clamps the bars to the fork, which holds the front wheel and turns with the bars.

The stem sets how far forward you reach.

Where the power goes

At the front

Crank arms turn the chainrings; the front derailleur moves the chain between them.

Pedals clip to the end of each crank arm.

At the back

The chain drives the cassette — the cluster of sprockets — and the rear derailleur selects one.

Chainrings at the front, cassette at the back.

Make it precise

Three that get confused

Cadence

The rate at which you turn the pedals, in revolutions per minute. Not your speed along the road.

Maintain a steady cadence.

Traction

Grip between the tyre and the surface you are riding on.

The rear tyre lost traction on the gravel.

Rim vs hub

The rim is the outer ring under the tyre. The hub is the centre. Spokes join the two.

The spokes connect the rim to the hub.

Sound like a rider

Words that ride together

maintain a steady cadence

English maintains a cadence. It does not achieve one or perform one.

Also: maintain a pace, maintain your line.

shift into a lower gear

You shift into a gear — not on it and not at it.

Also: change into a lower gear.

lose traction

A tyre loses traction. It does not drop it or miss it.

Also: lose grip, lose the back wheel.

ride into a headwind

A headwind blows towards you, so you ride into it.

Also: ride into a crosswind, ride with a tailwind.

Read between the lines

Buying speed

When Maya bought her first high-end road bike, she imagined speed arriving in a box. The carbon frame was lighter, the gear changes quieter and the riding position more aggressive. On her usual climb, the same neighbour still passed her on an ordinary-looking bicycle.

At first, she blamed the headwind. Then her tyre pressure. Eventually, a club rider asked: was she buying equipment to solve a problem she had never properly identified?

Read between the lines

Finding perspective

Maya focused on her cadence and changed gear before the road became steep. She also asked a mechanic to investigate a rubbing brake. None of this produced a spectacular transformation. It did make riding more enjoyable.

She still loved the bike. Beautiful engineering could be a pleasure without being a shortcut to fitness. What changed was the claim she made for it.

Yet dismissing every upgrade as vanity is too easy. A bike that fits badly or shifts unreliably may be worth replacing. The question is: worth it for whom, and for what?

Role play

“It only happens on hills”

Take a role each. Run it twice, then swap and try a rubbing brake instead.

A · The rider

Your chain slips when you press hard on the pedals. It started two rides ago.

Describe when it happens. Ask for an estimate. Agree a spending limit.

B · The mechanic

You need to inspect the bike before you can identify the cause.

Clarify the symptoms. Explain what you cannot yet know. Agree to call before extra work.

Borrow a phrase It seems to… When you say…, do you mean…? One possibility is…, although… I’d need to check before…

Rider: The chain seems to slip when I press hard on the pedals, especially uphill.

Mechanic: When you say “slips”, do you mean the pedals suddenly move without the bike pulling forward?

Rider: Yes. It started two rides ago. I assumed the cassette needed replacing.

Mechanic: Wear could be a factor, although I’d need to inspect it before drawing a conclusion.

Rider: Could you give me an estimate before replacing anything?

Mechanic: Of course. We’ll inspect it, then call you with our findings and a quote.

When cycling becomes a metaphor

Take the language further

backpedal

To retreat from a claim or a position you took earlier.

The spokesperson began to backpedal under questioning.

an uphill struggle

A task that is hard going and stays hard going.

Changing the policy was an uphill struggle.

get into gear

To start working actively and effectively, after a slow start.

We have talked for weeks — it is time to get into gear.
