Camp nine · future continuous

Future continuous

Camp seven told you what will happen. Camp nine tells you what will already be happening — the thing that starts before you arrive and is still going when you do. It is the tense of the appointed hour, and of asking for something without appearing to ask.

Basecamp briefing · 1 / 2

When to use it

In progress at a future moment

It starts before the moment and is still running at it.

"This time tomorrow I 'll be flying over the Alps."

Already arranged, running its course

Nobody has to decide anything; it is simply going to be happening.

"I 'll be seeing her at the meeting anyway."

Asking without asking

The politest way in English to find out someone's plans.

"Will you be using the car tonight?"

Basecamp briefing · 2 / 2

When to use it

Two futures side by side

Both in progress at the same future time.

"While you 're unpacking, I 'll be cooking."

Guessing what is going on now

A confident assumption about this very moment.

"Don't call — he 'll be driving."

Softening a plain future

Less pushy than will do, because nobody chose it.

"I 'll be sending the invoice on Friday."

Building the tense · 1 / 3

How it's built

Affirmative

subject + WILL BE + -ING

"They 'll be waiting at arrivals."

Negative

subject + WILL NOT (WON'T) BE + -ING

"I won't be working on Friday."

Yes / No questions

WILL + subject + BE + -ING?

"Will you be joining us?"

Wh- questions

question word + WILL + subject + BE + -ING?

"Where will you be staying?"

Building the tense · 2 / 3

Conjugation chart

Nothing agrees with anything. Will is a modal, be stays in its base form, and only the main verb takes -ing — which makes this one of the easiest tenses in English to build and one of the hardest to know when to use.

Building the tense · 3 / 3

Three mistakes worth naming

The form is simple, so the errors are nearly always the same three.

Not thisThisWhy
I will being working.I will be working.After a modal, be keeps its base form.
I will be know the answer.I will know the answer.State verbs take the plain future, not the continuous.
When I will be arriving, call me.When I arrive, call me.After when and if, use the present — the same rule as camp seven.
The fork in the path

Will do or will be doing?

Both are true about the same future. What changes is whether you are naming the whole event or standing in the middle of it — and, more often than learners expect, how much you are asking of the other person.

The fork in the path

Will do or will be doing?

This is why "Will you be using the car?" is polite and "Will you use the car?" sounds like an instruction: the continuous asks about the world, the simple asks about you.

Trail markers

Signal words

The appointed moment

  • this time tomorrow / next week
  • at eight o'clock on Friday
  • when you arrive
  • by then
  • during the flight
  • at that point

The stretch of time

  • all day tomorrow
  • all next week
  • between two and four
  • from Monday to Thursday
  • for the next few hours
  • while you…

If the time marker names a point and the action was under way before it, you are in camp nine.

    Use these will be 'll be…ing won't be Will you be…? this time tomorrow at eight all afternoon still
    🗣Speaking

    In pairs, three minutes each. Every answer is an action already under way at a future moment.

    • Describe this time tomorrow, this time next week and this time next year: What will you be doing? Where will you be sitting? Who will you be talking to?
    • You need a favour: a lift, a book, a spare key. Find out your partner's plans without asking directly: "Will you be going past…?", "Will you be using…?"
    • Your partner is on a long journey right now. Guess what they'll be doing at this exact moment, and what they'll be doing at nine tonight. Three confident guesses each.
    ✍️Writing

    150–200 words: a message to a colleague covering your work while you are away. Say what you will be doing each day, what you won't be checking, and ask two polite questions with WILL YOU BE. At least six future continuous verbs.
