The four types, side by side

What each conditional is actually claiming

Zero — always true

if + present → present

“If you heat water to 100°C, it boils.” No exceptions. Christopher’s favourite kind of sentence.

First — likely

if + present → will + verb

“Unless he follows his routine, he will become distressed.” A real future.

Second — imagined

if + past → would + verb

“If he were better at reading faces, it would be simpler.” Not true now, and unlikely.

Third — unreal past

if + past perfect → would have + participle

“If he hadn’t found Wellington…” It did happen. This imagines the version where it did not.

Everything else that starts a condition

Four connectors and two inversions

unless

= if…not. Takes the present simple.

“Unless he follows his routine…” Never will or would after it.

provided that / as long as

A condition with a promise attached.

“…provided that nobody interferes with his methods.” Present tense, always.

Were you to…

The formal second conditional, inverted.

= If you were to… Slightly literary; Siobhan speaks like this, Christopher does not.

Had he…

The formal third conditional, inverted.

“Had his father been honest earlier…” = If his father had been honest…
