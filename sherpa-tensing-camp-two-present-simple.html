Camp two · present simple

Present simple

Before now, before any of this climb, there's the mountain itself. Present simple is the solid ground everything else stands on: facts, habits, things that don't move.

Basecamp briefing · 1 / 2

When to use it

General facts and truths

Things that are always true, no matter when you check.

"Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius."

Permanent states

Not expected to change any time soon.

"My sister works as a nurse."

Habits and routines

Repeated actions, usually with a frequency word.

"We visit our grandparents every Sunday."

Basecamp briefing · 2 / 2

When to use it

Timetables and schedules

Fixed, official times — even for a future event.

"The train leaves at 6:45 tomorrow morning."

State verbs

Thoughts, feelings, senses and possession rarely take continuous.

"This bag belongs to my colleague."

Instructions and directions

Step-by-step sequences, like a recipe or a route.

"First you crack two eggs, then you whisk them."

Building the tense · 1 / 2

How it's built

Affirmative

subject + verb (+s / -ES for he / she / it)

"She works in marketing."

Negative

subject + DO / DOES + NOT + BASE VERB

"They don't live here anymore."

Yes / No questions

DO / DOES + subject + BASE VERB?

"Does he speak French?"

Wh- questions

question word + DO / DOES + subject + BASE VERB?

"Where do you work?"

Printed on the wall · 1 / 2

The future that is already on a timetable

The bedrock reaches further than it looks. When a time has been published by somebody else — a railway, a cinema, a school — English treats it as a fact rather than a plan, and a fact takes the present simple even when it happens next week. The block stands in future time, but it is the same solid ground.

Printed on the wall · 2 / 2

The future that is already on a timetable

Timetables and programmes

Trains, flights, films, lessons — anything with a published time.

"The train leaves at 6:40." · "The film starts at eight."

Somebody else set the time

You did not decide it and you cannot move it. That is the test.

"Our flight lands at midnight."

Not your own plans

A personal arrangement takes the present continuous instead.

"I meet Ana tomorrow." ✗  "I'm meeting Ana tomorrow." ✓

    Activation

    Now describe the routine

    Use these every day usually never once a week don't / doesn't Does she…? at 6:40 as a rule
    🗣Speaking

    In pairs, three minutes each. Present simple only: every verb a fact, a habit or a fixed time.

    • Tell your partner about your ordinary week: what you do every day, what you never do, and what happens once a month.
    • Read your partner a timetable — a train, a course, a cinema programme — and agree how you will both get there. Every time is a fact, not a plan.
    • Explain to your partner how you make a dish or how you get home from the station: step by step, first, then, after that.
    ✍️Writing

    150–200 words: a profile of a colleague or a friend for the company newsletter. What they do, what they believe, how their week runs. At least eight present simple verbs, four of them with -S, and two frequency words.
