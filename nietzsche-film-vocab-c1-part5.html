Nietzsche on Film

The vocabulary of film criticism, and the C1 skill of choosing a word by its register rather than its meaning

C1 · Advanced Film criticism vocabulary 21 slides
Before the questions

Terms that only look like ordinary words

Mise-en-scène is not the camera

It is everything composed inside the frame — set, light, costume, where the actors stand. Cinematography is the camera and the lighting technique; blocking is movement alone.

Three terms, three different jobs. Critics do not use them loosely.

Diegetic means inside the story

A piano playing in the room is diegetic: the characters could hear it. A score is non-diegetic: only the audience can. The line is drawn by who can hear it, not by what it sounds like.

A radio a character switches on is diegetic. The same tune over the credits is not.

Verisimilitude is not realism

Verisimilitude is seeming true within the conventions of the work. Realism names an artistic movement. Authenticity is a cultural claim; continuity is only consistency between shots.

A fantasy film can have high verisimilitude. It cannot be realist.

The C1 move

Four words, one meaning, four temperatures

The distractors are not wrong

At C1 the wrong answer is rarely false. Derivative, imitative, unoriginal and ersatz all say a work copies. Only ersatz accuses it of pretending to be the real thing.

Read what the sentence is doing, not only what it means.

Register follows the document

In a press release a director does not bail out or quit; she withdraws. The informal word is not a smaller version of the formal one — it carries a judgement the formal one refuses.

The right word in the wrong register is still the wrong word.

Praise has a scale too

Memorable is unforgettable. Outstanding is very good. Definitive claims something narrower and larger: the version future performances will be measured against.

Ask what the critic is committing to, not how enthusiastic they sound.

The mechanics

Collocation, and the shape of a word

Fixed phrases have no logic to appeal to

A studio greenlights a film; nothing else does. Funding arrives at the eleventh hour, never in or on it. These are learned whole or got wrong.

Authorised and permitted are not wrong English. They are the wrong industry.

Suffixes carry word class

-al and -ment tend to make nouns, -ive and -ic adjectives, -ly adverbs. Evoke becomes evocative, the way demonstrate becomes demonstrative.

If the suffix does not exist (evocational), the word does not either.

A nominalisation is not a gerund

Portrayal is the noun that formal criticism reaches for. Portraying is a verb doing a noun’s work — possible, weaker; portrait is a different object altogether.

Academic register prefers the derived noun. It is why this prose feels dense.

Activation

Review the film

Use at least four: mise-en-scène non-diegetic verisimilitude the definitive ersatz eschew for all its countenance restrained
🗣Roleplay · in pairs

One of you has just seen the film and thinks it works; the other reviews for a broadsheet and does not. Four minutes each, then swap.

  • Describe one scene by its mise-en-scène, without saying “good” or “beautiful”.
  • Criticise a performance you otherwise admired. Pick the word that carries exactly as much blame as you mean.
  • Defend the non-diegetic score against the charge that it tells the audience what to feel.
  • Say what the film gets right about Nietzsche and what it flattens — and hedge the second.
✍️Writing · 200–250 words

Write the review a quality newspaper would print. Praise one thing precisely, object to one thing precisely, and let the register do the work: no intensifiers, and no word chosen for being stronger rather than right.
