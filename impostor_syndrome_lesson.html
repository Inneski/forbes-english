Section 4 of 6
Language Focus
Notice how language is used in the text to describe feelings and experiences.

A. Describing feelings with can't + verb

Pattern
"Even after winning several awards, Maya Angelou couldn't escape the nagging doubt..."
We use can't / couldn't + verb to show inability to control a feeling or thought — the person tries but fails to stop it.
Another example
"Why can't so many of us shake the feeling that we haven't earned our accomplishments?"
Shake here means "get rid of" a feeling. Common collocations: shake a feeling, escape doubt, silence the voice in your head.

B. Hedging language — softening strong claims

Hedges in the text
"Imposterism may be more prevalent and disproportionately affect underrepresented groups."
The writer uses may because research is not fully conclusive. Academic and professional writing often hedges with: may, might, tends to, appears to, suggests that.

Discussion & Reflection
Use these questions for pair or group discussion, or write your own thoughts below.
Question 1 — Personal experience
"Can you think of a time when you felt you didn't deserve something you achieved? How did you deal with it?"
Try to use: I remember feeling... / At the time I thought... / Looking back, I realise...
Question 2 — Society & groups
"Why might impostor syndrome affect some groups more than others — for example, women, minorities, or first-generation university students?"
Try to use: It could be because... / One possible reason is... / I think society plays a role in...
Question 3 — Solutions
"The text says talking about impostor syndrome is the most effective way to combat it. Do you agree? What other strategies might help?"
Try to use: I agree / disagree because... / Another approach could be... / In my experience...
Question 4 — Language & culture
"Is there a word or phrase in your own language for the feeling of 'impostor syndrome'? Does your culture talk openly about self-doubt?"
Try to use: In my language we say... / Culturally, it's common / unusual to...
