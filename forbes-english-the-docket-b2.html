Before the case opens

Six words the courtroom cannot do without

defraud · fraudulent

To take money by deception, and the adjective for it.

The verb takes a person as its object: you defraud somebody out of something.

inadmissible

Evidence a judge will not let the court receive.

Ruled case by case, not automatic. Improperly obtained evidence is sometimes excluded — never always.

revoke · mandatory

To cancel officially · required by law.

Watch what mandatory attaches to: a duty, a check, a disclosure. Never an offence.

grievance · offender

A formal complaint · a person who breaks the law.

A grievance is raised, heard and either upheld or dismissed. It is the word both HR and the tribunal use.

The pair everyone gets wrong

prevalent, rampant, and the three reconciles

prevalent

Widespread. Common. Found all over.

Neutral. A dialect, a practice, a reputation, a species can all be prevalent, and nothing is implied about whether that is good.

rampant

Widespread, unchecked — and bad.

Weeds, inflation, corruption, rumour. If you would not mind it spreading, it is not rampant.

reconcile × 3

people · views · facts

Reconcile two people, two views (find where they agree), or one fact with another (make them fit). The third sense is what this case turns on.

🗣Roleplay · in threes

One witness, one counsel, one judge. Counsel may object; the judge rules.

  • Counsel: establish what the witness saw, without ever asking a yes/no question.
  • Witness: describe a practice as prevalent, then describe a different one as rampant. Justify both.
  • Judge: rule one piece of evidence inadmissible, and say why in one sentence.
  • All three: agree what the grievance actually was, in twelve words or fewer.
