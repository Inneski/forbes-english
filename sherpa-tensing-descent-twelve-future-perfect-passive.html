Descent twelve · future perfect passive

It will have been done

Four words of auxiliary and not a person in sight. Stand at a point in the future, look back, and this says the thing is already behind you — without committing anybody to having done it. Contracts are full of it, and that is not an accident.

Descent briefing

When to use it

The passive is not a different tense. It is the future perfect with the doer let go — and the reason you let them go is always one of these six.

You do not know who

Nobody can tell you the doer, so the sentence does without one.

"The road will have been resurfaced by then."

The doer does not matter

The event is the news; who did it is a footnote.

"All the units will have been sold before completion."

The doer is obvious

Saying it would be a waste of everyone's time.

"You will have been notified in writing."

You would rather not say

The passive is how English removes a person from a sentence.

"The matter will have been resolved by Friday."

Putting the important thing first

Whatever you put at the front is what the sentence is about.

"Every invoice will have been settled by the year end."

Putting the doer back with by

When the doer is the point, it goes at the end.

"The site will have been cleared by the contractor."
Building the passive

How it's built

Affirmative

subject + will have been + past participle

"It will have been finished by six."

Negative

the not goes after the first auxiliary

"The forms won't have been processed by Monday."

Questions

the first auxiliary moves to the front

"Will the work have been completed?"

With the doer

… + by + doer, at the end

"It will have been cleared by the contractor."

Turning an active sentence around

Three moves, always the same: the object comes to the front, the verb becomes will have been + participle, and the old subject either disappears or goes to the back behind by. Only the middle step changes from camp to camp.

ActivePassiveWhat moved
They will have resurfaced the road.The road will have been resurfaced.The road came first.
Someone will have notified you.You will have been notified.Someone was nobody.
The contractor will have cleared the site.The site will have been cleared by the contractor.The contractor stayed, at the end.
They will have sold all the units.All the units will have been sold.No seller anywhere.

Three mistakes worth naming

Almost every error with this one is one of these three.

Not thisThisWhy
It will have been finish by six.It will have been finished by six.After been comes the participle. Four auxiliaries do not change that.
It will has been finished.It will have been finished.After will the verb is always the plain form: have, never has.
By Friday it will be resolved.By Friday it will have been resolved.A deadline needs the perfect. Will be resolved says when, not already done.
The fork in the path

Active or passive?

Neither is better. The question is only what the sentence is about — because whatever you put at the front is what the reader will think it is about.

QuestionPassiveActive
What is the sentence about?The thing it happens to.
"The road will have been resurfaced."		The one who does it.
"They will have resurfaced the road."
Do you know the doer?Often not — and you do not need to.Yes, and you are naming them.
Where does the doer go?At the end with by, or nowhere at all.At the front, as the subject.
Which is shorter?Longer by two or three words.Usually shorter. Prefer it when both work.

Read a completion clause in any contract and you will find this form doing quiet work: the works will have been completed to the satisfaction of the surveyor. Deadline met, standard stated, and nobody named as the one who has to meet it. That is the sentence to learn to notice.

Trail markers

Signal words

Reach for the passive when

  • the doer is unknown
  • the doer is irrelevant
  • the doer is obvious
  • the process is the point
  • a report needs distance
  • the object is the topic

Verbs with no passive at all

  • happen · occur
  • arrive · go · come
  • die · live
  • exist · belong
  • seem · appear
  • become · fall

The test for whether a verb can go passive: does it take an object? They built it has one, so it turns. It happened has none, so it cannot.
