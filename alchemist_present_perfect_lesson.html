B2 · GRAMMAR

Present Perfect: Simple vs Continuous

Explore the difference between "has walked" and "has been walking" through Santiago's journey across the desert in Paulo Coelho's The Alchemist.

Warm-up

Before you start

Look at these two sentences. Both are grammatically correct — but they don't mean exactly the same thing. Can you feel the difference?

Present Perfect SimpleSantiago has walked across the desert.
Present Perfect ContinuousSantiago has been walking across the desert.

By the end of this lesson, you'll be able to explain exactly why we choose one over the other — and use both correctly yourself.

Grammar focus

The rules

Form — Simple: have/has + past participle  ·  Santiago has crossed the desert.

Form — Continuous: have/has + been + verb-ing  ·  Santiago has been crossing the desert.

1. Result or completion

Use the Simple form when we care about the result or the fact that something is finished.

The alchemist has taught Santiago the secret of the desert. (It's done — he knows it now.)

1. The process itself

Use the Continuous form when we care about the activity or process, not whether it's finished.

The alchemist has been teaching Santiago about the desert. (Focus on the teaching process.)

2. Quantity or number of times

Use the Simple form with numbers — "how many", "three times", etc.

Santiago has lost his money twice on this journey.

2. Duration — "how long"

Use the Continuous form to emphasise how long an action has been going on, often with "for" or "since".

Santiago has been travelling for almost a year.

3. State verbs

Verbs like know, believe, want, own, understand are rarely used in continuous forms at all.

Santiago has always believed in the power of omens.

3. Visible evidence or effect right now

The Continuous form often explains a present, visible result — tiredness, dust, sweat.

Santiago's clothes are covered in sand — he's been walking all day.

A useful test: if you can naturally ask "How many...?" the Simple form usually fits; if you can naturally ask "How long...?" the Continuous form usually fits.

Reading

Santiago's journey, so far

Santiago has been travelling since he left his flock behind in Andalusia, and in that time he has crossed a sea, been robbed once, and rebuilt his savings from nothing. He has been working at the crystal shop for several months now, and his patience there has taught him more than he expected.

Since joining the caravan, Santiago has been listening carefully to the desert, trying to understand what the old man called the Soul of the World. He has already learned to read the flight of hawks, and he has been practising this skill every day since the alchemist first showed him how.

Recently, Santiago has fallen in love with Fatima, a woman from the oasis, and the two of them have been meeting quietly by the well each afternoon. He hasn't reached the pyramids yet, but he has come further than he ever imagined possible when he was still a shepherd.
