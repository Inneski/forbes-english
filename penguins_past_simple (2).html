A Penguin Swims 5,000 Miles — Every Year!

🇩🇪 Wortschatz-Hilfe / Vocabulary help

Die Verben in blau sind im Past Simple (Vergangenheit). Finde sie in der Geschichte!
The verbs in blue are in the Past Simple (past tense). Find them in the story!

João Pereira de Souza is 71 years old. He lives on a small island near Rio de Janeiro, Brazil. He is a retired bricklayer. He still works as a part-time fisherman.

Five years ago, João found a tiny, sick penguin on the beach near his home. The penguin's feathers were covered in oil. João felt sorry for the sick, little bird. He took the penguin back to his home. He cleaned him and fed him sardines. He gave him the name Dindim.

The penguin soon became strong and healthy again.

After a week or two, João went down to the seashore and tried to put Dindim back into the water. The penguin didn't want to go. He stayed with João. Eight months passed. Then suddenly, one day in February, Dindim disappeared.

João hoped that the bird just wanted to return to his penguin family. Four more months passed. Then João heard a familiar "honking" sound — Dindim was back!

Every year since then, Dindim spends eight months with João and then disappears for four months. It is thought that he swims 2,500 miles to Argentina or Chile, starts a penguin family, and then swims 2,500 miles back. That's 5,000 miles a year — to be with the man who saved his life.

🇩🇪 Grammatik-Notiz: Im Englischen gibt es für die Vergangenheit das Simple Past. Regelmäßige Verben bekommen -ed am Ende: clean → cleaned, pass → passed. Unregelmäßige Verben ändern sich: find → found, take → took, go → went.
In English, the past tense is called the Simple Past. Regular verbs add -ed. Irregular verbs change.

🔑 Key rule: In the negative and in questions, use did / didn't + the base form (infinitive) — NOT the past form!

✅ He didn't want to go.   ❌ He didn't wanted to go.

🇩🇪 Im Deutschen ist das ähnlich: Das Hilfsverb „did" trägt die Vergangenheit, das Hauptverb bleibt im Infinitiv.
