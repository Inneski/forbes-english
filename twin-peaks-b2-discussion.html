Part 1 — Two Writers

An Unlikely Partnership

In the mid-1980s, film director David Lynch and television writer Mark Frost worked together on a couple of projects that never reached the screen. When the pair pitched a new idea to a network in 1989 — a drama built around the death of a popular teenager in a small American town — nobody expected it to become one of the most talked-about shows of the decade.

Choose the best option

In this context, what does “pitched” mean?

Part 2 — Writing the Pilot

A Script Written at Speed

According to Frost, the two writers worked with unusual speed, producing a complete script for the opening episode in only a few weeks. That first episode, broadcast in April 1990, introduced viewers to a close-knit town, appearances hid a series of long-buried secrets.

Relative Clauses · 2/10

Choose the best option

Choose the correct possessive relative pronoun to complete the sentence: “...introduced viewers to a close-knit town, ______ quiet appearance hid a series of long-buried secrets.”

Part 3 — The Premise

A Mystery With a Difference

At the centre of the story was an FBI agent, sent to a small town to investigate the death of a local teenager. What made the show unusual was not the mystery itself, but its tone: scenes of genuine grief and small-town gossip sat comfortably beside moments of dark comedy and strange, dreamlike events.

Choose the best option

Complete the sentence: “An FBI agent ______ to the town to lead the investigation.”

Part 4 — A Genre of Its Own

Impossible to Categorise

Critics struggled to place the series in a single category, since it moved between genres that rarely appeared together in mainstream television: crime drama, soap opera, horror, and slapstick comedy, sometimes within the same scene.

The opening episode drew an enormous audience for its time — reportedly more than thirty million viewers in the United States alone — and the series briefly became a genuine pop-culture talking point, discussed on magazine covers and in living rooms across the country.

Part 6 — The Excitement Fades

A Difficult Second Season

That early excitement did not last. As the second season introduced new storylines, ratings dropped, and the network moved the show to a less favourable time slot. The series was cancelled after two seasons, leaving several plot threads unresolved.

Part 7 — Life After Cancellation

A Following That Would Not Disappear

Even after cancellation, the show refused to fade away. Fans kept discussing its unanswered questions for decades, and it gradually built the kind of devoted, long-term audience known as a cult following.

A Return, Decades Later

In 2017, twenty-five years after it first ended, the series returned for a new season on a different network, reuniting much of the original cast and proving that its strange world still had plenty of stories left to tell.

Part 9 — Changing Television

An Influence That Outlasted the Show

Decades later, the series is widely credited with changing what a television mystery could look like, paving the way for later dramas that treated ongoing mysteries and flawed characters as more important than tidy, one-episode resolutions.
