I
Modal Verbs — The Language of Will

Select the modal verb that best fits the meaning of each sentence.

You ___ read Beyond Good and Evil if you want to understand Nietzsche's critique of morality. It is essential.

💡 Must expresses strong necessity or obligation. "Might" is uncertain possibility, "could" is ability or weak possibility — neither fits "it is essential."

Nietzsche ___ have felt very lonely; he had almost no close friends in his final years.

💡 Must have + past participle expresses a logical deduction about the past ("I'm almost certain that..."). This is different from obligation.

You ___ misquote Nietzsche to support nationalist ideology — he explicitly rejected German nationalism.

💡 Shouldn't = moral advice / recommendation against something. Mustn't = strict prohibition. Here, we are advising, not issuing an absolute ban.
II
Reported Speech — Zarathustra Speaks… Indirectly

Transform or identify the correct reported speech form.

"Without music, life would be a mistake."

— Twilight of the Idols

Nietzsche said: "I am dynamite." → He said that he ___ dynamite.

💡 In reported speech, the present tense "am" shifts back to the past tense "was". This is called "backshifting."

Lou Salomé told him: "I will never marry you." → She told him that she ___ marry him.

💡 "Will" shifts to "would" in reported speech. Direct: "I will go." → Reported: "She said she would go."

His publisher asked him: "Have you finished the manuscript?" → His publisher asked him ___ the manuscript.

💡 Reported yes/no questions use whether / if + subject + verb (no inversion). The present perfect "have finished" shifts to past perfect "had finished."
III
Relative Clauses — Who, Which, That, Whose

Choose the correct relative pronoun or decide if one is needed.

Nietzsche, ___ sister distorted his manuscripts, is often misunderstood as an antisemite.

💡 Whose shows possession in relative clauses ("...his sister" = the sister belonging to Nietzsche). It works for both people and things.

The concept ___ influenced Nietzsche the most was the philosophy of Arthur Schopenhauer.

💡 That (or which) is used for things in defining relative clauses. Who / whom is reserved for people.

The philosopher ___ Nietzsche admired most, then despised, was Richard Wagner.

💡 Whom is used when the relative pronoun is the object of the clause (here: Nietzsche admired him → whom). It sounds formal but correct.
IV
Comparatives & Superlatives — Beyond Good, Better, Best

Select the correct comparative or superlative form.

"The higher we soar, the smaller we appear to those who cannot fly."

— Thus Spoke Zarathustra

Nietzsche's later works are considered ___ his earlier writings on Wagner.

💡 Far more ... than is a common intensified comparative. Note: comparatives always use than, not as. "As radical as" is used for equality.

Thus Spoke Zarathustra is arguably ___ of all Nietzsche's books.

💡 Superlatives of longer adjectives use the most + adjective. "The" is required before superlatives. "Poeticest" is not a real word!

Nietzsche's handwriting became ___ as his eyesight deteriorated over the years.

💡 Bad → worse → worst is an irregular comparative. Never say "more bad" or "more worse." Like German schlecht → schlechter → am schlechtesten.
V
Word Order — English is Not German

German puts verbs at the end of clauses — English does not. Choose the correctly ordered sentence.

Which sentence has the correct English word order?

💡 English word order: Subject → Verb → Object → Place → Time. Unlike German, the verb must follow the subject directly in a main clause.

Which sentence correctly places the adverb?

💡 Frequency adverbs like always, often, never, usually go before the main verb but after auxiliary verbs: "He always reads" / "He has always read."

Which is the correct question form?

💡 English questions use the auxiliary do/did: Question word + did + subject + base verb. The verb "write" does NOT move before the subject (unlike German).
